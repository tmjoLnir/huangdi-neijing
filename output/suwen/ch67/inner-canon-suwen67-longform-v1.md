## Why 13:00, and not 12:00 or 14:00

The requested window was 12–14 minutes with a hard floor of 11:30, and the brief
asked for the length that minimises padding and shortening. **78 blocks — 13:00 —
is where this chapter's usable material actually lands**, and the reasoning is
arithmetic rather than taste, because a 10s block is the unit that gets padded or
cut.

The chapter's problem is the opposite of most chapters on this slate: it is **long
and mostly unusable**. More than half the received text is a correspondence table
that this channel has already covered in Suwen 5, and a further stretch is
prognostic pulse material that is off-limits. What is left is a
**cross-examination**, and a cross-examination has a fixed number of moves in it.
Costing the material by how much each part can carry without inventing filler:

| Material | Blocks | Why that many |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open — the buried question, the answer stated bare, the demand, citation, title, thesis | 8 | the frame, and the whole exchange previewed in one block so the episode has somewhere to arrive |
| Act I — the almanac, the stems, the dating caveat, **the first refusal**, 不以數推以象之謂也 | 10 | a viewer who thinks this is an astronomy text cannot hear the rest, and the dating cannot wait |
| Act II — the star register, the gates, **the second refusal**, the frame of reference, 上者右行下者左行 | 10 | the chapter's claim to observation, and the one place it does bookkeeping |
| Act III — **the third refusal**, the correction of Guiyuqu, 天垂象地成形, root and branch | 10 | a named authority corrected by name, which is the episode's second-best material |
| **Act IV — the fourth refusal, 地之為下否乎 → 大氣舉之也, reckoning one, the comparisons, the six qi** | 14 | the reason the episode exists, and the reckoning has to be attached to it rather than deferred |
| Act V — the six prevails, the earthquake, **天地之變無以脈診** | 10 | the second payoff, and the proof the chapter is not a one-sentence curiosity |
| Act VI — what the cosmology was for, the five directions, reckoning two, the hedge, 侮 | 10 | the chapter's own second half, scored honestly and kept short on purpose |
| Act VII — the road not taken, Newton, the close, thesis, end card | 6 | |

That totals **78**. Both edges of the requested window cost something real:

- **12:00 is 72 blocks — 6 short.** The only six that can go without breaking a
  refusal are the star register and the frame-of-reference pair in Act II, which is
  most of that act. **Cost:** the chapter's single claim to being anchored in
  observation, and Lei-Gong's bookkeeping finding at block 27 — which is the one
  place in the episode where the text is credited with a *method* rather than an
  intuition. A 12:00 cut is an episode about one good sentence; a 13:00 cut is an
  episode about a document. It is available and is specified exactly in *Runtime
  levers*.
- **14:00 is 84 blocks — 6 too many, and the six are the wrong six.** The material
  that would fill them is the five-direction correspondence table expanded to its
  full five sections, or the §5 prognostic passage. **The first is the chapter's
  compliance-sensitive half and duplicates an episode this channel has already
  produced; the second is the passage that says a named pulse finding means
  death,** which is exactly the content the slate excluded whole chapters for. **The
  honest answer is that 14:00 is reachable and is a worse and riskier episode**, and
  *Runtime levers* says where the six would have to come from.
- **11:30 (69 blocks) is the format floor, not a target.** It cannot be reached
  without dropping one of the four refusals, at which point the structural form the
  slate assigned this chapter is gone.

**13:00 is the recommendation.** It sits in the middle of the requested window
rather than at either edge, it is where the usable material genuinely stops, and the
thing that sets the length here is not the source's length but the **shape of a
cross-examination**: four refusals, each needing a push, an answer and a reckoning,
and no way to run three of them and still arrive anywhere.

## What this episode argues

The obvious reading of this chapter is that an ancient Chinese doctor guessed
gravity. That reading is wrong, it is the reading the internet already has, and the
slate names it as this chapter's one discipline: *"this is not a discovery of
gravity, and claiming so would invite well-deserved correction. Frame it as a
cosmological intuition arrived at by reasoning about qi — remarkable for what it
rejects (the Earth resting on something) rather than for what it proves."*

**The spine is that a refusal can be worth more than an answer.** Four things
happen in this chapter:

1. **An almanac is interrogated until it produces a cosmology.** The chapter is a
   working document about which of the five movements governs which year. The
   Emperor keeps refusing to accept its terms, and on the fourth refusal the thing
   underneath the terms comes out: 地為人之下，太虛之中者也 — *the earth is beneath
   man, and it is in the midst of the great void.* Then 大氣舉之也 — *the great qi
   lifts it.*
2. **What is remarkable is the list of things that sentence does not contain.**
   Every neighbouring cosmology of the ancient world put the world on top of
   something — a shell, a pillar, an ocean, an animal. Each of those moves the
   problem down one floor and stops. **This one declines to add a floor.** That is
   an absence, and an absence is exactly the kind of achievement that is easy to
   oversell and easy to state precisely, so the episode states it precisely.
3. **A named authority is corrected by name, and a reason is given.** Guiyuqu — the
   Emperor's other teacher, whose stem table the whole almanac runs on — held that
   what answers to the earth is still. The chapter says he *watched only the upper
   signs and could not illuminate the whole.* Not that he lied and not that he was a
   fool. That he was standing where only half the evidence reaches. **That is what
   an argument looks like, and it is rarer in ancient technical literature than the
   cosmology is.**
4. **And then the chapter names the limit of its own instrument.** 天地之變，無以脈診
   — *the changes of heaven and earth cannot be examined by the pulse.* A book of
   medicine quoting another book of medicine to tell the man holding a wrist that
   what he is asking about will never arrive under his fingers.

**The thesis, stated once and hedged hard:** this is not physics and it never
became physics, because nobody asked *how much*. The great qi has no magnitude, no
distance law and nothing that could be checked. What the chapter has instead is a
refusal to invent a support it could not see — and a refusal is cheaper to get right
than a mechanism, which is the whole of the claim the episode makes. **The episode
says that sentence out loud at block 45 and again at block 77**, and the second
half's correspondence table is scored just as flatly at block 68.

**The dramatic engine:** the cross-examination is staged on the floor. Fan-di
begins on the high seat and **comes down one step of the eight-direction chart on
each refusal**, so the fourth question is asked standing directly in front of
Dr-Qi with nothing between them. **The fan is the tell** — snapped open he is
performing, set down he means it. It goes down once, at **block 76**, when he
realises that the ground under his own hall is held up by nothing anybody can name.
It does not come back up.

**Lei-Gong is the auditor**, and his brief this episode is *evidence*. Each of his
six blocks is a finding: that "the numbers do not work" answered with "numbers are
not the point" is the oldest move in any trade (17); that writing down which way
you must be facing before left and right mean anything is bookkeeping rather than
mysticism (27); that a named correction with a stated reason is how an argument
works (33); that every neighbouring answer moves the problem down one floor and
stops (47); that a trade manual naming the limit of its own instrument was not
selling anything (61); and that the correspondence table was generated rather than
counted (71).

> the assembler's floor**, which is a hard error that stops the whole build.
> **Every Arthur block below is 38–44 words and every Vesper block is 41–43.**

**Every one of the 78 blocks below is inside its voice's range**, verified by
parsing this document's own narration table and dropping tokens containing no letter
or digit — because a spaced em-dash is not a word and counting it as one spends real
budget on a window 1.4s wide.

Four consequences shape every line here, and none is stylistic:

- **The short interjection is dead.** Under an 8.6s floor there is no quick jab, and
  `--clip-seconds` moves the window rather than widening it. **Every character line
  is a full paragraph** — including four refusals that would each be one sentence in
  a stage play. This is why Lei-Gong has six blocks rather than twenty: at 47–52
  words each, an auditor who comments on everything becomes the narrator.
- **No comma list of three or more items appears anywhere in this script.** The
  2026-08-09 measurement put a 31-word Arthur line with seven commas at **12.022s**
  against **8.913s** for the same 31 words with none — a 3.1s swing from punctuation
  alone, on a window 1.4s wide. **This chapter is made of enumerations** — five
  stems, six branches, five heavens, six qi, six prevails, five directions — and is
  therefore the worst possible case for that failure. **Every enumeration below is
  joined with *and* or split into sentences, and there is not a single comma in any
  of the 78 narration cells.** Do not add one.
- **The rates are survivor-biased.** ~4.4 w/s is what Arthur's *passing* takes
  delivered, and his rate moved between **3.98 and 4.80 w/s within a single
  seven-block cut** on lines of near-identical length. A word count predicts a take
  only to about ±1.5s. **Generate two variants per block and keep the better one** —
  at ~1.45 a take that is the cheapest reliable method.
- **Reach the window with words, not full stops.** The assembler warns on internal
  pauses ≥0.8s, so padding a thin line with sentence breaks passes the duration gate
  and trips the pause warning. **No block below exceeds four sentences.**

These are **arithmetic against measured rates, not measurements**. Treat the first
pass as calibration and budget re-takes explicitly — the preflight below does.

### How to read this script

`CLAUDE.md` asks for **SOUND / VISUAL / CHARACTER** blocks and a numbered shot list;
`build_subtitles.js` needs a **three-column narration table** to build the required
`.srt`/`.vtt` sidecars. They are combined rather than duplicated:

- **The narration tables are canonical for words.** Blocks run continuously
  **1–78 across the whole episode**, never per-act; the assembly manifest is flat and
  act-local numbering produces off-by-one errors that are expensive to find in a
  thirteen-minute render.
- **The SOUND / VISUAL / direction prose between them is canonical for
  performance**, and the **numbered shot list after block 78 is canonical for
  picture.** Neither restates a narration line.
- **The speaker is named in the beat column**, not a fourth column — the parser reads
  exactly three, and a fourth would leak into the caption text.
- **No markdown inside a narration cell.** `build_subtitles.js` copies the third
  column into the cue verbatim and strips nothing, so a `**bold**` emphasis burns
  into the picture as literal asterisks. **Put emphasis in the direction prose, never
  in the line.**

> **Do not insert a table whose first cell is a bare integer anywhere between blocks
> 1 and 78.** `parseNarration` keeps the first consecutive run from block 1 and stops
> at the first row that breaks it, so a stray numeric table silently truncates the
> sidecar. Every other table in this document sits before block 1 or after block 78
> for that reason, and the ON-SCREEN TEXT card table prefixes its first column with
> `Block` so it cannot collide. The shot list after block 78 is a numbered *list*,
> not a table, for the same reason.

## The hall and the disc — the visual system

The slate assigns this chapter the **cross-examination** form, and §2.2 makes clear
why that matters beyond taste: twenty episodes in the same Q&A shape is the
Generic/Repetitive Content failure mode, so **every episode carries a distinct
structural form.** Here the form is staged as ground, and the argument is staged as
a gap.

**The whole episode plays in the Hall of Brightness — a square open hall with an
eight-direction chart inlaid in its floor — and a single dark ink disc hangs high
above it.**

| Act | The floor | The disc, and the field around it |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | Fan-di on the high seat at the north edge | hangs unexplained, small and high, nothing beneath it |
| Act I | he comes down one step on the first refusal | a brushed cord climbs toward it and stops short |
| Act II | one step further on the second refusal; the chart's rim lights with lodge marks | five faint roads cross the field, none of them touching the disc |
| Act III | one step further on the third refusal; the chart turns under him so left and right swap | the cord from Act I is rubbed out |
| **Act IV** | he arrives at the centre and stands facing her with nothing between them | **the field fills with drifting ink grain for the first time, and the disc is seen to have nothing under it at all** |
| Act V | the chart's surface dries and cracks and closes | the grain moves in six directions at different speeds |
| Act VI | five threads run down from the disc to five points on the chart | the grain thins |
| Act VII | the hall is empty and the fan lies on the floor | **the camera pulls back until the disc is very small in a very large field, and the episode ends on it** |

Three rules keep it from becoming decoration:

- **Nothing ever touches the disc from below, in any of the seventy-eight shots.**
  Not a pillar, not a column of smoke, not a beam of light, not a mountain, not a
  hand. The cord in the cold open **stops short and is later rubbed out**. This is
  the episode's entire argument rendered as a rule about the picture, and any shot
  that breaks it says the opposite of its own narration.
- **The ink grain is present in every frame from block 1 and only becomes visible at
  block 43.** It is not added; it is lit. The answer was in the picture the whole
  time and the viewer could not see it, which is the honest version of what the
  chapter does to its reader.
- **The register never changes.** Everything stays flat 2D ink-wash, and the modern
  reckoning is carried by Arthur's voice over the same brushed idiom. **There is no
  photograph, no globe, no orbital diagram, no starfield render and no falling
  apple in seventy-eight shots.** See *Compliance notes* — for this chapter those
  four are the single largest prompt-stage risk, because each one imports the
  gravity reading the episode spends two blocks refusing.

### Seven departures from the supplied English

The supplied translation is serviceable and is quoted above unaltered. This script
departs from it in seven places, each deliberately, and each a translation judgement
rather than a dramatisation:

1. **大氣舉之也 is *the great breath lifts it*, not *it is upheld by the great qi*.**
   舉 is the ordinary verb for raising or lifting a weight — the word used of lifting
   a cauldron. The passive *is upheld by* turns an action into a state and quietly
   supplies the support the sentence is refusing to supply. **Block 44 says so
   explicitly** because this is the single most consequential word in the episode.
2. **馮乎 is *what does it lean on*, not *what supports it*.** 馮 here is 憑, to lean
   or rely on. *Support* pre-loads the answer with the very thing the chapter
   declines to name, so Fan-di's push at block 42 asks the question the Chinese asks.
3. **太虛 is *the great emptiness*, not *the great void*.** *Void* reads to a modern
   English ear as outer space or as vacuum, both of which are anachronisms this
   episode cannot afford. *Emptiness* keeps it a place rather than a physics.
4. **氣 is *breath* in every character line and *qi* only in Arthur's.** Dr-Qi speaks
   in period and says *the great breath*; Arthur glosses it as *qi* at block 49 when
   comparing traditions. The point is that a viewer never hears an untranslated term
   asserted at them by a character.
5. **神 is not rendered at all.** It appears in §1's 神明為之紀 and §6's 玄生神, and
   both are omitted rather than translated. *Spirit* would import the whole
   vocabulary of the Lingshu 8 episode into a cosmology episode and would be the one
   word most likely to pull the thumbnail toward the occult.
6. **侮 is *insult*, not *belittle*.** 侮 carries humiliation and bullying. *Belittle*
   is too mild for a passage whose point is that the aggressor is the one who gets
   sick, and block 70's whole force is that the insult comes back.
7. **寡於畏也 is *too little fear in him*, not *a lack of fear or respect*.** The
   doubling in the supplied English hedges a blunt clause. The Chinese says the
   scarcity is *of fear*, and the episode's last card depends on that bluntness.

**One factual claim in the script is not from the chapter at all and needs checking
before render: block 11's dating.** The seven great discourses on the five movements
and six qi — this chapter among them — are generally held to be a later stratum,
absent from earlier recensions and supplied by **Wang Bing in his 762 CE edition**,
which claims to restore lost chapters from an older text. **The claim in block 11 is
hedged as *most scholars hold* for that reason**, and it is the one line in the
episode that a specialist would check first. It is in the script rather than out of
it because an episode praising a text's cosmology while concealing that the text may
be a thousand years younger than the viewer assumes would fail this channel's own
standard. **Verify the attribution and the date against a reference edition before
the take is recorded.**
