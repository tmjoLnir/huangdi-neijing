## Why 18:00, and not 17:00 or 20:00

The requested window was 17–20 minutes with a hard floor of 11:30, and the brief
asked for the length that minimises padding and shortening. **108 blocks — 18:00 —
is where this chapter's usable material actually lands**, and it sits mid-window
rather than at either edge.

This chapter's problem is the opposite of Suwen 77's. That chapter was five
charges the source sizes unequally, so the work was refusing to give them equal
runtime. **This one is five regions the source sizes unequally *and* describes to
an identical formula** — terrain, then diet, then what the writer believes the
people are like, then the characteristic illness, then the therapy, then the
refrain *and so it came from there*. **Six beats, five times, and the beats are
not the same size in each.** The east gets the longest passage in the chapter and
the north gets one sentence. An episode that gives each region the same runtime is
padding the north by four blocks and rushing the east by four, and the symmetry
would be the episode's own invention rather than the text's.

Costing it by what each part carries without inventing filler:

## What this episode argues

The obvious reading is that this chapter is early medical geography, and that
reading is true and is not what makes the chapter remarkable. **The spine is that
this is a theory of why medical traditions differ, written from inside one of
them, and its answer is that no medicine is universal because no place is.** Four
things happen in it:

1. **It starts from a fact that ought to be embarrassing.** Physicians cure the
   same illness by different means and all of them work. A system that wanted to
   be universal would explain that away. **This one takes it as the datum** and
   asks what the world must be like for it to be true.
2. **It answers with the ground.** 地勢使然也 — the lie of the land makes it so.
   Not the physicians and not the illness. **Five paragraphs later the therapies
   have become properties of terrain**, which is a claim about medicine that
   nothing else in the canon makes.
3. **It never ranks them.** Five regions, five therapies, and not one sentence
   saying which is best. The chapter's own summary is 各得其所宜 — each obtains
   what suits it. **A text written in the capital declines to say the capital's
   medicine is the good one**, which is not what a court document usually does.
4. **And it pays for that with a second move that goes wrong.** If terrain makes
   the medicine, terrain makes the people — so the chapter describes the
   complexions, the skin, the diet and the habits of everybody who lives somewhere
   else, in the vocabulary of diagnosis. **Applying a diagnostic category to a
   population is where it stops being medicine.** The two moves are the same move,
   and the episode says so.

**The thesis, stated once and hedged hard:** the chapter has no mechanism, its
aetiologies are assertions, and there is no way to tell from the text whether the
five therapies were observed in five places or assigned to five slots in a
five-directions grid that already existed. What it has instead is **the right
question, asked from inside a tradition, about the tradition's own limits** — and
the honest reckoning is that its answer to that question is inseparable from a
description of foreigners nobody would write now. **The episode says that out loud
at blocks 105 and 106, and it does not save the reckoning for the end**: the
distance is stated at **block 9 before a single word is quoted**, and again at
**28, 42, 56 and 68** — once inside each of the four regions the chapter describes
from outside. **The fifth region is the capital itself and it gets an inversion
rather than a distance**, at 80 to 82, because the passage there is the one place
the writer turns the same vocabulary on his own household.

**The dramatic engine is the map, and the inversion is that nobody travels.** In
the source the Emperor asks a single question and never speaks again — the whole
chapter after that is Qibo's answer. **The script does not fix that.** Fan-di asks
his one question at block 10 and then spends seventeen minutes listening to a
description of the world without leaving the room, which is exactly what the
chapter's author did. **The fan is the tell:** it is open and moving through all
five regions, and it goes down at **block 97**, when he admits he would have built
the same list.

**Lei-Gong is not in this chapter and is written in as the one who counts.** His
ten blocks are findings about the text's shape and never about a body: the brief
he sets himself (8); that the count is exact and therefore suspicious (16); that
the stone is the only hard object in the eastern passage (34); that the west is
the only region whose illness has no external cause and the only one given an
inward medicine (49); that the shortest passage got the therapy with the best
evidence (61); that the chapter's refrain contradicts its own clause (76); that
the centre fails the chapter's own test (87); that the chapter never ranks them
(93); that five slots with five fillers and none left over is what a system looks
like rather than what a survey looks like (96); and that the map has no edges
(103).

**Dr-Qi is Qibo and the whole answer is hers.** She has thirty-five blocks and
almost every one of them is recitation or close paraphrase, which is textually
right here in a way it was not on Suwen 77 — **歧伯對曰 is the source's own
attribution.** The constraint that follows is that **she never editorialises about
the regional descriptions**; she reads them as written and Arthur carries every
frame around them. Her two blocks that are not recitation are both refusals, at
102 and 107, and the second is the quietest line in the episode.

**Every one of the 108 blocks below is inside its voice's range**, verified by

spends real budget on a window 1.4s wide.

Four consequences shape every line here, and none is stylistic:

- **This chapter is the worst case in the repo for the comma.** It is built out of
  enumerations — five regions, and inside each one a terrain and a diet and a
  complexion and an illness and a therapy — and the 2026-08-09 measurement put a
  31-word Arthur line with seven commas at **12.022s** against **8.913s** for the
  same 31 words with none. **There is not a single comma in any of the 108
  narration cells.** Every list is joined with *and* or split into sentences. **Do
  not add one**, and note the cost this pays: it makes the clauses wide, which is
  why the caption report runs long. The take window wins.
- **The short interjection is dead.** Under an 8.6s floor there is no quick
  reaction from the throne, so **every one of Fan-di's nine blocks is a
  paragraph.** In a chapter whose Emperor speaks once, that is a real constraint
  rather than a stylistic one: it is why he has nine blocks and not thirty.
- **The rates are survivor-biased.** ~4.4 w/s is what Arthur's *passing* takes
  delivered, and his rate moved between **3.98 and 4.80 w/s within a single
  seven-block cut** on lines of near-identical length. A word count predicts a take
  only to about ±1.5s. **Generate two variants per block and keep the better one.**
- **Reach the window with words, not full stops.** The assembler warns on internal
  pauses ≥0.8s, so padding a thin line with sentence breaks passes the duration
  gate and trips the pause warning. **No block below exceeds four sentences.**

These are **arithmetic against measured rates, not measurements**. Treat the first
pass as calibration and budget re-takes explicitly — the preflight below does.

### How to read this script

`CLAUDE.md` asks for **SOUND / VISUAL / CHARACTER** blocks and a numbered shot
list; `build_subtitles.js` needs a **three-column narration table** to build the
required `.srt`/`.vtt` sidecars. They are combined rather than duplicated:

- **The narration tables are canonical for words.** Blocks run continuously
  **1–108 across the whole episode**, never per-act; the assembly manifest is flat
  and act-local numbering produces off-by-one errors that are expensive to find in
  an eighteen-minute render.
- **The SOUND / VISUAL / direction prose between them is canonical for
  performance**, and the **numbered shot list after block 108 is canonical for
  picture.** Neither restates a narration line.
- **The speaker is named in the beat column**, not a fourth column — the parser
  reads exactly three, and a fourth would leak into the caption text.
- **No markdown inside a narration cell.** `build_subtitles.js` copies the third
  column into the cue verbatim and strips nothing, so a `**bold**` emphasis burns
  into the picture as literal asterisks. **Put emphasis in the direction prose,
  never in the line.**

> **Do not insert a table whose first cell is a bare integer anywhere between
> blocks 1 and 108.** `parseNarration` keeps the first consecutive run from block 1
> and stops at the first row that breaks it, so a stray numeric table silently
> truncates the sidecar. Every other table in this document sits before block 1 or
> after block 108 for that reason, and the ON-SCREEN TEXT card table prefixes its
> first column with `Block` so it cannot collide. The shot list after block 108 is
> a numbered *list*, not a table, for the same reason.

## The map — the visual system

The slate assigns this chapter the **compass rose** form, and §2.2 makes clear why
that matters beyond taste: twenty episodes in the same Q&A shape is the
Generic/Repetitive Content failure mode, so **every episode carries a distinct
structural form.** Here the form is a journey that nobody takes.

**The whole episode plays over one object — a large blank silk map laid flat on a
low lacquer table in a plain room, seen mostly from directly above.** The three
cast characters stand at its four sides. Nobody leaves the room until block 103.

| Act | The quarter that inks in | Its weather, its implement and the camera |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | none — the silk is blank | the room from table height; the map has one mark at its centre and nothing else |
| Act I | the centre mark and four faint bearings | flat even light; the camera settles directly overhead and stays there |
| Act II — **east** | a shoreline, a salt pan, a net | grey sea-light; **a sharpened stone lancet laid on the eastern edge** |
| Act III — **west** | high broken ground, sand and stone, a vein of jade | hard dry light; **a small sealed jar with a wax seal laid at the west** |
| Act IV — **north** | a high plateau under white | near-white light and no shadow; **a cone of dried herb on a clay dish laid at the north** |
| Act V — **south** | low ground under mist with standing water | wet green-grey light; **a cloth roll opened to nine fine needles laid at the south** |
| Act VI — **centre** | level fields and a river and a town | flat noon light with no direction in it; **a pair of empty cloth shoes and a small painted chart of figures laid at the centre** |
| Act VII | nothing new inks | **the light leaves the map and comes from the doorway instead**, and at 103 the camera goes outside for the only time in the episode |

Four rules keep it from becoming decoration, and the first is a compliance rule
before it is an aesthetic one:

- **No population from any region is ever depicted, in any of the hundred and
  eight shots.** The chapter's regional descriptions are of complexions, skin
  texture, diet and habits; **drawing them would convert a reported Han-dynasty
  belief into this channel's own picture of a people.** Every region is carried by
  terrain, weather and objects — a salt pan, a drying net, a vein of jade in
  stone, a windbreak of stacked stones, a churn, a fermenting jar, a broom against
  a wall. **The only human figures in the episode are the three cast characters,
  and they never leave the room the map is in.**
- **No implement ever touches a person, and no body is ever treated.** The five
  implements are laid on silk and lifted from silk. Nothing enters a body, nothing
  is applied to a body, and there is no patient in this episode at all. The
  chapter is about where therapies came from rather than about how they are done,
  so a shot of a therapy being performed answers a question the episode is not
  asking — and it is the class of imagery that most easily reads as a person
  restrained.
- **The map's edges stay blank in every shot.** Ink reaches out from the centre and
  stops well short of the silk's edge, in all four directions, for the whole
  episode. **Nobody remarks on it until block 103.** It is the episode's closing
  argument and it has to have been true in frame from the first shot for that
  argument to land.
- **The register never changes.** Everything stays flat 2D ink-wash, and the
  external comparisons at 59, 60, 86, 99 and 100 are carried by Arthur's voice over
  the same brushed idiom. **There is no photograph, no excavation site, no museum
  case, no modern map, no national border and no modern clinical imagery in a
  hundred and eight shots.** The Mawangdui and Zhangjiashan material is carried by
  **a bound bundle of silk sheets and a painted chart of small figures**, drawn in
  the same hand as everything else.

**One more rule, and it is the episode's structural joke.** The five implements are
laid down one per act at the compass point of the region they came from — and in
**shot 101** they are gathered into the middle of the map, where the chapter says
guided exercise came from. **The centre ends up holding all five**, which is
exactly what 聖人雜合以治 says, and it is also exactly what a text written in the
centre would conclude. **The episode does not choose between those two readings**
and shot 101 is composed so that both are visible at once.

# COLD OPEN — *One Illness, Five Cures* · blocks 1–9 · 0:00–1:30

**SOUND:** Silk being unrolled and laid flat. Then nothing. **No music anywhere in
the cold open**, and nothing that reads as travelogue scoring — a score under a
sequence of places is what turns ethnography into tourism, and this episode cannot
afford that in its first ninety seconds.
**VISUAL:** Black. The camera comes up at table height on a low lacquer table in a
plain room, and a very large sheet of blank silk is being laid across it. **One
mark at the centre of the silk and nothing else on it.** The light is flat and
even and comes from nowhere in particular. Flat 2D ink-wash throughout,
**landscape 16:9 framing**.

*History lower-third sits on block 1 — it is required inside the first ten seconds,
and on this episode it is the earliest available signal that the regional material
is a historical document rather than the channel's own description of anybody.*

| Block | Beat | Narration |

### §1 — the question, and the east

> 黃帝問曰：醫之治病也，一病而治各不同，皆愈何也。岐伯對曰：地勢使然也。故東方之域，天地之所始生也，魚鹽之地，海濱傍水，其民食魚而嗜鹹，皆安其處，美其食。魚者使人熱中，鹽者勝血，故其民皆黑色踈理，其病皆為癰瘍，其治宜砭石。故砭石者，亦從東方來。

**Working translation.** *The Yellow Emperor asked: When physicians treat illness,
they treat one and the same illness by different methods, and all of them cure it.
How is this so? — Qibo replied: The lie of the land makes it so. So the eastern
region is where heaven and earth first gave birth to living things. It is a land of
fish and salt, on the seashore with water beside it. The people there eat fish and
have a taste for salt. They are settled where they are and they think their own
food fine. Fish makes heat gather within a person; salt overcomes the blood. And so
the people there are all dark in colour and loose in the grain of the skin, and
their illnesses are all abscesses and sores. The fitting treatment for them is the
stone lancet. And so the stone lancet also came from the eastern region.*

### §2 — the west

> 西方者，金玉之域，沙石之處，天地之所收引也。其民陵居而多風，水土剛強，其民不衣而褐薦，其民華食而脂肥，故邪不能傷其形體，其病生於內，其治宜毒藥。故毒藥者，亦從西方來。

**Working translation.** *The western region is a land of metal and jade, a place
of sand and stone, where heaven and earth gather in and draw back. The people there
dwell on high ground and there is much wind; the water and the soil are hard and
strong. They do not wear fine clothes but go in coarse hemp and straw matting, and
they eat richly and are fat on it. So evil cannot injure their bodies from without,
and their illnesses arise from within. The fitting treatment for them is the potent
drugs. And so the potent drugs also came from the western region.*

### §3 — the north

> 北方者，天地所閉藏之域也。其地高陵居，風寒冰冽，其民樂野處而乳食，藏寒生滿病，其治宜灸焫。故灸焫者，亦從北方來。

**Working translation.** *The northern region is the domain where heaven and earth
shut in and store. The land is high and they dwell on the heights; the winds are
cold and the ice is bitter. The people there are glad to live out in the open
country and they live on milk. Cold is stored within and produces illnesses of
fullness. The fitting treatment for them is burning and cauterising. And so burning
and cauterising also came from the northern region.*

### §4 — the south

> 南方者，天地所長養，陽之所盛處也。其地下，水土弱，霧露之所聚也，其民嗜酸而食胕，故其民皆緻理而赤色，其病攣痺，其治宜微鍼。故九鍼者，亦從南方來。

**Working translation.** *The southern region is where heaven and earth grow and
nurture, the place where the yang is most abundant. The land there lies low, the
water and the soil are weak, and it is where the mist and the dew gather. The
people there have a taste for sour things and eat food that has been left to
change. And so the people there are all fine in the grain of the skin and red in
colour, and their illnesses are cramping and blockage. The fitting treatment for
them is the fine needle. And so the nine needles also came from the southern
region.*

### §5 — the centre, and the conclusion

> 中央者，其地平以濕，天地所以生萬物也眾。其民食雜而不勞，故其病多痿厥寒熱，其治宜導引按蹻。故導引按蹻者，亦從中央出也。故聖人雜合以治，各得其所宜，故治所以異而病皆愈者，得病之情，知治之大體也。

**Working translation.** *The central region is level ground and it is damp, and it
is where heaven and earth produce the greatest number of living things. The people
there eat a mixed diet and do not labour hard, and so their illnesses are many —
wasting and reversal and alternating cold and heat. The fitting treatment for them
is guiding and pulling together with pressing and lifting. And so guiding and
pulling and pressing and lifting also came out from the central region. So the sage
combines them in treating, and each obtains what suits it. Therefore the treatments
differ and the illnesses are all cured alike: it is because he has got hold of the
circumstances of the illness, and knows the general shape of treating.*

### Departures, exclusions and things to verify

Twelve items, and each is argued rather than asserted.

1. **The source prints 歧伯 where the received text reads 岐伯.** The character
   above is corrected to 岐. This is a common substitution in digital texts and
   nothing depends on it; it is recorded because it is a signal about the
   provenance of the export rather than because it changes a reading. **岐伯 does
   not appear on any card**, so no burn depends on it.
2. **緻理 at §4 is a hand correction and it is the one glyph decision in the
   script that a reader could call wrong.** The source prints 致理. Editions
   differ, and 緻 — fine, close-grained — is the reading that makes the passage
   work, because it is the exact opposite of the east's 踈理 and the whole point of
   blocks 69 and 70 is that the two are opposites. **緻理 does not appear on a card
   and the narration renders it in English**, so the risk is confined to this
   section. **Verify against a reference edition before the block-69 take.**
3. **踈理 at §1 is kept as the source prints it.** It is a variant of 疏 and many
   editions print 疏理. **It does appear on a card — block 27** — so this is the
   one variant that reaches the screen. Either form is defensible; **do not switch
   it after the card is generated**, since the clip carries the glyph.
4. **鍼 rather than 針 throughout.** Editions print both and they are the same
   word. Two cards are affected — block 72 — and the choice is for consistency with
   the older graph used elsewhere in the received Suwen. **Nothing turns on it.**
5. **痺 rather than 痹 at §4.** The source's simplified 痹 converts to either; 痺 is
   the standard traditional form and is used on block 72's card.
6. **食胕 is the compliance-load-bearing reading and the script argues against the
   supplied English.** The source renders it *"consume spoiled food."* 胕 here is
   read as 腐, and 腐 covers what is fermented, what is pickled and what is
   deliberately preserved as well as what has simply gone off. **Block 67 puts both
   readings on screen and says the disparaging one is available and not the only
   one.** This is a judgement and it is deliberate: *spoiled* imports the Han
   writer's contempt into the English, where a modern viewer will hear it as the
   channel's own. **Do not revert block 67 to the supplied translation.**
7. **毒藥 is rendered *potent drugs* and is glossed on screen at block 46.** The
   supplied English gives *"toxic herbs"* and *"internal prescriptions."* 毒 in
   this canon carries the sense of a drug strong enough to act rather than a drug
   that harms, and the standard scholarly rendering is *potent* or *drastic*. The
   episode says so out loud rather than translating it quietly, because *toxic
   herbs* is the phrase most likely to be screenshotted out of context.
8. **灸焫 is rendered *burning and cauterising* and only then narrowed to
   moxibustion.** 焫 is to burn or sear. The received compound covers cautery
   generally and later tradition settles on burning mugwort specifically. **Block
   58 states that narrowing rather than assuming it**, and the distinction matters
   because block 59's manuscript evidence is about cautery in general.
9. **按蹻 is rendered *pressing and lifting* and this is a judgement.** 蹻 relates
   to raising and to the limbs; the compound is also written 按撟 and is often
   glossed simply as massage. Block 84 renders it as what a pair of hands does to a
   limb, which is the least committed reading available. **The second thing on this
   list to verify.**
10. **亦從中央出也 uses 出 where the four preceding refrains use 來, and block 88 is
    built entirely on that.** The received text does read 出 at §5 and 來 at §1 to
    §4. **This is the third thing to verify against a reference edition, and it is
    the one that would cost a whole block if it is an artefact of one edition
    rather than a feature of the text.** If a reference edition prints 來 there,
    **block 88 must be rewritten before its take is recorded.**
11. **微鍼 in the clause and 九鍼 in the refrain is a feature of the received text
    and not a transcription error here.** §4 says the fitting treatment is the
    *fine* needle and then says the *nine* needles came from the south. Block 76 is
    Lei-Gong noticing exactly that. It is the only place in five parallel passages
    where the formula does not hold, and **it is reported as an observation about
    the text rather than as a correction to it.**
12. **Nothing is excluded from this chapter on compliance grounds, and that is
    unusual for this series.** There is no mortality clause, no graphic passage, no
    fertility content, no supernatural surface and no longevity claim anywhere in
    the four hundred characters. **癰瘍 — abscesses and sores — is the only
    clinically vivid term in it**, it appears in the narration at block 27 and on
    the block-27 card, and it appears in **no shot**. That is a picture decision
    rather than an exclusion: the word stays, the image does not.

**Six claims in the script are not from this chapter at all and each is marked as
ours in the narration:** the five-directions correlation system (17, 39, 52, 64,
94 and 96), the salt monopoly (23), the archaeology of the stone lancet (33), the
excavated cautery manuscripts (59 and 60), the excavated exercise charts (85 and
86), and *Airs, Waters, Places* (99 and 100). **Block 101's line of descent is also
ours**, and it is phrased as *generally traced back to* rather than as a fact about
transmission.

**Block 18's dating caveat is ours and is the line a specialist checks first.** The
received Suwen reaches us through the Tang-dynasty recension of Wang Bing and its
dating is genuinely argued over. Block 18 says exactly that and no more, and block
1's *roughly two thousand years old* is deliberately approximate for the same
reason.

**Blocks 59, 60, 85 and 86 are the episode's only external evidence and they are
hedged in the same breath they are stated.** The manuscripts named are the two
cautery texts and the painted exercise chart from the Changsha tomb and the
exercise manual from the tomb near it; **block 60 and block 86 both say that a tomb
records a burial rather than an origin**, and neither block claims that the chapter
knew of any of them. **The claim that the tombs predate the received text of this
chapter should be verified against a reference edition's dating discussion before
block 59's take**, since it is the one comparative claim in the episode that
depends on a date rather than on a place.

### Assembly strategy — act by act, and the join is the open problem

**Do not attempt 108 clips in one unbroken pass.** A 108-block assembly means ~216
files downloaded inside a single chained command before assembly begins, and
nothing at this scale has ever been run on this pipeline.

Per act, in order:

1. Generate that act's clips and takes.
2. **Append the job IDs to this document immediately**, before moving on.
3. Assemble the act as its own `assemble_final.sh` run to check pacing early,
   exporting each act's MP4 out of the sandbox **inside the same call**.

**`background: true` is the decision to get right and it has already lost a run.**
A trailer-scale assembly finishes inside the 120s foreground budget; **an act of 12
to 20 blocks probably does not**, because it is 24 to 40 downloads before assembly
starts. On the Suwen 1 v5 run `background: true` timed out the transport call, the
sandbox was reclaimed, and the finished render went with it. **If a background job
is used here, poll the returned log with `tail` at least every 60 seconds** — the
sandbox dies under an unpolled job. **The cold open at 9 blocks is the safest
foreground candidate**, so run it first and time it before committing to a posture
for the rest. **Act VII at 20 blocks is the largest single assembly this repo would
ever have attempted** and should be run last, after the posture is known.

**The join is the open problem and it must be decided before an episode's worth of
takes is generated, not at the join.** `assemble_final.sh` takes clip/voice pairs
against a declared block count and asserts an `N × 10s` output, so an assembled act
is not a valid input to it. Two routes, neither yet run here:

- **Assemble the whole episode in one call** and skip the join. Removes the problem
  and is the ~216-file download.
- **Concat the act outputs** with our own ffmpeg. Defensible — the acts are already
  assembled correctly and a concat of equal-geometry MP4s is a lossless remux — but
  it puts the −16 LUFS normalisation per act rather than across the episode.
  **Check levels at the seven seams**, and note that this episode's seams are
  unusually exposed: **each act has its own environmental sound bed and Act IV is
  near-silent throughout**, so a level step at the 50/51 seam or the 62/63 seam will
  be audible in a way it would not be on a uniformly scored cut.

Flags for every act run: `--out`, `--blocks <act count>`, `--manifest pairs.txt`.
No `--clip-seconds` (leave the window alone). No `--music` until guqin licensing is
cleared. **No `--subs`** — it is a hard error.

Name files `blockNN.mp4` / `voiceNN.wav` with the numbers aligned, **using the
episode's continuous numbering rather than act-local numbering.** The script
cross-checks the numbers in each pair; act-local numbering is how audio ends up on
the wrong block.

Record afterwards, per act: block count, the flags used, the manifest, the exported
`media_id`, the **whole download URL**, and any assembler WARNs accepted rather
than fixed.
