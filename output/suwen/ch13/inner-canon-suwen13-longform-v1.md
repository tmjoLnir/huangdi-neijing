## What this episode argues

The obvious reading of this chapter is that it is the moment Chinese medicine grew
up: the spell is retired, observation arrives, science begins. That reading
produces fourteen minutes of triumphalism, and the chapter does not support it.

**The spine is that this is a text caught mid-transition, and it does not think the
transition is going well.** Four things happen in a very short chapter:

1. **The canon retires its own inheritance, on the record, in front of the
   throne.** 故祝由不能已也 — *therefore the invocation cannot bring it to an end*.
   That is not a modern critic and not a foreign one. It is the tradition's own
   foundational text saying the old method has stopped working, and there is no
   softer reading of it available.
2. **The reason it gives is a mechanism, not a mood.** The ancients were not
   holier. They were less exposed and less encumbered — 內無眷慕之累，外無伸宦之形
   — so the illness stayed shallow, and a shallow illness could be reached by
   words. **故毒藥不能治其內** does not say the ancients had no drugs. It says drugs
   *could not treat them*. That is an argument with a shape, and it can be scored.
3. **What replaces the spell is a sign.** 色脈 — colour and pulse — attributed not
   to a spirit but to 先師, the teachers who handed it down, and to a **named**
   individual, 僦貸季, in a canon that almost never names anybody. The authority
   moves from revelation to transmission, and both instruments are located **on the
   patient**, which means the physician now has to go and stand next to one.
4. **And then the chapter says the profession has been losing it ever since.**
   中古 treated on arrival; 暮世 waits until 病形已成 and then attacks. 粗工兇兇 —
   *the crude practitioner, fierce and in a hurry* — is contempt written into a
   medical classic and copied by hand for two thousand years. 故病未已，新病復起 is
   iatrogenic harm, named.

**The payoff is the last passage, and it is the reason this episode exists.** Asked
for the single essential thing, the physician does not name an instrument. He says:
**閉戶塞牖，繫之病者，數問其情，以從其意** — shut the door, close the window, stay
with the patient, ask again and again what his condition is, and follow where his
own account leads. That is the oldest description of a clinical consultation
anybody has. **The chapter demotes the incantation in its first section and, in its
last, describes a closed room, a single patient, and a practitioner repeating
himself until the patient feels heard.** Whatever that is, medicine did not stop
doing it.

**The thesis, stated once and hedged hard:** medicine did not stop being magic
here. It stopped *pretending the magic was the medicine*. Attention is part of
care and is studied as part of care; it is not treatment, and this chapter is the
first document to draw that line.

**The dramatic engine:** Fan-di has called this audience to ask whether the
profession he funds has been in decline since before he was born — and is answered
with a description of his own court. **The fan is the tell**: snapped open he is
performing, set down he means it. It goes down once, at block 41, when he realises
that the chapter's diagnosis of the present age is a diagnosis of the world he
rules. It does not come back up.

**Lei-Gong is the auditor**, and his brief this episode is nostalgia. He is not
here to be charmed. Each of his six blocks is a finding: that a thousand-year
institution deserves an explanation rather than a laugh (16); that the chapter has
handed him something falsifiable (33); that every age in it envies the last (42);
that the two new instruments are on the *patient* (55); that a ladder of declining
ages has no bottom rung (74); and — the episode's best line, at block 82 — that
they never stopped doing the thing, they only stopped calling it a spell.

### Writing constraint — sized to the 8.6–10.0s window

The assembler gates on **8.6–10.0s of detected speech per block**, both edges hard
errors, computed as `--clip-seconds − 1.4` to `--clip-seconds`.

Word budgets as measured 2026-08-09 (`SKILL.md` step 3, from the Suwen 8 trailer v1
run — the only cut in this repo with all four voices measured against the
assembler's own gate):

| Voice | Measured rate | Words per block | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arthur | 3.50 w/s | **31–34** | measured across 2 runs |
| Xavier (Fan-di) | 4.53 w/s | **40–44** | measured across 2 runs |
| Vesper (Dr-Qi) | 4.15 w/s | **37–41** | measured across 2 runs, unchanged |
| Zane (Lei-Gong) | 5.34 w/s | **47–52** | measured at length, 2026-08-09 |

**Every one of the 84 blocks below is inside its voice's range**, verified by
parsing this document's own narration table and dropping tokens containing no
letter or digit — because a spaced em-dash is not a word and counting it as one
spends real budget on a window 1.4s wide.

Four consequences shape every line here, and none is stylistic:

- **The short interjection is dead.** Under an 8.6s floor there is no quick jab,
  and `--clip-seconds` moves the window rather than widening it. **Every character
  line is a full paragraph.** Lei-Gong's six blocks are arguments, which is why he
  reads as an auditor rather than a mascot.
- **No comma list of three or more items appears anywhere in this script.** The
  same measurement run put a 31-word Arthur line with seven commas at **12.022s**
  against **8.913s** for the same 31 words with none — a 3.1s swing from
  punctuation alone, on a window 1.4s wide. Every enumeration below is joined with
  *and* or split into sentences. **Do not add commas to these lines**; it is free
  in the document and catastrophic in the take.
- **The rates are survivor-biased.** 3.50 w/s is what Arthur's *passing* takes
  delivered; across every recorded attempt on one measured run he averaged **3.04
  w/s**. These counts are sized for his fast mode and will regularly return over
  the ceiling. Re-roll before rewriting — at ~1.45 credits a take that is the cheap
  move.
- **Reach the window with words, not full stops.** The assembler warns on internal
  pauses ≥0.8s, so padding a thin line with sentence breaks passes the duration
  gate and trips the pause warning. Blocks **55, 76 and 82** were each drafted with
  five or six sentence boundaries and restructured down to four or fewer for exactly
  this reason; **do not re-break them**, and do not re-insert the parenthetical
  commas removed from **65** and **69**.

These are **arithmetic against measured rates, not measurements**. Treat the first
pass as calibration and budget re-takes explicitly.

### How to read this script

`CLAUDE.md` asks for **SOUND / VISUAL / CHARACTER** blocks and a numbered shot
list; `build_subtitles.js` needs a **three-column narration table** to build the
required `.srt`/`.vtt` sidecars. They are combined rather than duplicated:

- **The narration tables are canonical for words.** Blocks run continuously
  **1–84 across the whole episode**, never per-act; the assembly manifest is flat
  and act-local numbering produces off-by-one errors that are expensive to find.
- **The SOUND / VISUAL / direction prose between them is canonical for
  performance**, and the **numbered shot list after block 84 is canonical for
  picture.** Neither restates a narration line.
- **The speaker is named in the beat column**, not a fourth column — the parser
  reads exactly three, and a fourth would leak into the caption text.

> **Do not insert a table whose first cell is a bare integer anywhere between
> blocks 1 and 84.** `parseNarration` keeps the first consecutive run from block 1
> and stops at the first row that breaks it, so a stray numeric table silently
> truncates the sidecar. Every other table in this document sits before block 1 or
> after block 84 for that reason, and the ON-SCREEN TEXT card table prefixes its
> first column with `Block` so it cannot collide. The shot list after block 84 is
> a numbered *list*, not a table, for the same reason.

## The diptych — the visual system, and why it is structural

The slate assigns this chapter the **diptych** form, and §2.2 of the slate makes
clear why that matters beyond taste: twenty episodes in the same Q&A shape is the
Generic/Repetitive Content failure mode, so **every episode carries a distinct
structural form**. Here it is taken literally.

**The whole episode plays on one hanging silk scroll, divided down the middle.**

| Act | Left panel | Right panel | The divide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | blank | blank | a single vertical crease, unexplained |
| Act I | 上古 inks in | stays blank | the right panel's emptiness is the joke |
| Act II | 上古 | 今世 inks in, crosscut | the divide is at its hardest and blackest |
| Act III | colour / the sun | pulse / the moon | the divide becomes the instrument |
| Act IV | 上古 · 中古 | 暮世 | briefly **three** panels, then back to two |
| Act V | — | — | the divide dissolves into one room, one lamp, one door |

Two rules keep it from becoming decoration:

- **Nothing crosses the divide until Act V.** Ink that touches it stops. This is
  what makes the final dissolve read as an argument rather than a transition.
- **The right panel is always the present**, including when the present is the Han
  dynasty. The episode never signals *modern* with photography or with a change of
  medium — everything stays flat 2D ink-wash, and the modern reckoning is carried
  by Arthur's voice over the same brushed idiom. **There is no live action, no
  photograph, and no anatomical rendering in eighty-four shots.**

### §1 — The spell, and why it stopped working

> 黃帝問曰：余聞古之治病，惟其移精變氣，可祝由而已。今世治病，毒藥治其內，鍼石治其外，
> 或愈或不愈，何也？
> 歧伯對曰：往古人居禽獸之間，動作以避寒，陰居以避暑，內無眷慕之累，外無伸宦之形，
> 此恬憺之世，邪不能深入也。故毒藥不能治其內，鍼石不能治其外，故可移精祝由而已。
> 當今之世不然，憂患緣其內，苦形傷其外，又失四時之從，逆寒暑之宜，賊風數至，虛邪朝夕，
> 內至五藏骨髓，外傷空竅肌膚，所以小病必甚，大病必死，故祝由不能已也。

The Yellow Emperor asked: I have heard that in treating illness the ancients had
only to shift the essence and transform the qi, and it could be accomplished by
invocation alone. In the present age, illness is treated with toxic medicines
within and with needles and stones without — and some are cured and some are not.
Why is this?

Qibo replied: In the old time people lived among the birds and the beasts. They
moved about to avoid the cold and stayed in shaded places to avoid the heat.
Within, they had no burden of longing and attachment; without, they had no shape
of striving after office. This was an age of calm, and the evil could not enter
deeply. And so toxic medicines could not treat their inside, and needles and stones
could not treat their outside; it was enough to shift the essence and invoke the
origin.

The present age is not like this. Worry and grief wind around a man from within;
hard labour injures him from without. They have also lost their compliance with the
four seasons and gone against what is fitting for cold and heat. Thief winds arrive
again and again; hollow evils come at morning and at evening. Within, it reaches as
far as the five organs and the marrow of the bones; without, it damages the
openings and the pores and the skin. Therefore a small illness must become severe
and a great illness must end in death — **and therefore the invocation cannot bring
it to an end.**

### §2 — Colour and pulse, and the decline

> 帝曰：善。余欲臨病人，觀死生，決嫌疑，欲知其要，如日月光，可得聞乎？
> 歧伯曰：色脈者，上帝之所貴也，先師之所傳也。上古使僦貸季，理色脈而通神明，
> 合之金木水火土、四時、八風、六合，不離其常，變化相移，以觀其妙，以知其要。
> 欲知其要，則色脈是矣。色以應日，脈以應月，常求其要，則其要也。
> 夫色之變化，以應四時之脈，此上帝之所貴，以合於神明也，所以遠死而近生。生道以長，命曰聖王。
> 中古之治病，至而治之，湯液十日，以去八風五痹之病。十日不已，治以草蘇草荄之枝，
> 本末為助，標本已得，邪氣乃服。
> 暮世之治病也則不然，治不本四時，不知日月，不審逆從，病形已成，乃欲微鍼治其外，
> 湯液治其內，粗工兇兇，以為可攻，故病未已，新病復起。

The Emperor said: Well said. I wish to stand before the sick, to observe life and
death, to resolve doubt and uncertainty, and to know the essentials as plainly as
the light of the sun and moon. May I hear of this?

Qibo said: Colour and pulse are what the high sovereign valued and what the
teachers before us handed down. In high antiquity, Jiu Dai-ji was set to order
colour and pulse and by that reached clarity, matching them to metal and wood and
water and fire and earth, to the four seasons, the eight winds and the six
directions, never departing from their constants; and by watching how the changes
move against one another, the subtle things were seen and the essentials known. If
you would know the essentials, then colour and pulse are they. Colour answers to
the sun; the pulse answers to the moon. Seek their essentials constantly and you
will have them. The changes of colour answer to the pulses of the four seasons —
this is what the high sovereign valued, as it accords with clarity, and it is what
lets one keep away from death and stay near to life. One who lengthens the way of
life by this is called a sage king.

In middle antiquity, illness was treated as it arrived: decoctions for ten days, to
drive out the diseases of the eight winds and the five blockages. If ten days did
not end it, treatment went on with the stems and roots of herbs, root and tip used
together as helpers; once root and branch were grasped, the evil qi submitted.

In the latter age it is not like this. Treatment does not take the four seasons as
its root; it does not know the sun and moon; it does not examine what runs with the
current and what runs against it. The shape of the disease has already formed, and
only then do they want a fine needle for the outside and a decoction for the
inside. **The crude practitioner, fierce and hurried, believes it can be attacked —
and so the illness is not finished before a new illness has already begun.**

### §3 — The one thing

> 帝曰：願聞要道。
> 歧伯曰：治之要極，無失色脈，用之不惑，治之大則。逆從倒行，標本不得，亡神失國。
> 去故就新，乃得真人。
> 帝曰：余聞其要於夫子矣，夫子言不離色脈，此余之所知也。
> 歧伯曰：治之極於一。
> 帝曰：何謂一？
> 歧伯曰：一者因得之。
> 帝曰：奈何？
> 歧伯曰：閉戶塞牖，繫之病者，數問其情，以從其意，得神者昌，失神者亡。
> 帝曰：善。

The Emperor said: I wish to hear the essential way.

Qibo said: The utmost of treatment is not to lose colour and pulse, and to use them
without confusion — this is the great rule of treatment. Run with and against the
current the wrong way round, fail to grasp root and branch, and the spirit is lost
and the state is lost with it. Discard the old and take up the new, and so one
becomes a true person.

The Emperor said: I have heard the essentials from you, Master. You say one cannot
depart from colour and pulse — this I already know.

Qibo said: The utmost of treatment lies in one thing.

The Emperor said: What is meant by "one"?

Qibo said: The one is obtained by asking.

The Emperor said: How so?

Qibo said: **Shut the door and close the window; stay with the patient; ask again
and again after his condition and follow where his own account leads. The one who
keeps his spirit flourishes; the one who loses it dies.**

The Emperor said: Well said.

### Four departures from the supplied English

- **移精 read as *shift the essence*, not *shift the spirit*.** 精 (jing) is
  essence; 神 (shen) is spirit. The supplied PDF renders both as "spirit," and that
  collapse destroys the chapter's own arc — the old method **moved the 精**, and the
  final sentence is about keeping the **神**. Blocks 6, 10 and 80 depend on the
  distinction and it is preserved throughout, on screen and in the cards.
- **祝由 rendered as *invocation* and *speaking to the origin*, never as "reciting
  incantations."** 由 is the origin or from-what; the practice addressed the
  *cause*, and "reciting incantations" loses the second character entirely. Block
  11 gives the term and its two halves on screen rather than smuggling the choice
  past the viewer.
- **一者因得之 read as *the one is obtained by asking*.** The supplied English gives
  "attaining it through proper causes," which is not wrong but leaves the Emperor's
  next question unmotivated. 因 here is the asking-after that the very next sentence
  spells out — 數問其情. The reading is the chapter's own, one clause later.
- **粗工兇兇 rendered as *fierce and hurried*, not "unskilled physicians in great
  haste."** 粗工 is the coarse or crude workman, an insult with a class edge, and
  兇兇 is a doubled character. Block 69 says on screen that it is a repetition,
  because the repetition is the tone.

so the characters above are not from the PDF and must be checked against a
