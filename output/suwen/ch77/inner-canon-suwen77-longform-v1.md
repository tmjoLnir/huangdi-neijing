## Why 15:30, and not 14:00 or 17:00

The requested window was 14–17 minutes with a hard floor of 11:30, and the brief asked
for the length that minimises padding and shortening. **93 blocks — 15:30 — is where
this chapter's usable material actually lands**, and the number is odd on purpose: it
is what the arithmetic gives, and rounding it to 90 or 96 would have to come out of a
charge or go into filler.

This chapter's problem is not the one Lingshu 43 had. That chapter was a catalogue of
twenty-seven equal entries, so its length was a multiplication. **This one is five
charges of wildly unequal size.** The text gives the third fault a single sentence and
the first and fifth a paragraph each, so an episode that gives each charge the same
runtime is padding three of them and rushing two. Costing it by what each part carries
without inventing filler:

| Material | Blocks | Why that many |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open — the patient no examination explains, the answer stated bare, the bench, citation, title, thesis, the auditor's brief | 8 | the frame, and the hook stated before anything is quoted |
| Act I — the abyss and the clouds, the sage's standard, **the title unpacked**, the five faults and four virtues announced, Leigong's bow, the dating caveat | 9 | the chapter's own opening scene, which is also the episode's staging |
| Act II — **first charge**: 脫營 and 失精, the null examination, the wasting, the good physician in the dock, the social gradient, bereavement | 14 | the longest passage in the text and the whole reason the chapter is on the slate |
| Act III — **second charge**: diet and dwelling, sudden joy and sudden grief, 暴怒傷陰, the counterflow, the foolish physician | 12 | the second-longest passage and the one that puts *joy* on a list of harms |
| Act IV — **third charge**: 比類奇恆從容, the technician, **the midpoint refusal**, the Hippocratic comparison | 8 | **one sentence in the source.** Anything longer is padding, and this is where an even split would have put it |
| Act V — **fourth charge**: the three constants, the fallen noble, **the 神 debunk**, 醫不能嚴不能動神, and the reckoning that refuses this fault | 13 | the fault a modern ethicist rejects, which needs its counterweight in the same act |
| Act VI — **fifth charge**: 終始 and 餘緒, the emotional catalogue, the old injury, the coarse practitioner, the death date | 13 | the chapter's most graphic passage, held at report distance, plus the exclusion stated on screen |
| Act VII — the verdict: 受術不通 and 人事不明, the curriculum, **the four virtues that never arrive**, two lost books, both reckonings, Engel, the fan goes down, thesis, end card | 16 | the chapter's own summary plus the episode's two reckonings, neither of which can be a single line at fifteen minutes |

That totals **93**. Both edges of the requested window cost something real:

- **14:00 is 84 blocks — 9 short**, and there is no clean nine. The only nine that can go
  without breaking a charge or losing a refusal is **the whole comparative apparatus plus
  five blocks of unpacking**: the social gradient, bereavement mortality, the Greek case
  notes and the 1977 paper, plus five blocks that explain what the text has just said.
  **Cost:** every comparison the episode makes with another medical tradition, ancient or
  modern, which is what makes this intellectual history rather than an indictment. It is
  available and is specified block by block in *Runtime levers*, and it is the version to
  make if the runtime is forced down.
- **17:00 is 102 blocks — 9 too many, and only three of the nine are good.** Those three
  are named in *Runtime levers* as the 16:00 stretch: the 八正九候 apparatus, a fuller
  Hippocratic *Epidemics* case, and Suwen 78's four failings given their own beat.
  **The remaining six are not.** The material left is 明堂 face inspection, which imports
  physiognomy into an episode that has no occult surface at all, or a sixth-charge-shaped
  segment the chapter does not contain. **The honest answer is that 17:00 is reachable
  and that six of the nine blocks that get it there are padding**, which is precisely
  what the brief asked to avoid.
- **11:30 (69 blocks) is the format floor, not a target.** It cannot be reached without
  dropping two whole charges, at which point the episode is called *The Five Ways* and
  delivers three.

**15:30 is the recommendation.** It sits inside the requested window rather than at
either edge, it is where the usable material genuinely stops, and the thing that sets
the length is not the source's word count but **five charges that the source itself
sizes unequally** — 14, 12, 8, 13 and 13 blocks, in that order, because that is the
order of the evidence the chapter attaches to each.

## What this episode argues

The obvious reading of this chapter is that ancient Chinese medicine had a code of
medical ethics. That reading is close enough to be useful and wrong in the way that
matters, and the slate names this chapter's one discipline separately: *"do not extend
the argument into criticism of any identifiable contemporary healthcare system, which
would reclassify the episode as controversial-issues content."*

**The spine is that this is the oldest surviving text to make a patient's circumstances
into evidence and a physician's failure to ask about them into malpractice.** Four
things happen in it:

1. **It names two illnesses after losses rather than after organs.** A man who was noble
   and is not has 脫營. A man who was rich and is not has 失精. Neither name points at a
   part of the body and neither points at a symptom. **Both point at a biography.**
2. **It then insists the examination will come back empty.** Nothing in the depots and
   nothing changed in the form; the physician examines and doubts and cannot name what
   he has. **The chapter's position is that a clean examination is a finding rather than
   a discharge**, and that a physician who takes it as a discharge has already failed.
3. **The accused descends.** The first charge convicts 良工, the *good* physician. The
   second convicts 愚醫, the foolish one. The fifth convicts 粗工, the coarse one. **The
   text's best practitioner is the one it charges first**, which is not how a complaint
   is written and is exactly how a standard is written.
4. **And then it stops short of the thing it seems to be arguing for.** Having made a
   man's rank and his ruin into clinical evidence, the chapter's remedy is still to
   drain what is full and supplement what is empty. **It never once says to change the
   circumstances.** It says to know them.

**The thesis, stated once and hedged hard:** there is no mechanism in this chapter and
no quantity anywhere in it, 脫營 and 失精 are names rather than explanations, and its
fourth fault — that a physician who cannot command a room fails — is an argument for
authority that a modern ethicist would refuse outright. What the chapter has instead is
the right *question*, asked eighteen centuries before the profession that inherited it
was willing to ask it again. **The episode says that out loud at blocks 86 to 88 and
again at 92, and the reckoning is deliberately not saved for the end**: the midpoint
carries its own at block 47.

**The dramatic engine is the hearing, and the inversion is the point.** In every other
episode Fan-di interrogates and Dr-Qi answers. Here **Fan-di presides and reads the
charges**, because in the source it is the Emperor who does the teaching; **Lei-Gong is
the student in the dock**, because in the source it is Leigong who admits he has never
heard of any of it; and **Dr-Qi is written in as the practising physician who attaches
a case to each charge**, because Qibo is not in this chapter at all and somebody has to
have seen these patients. **The fan is the tell** — Fan-di has it open and moving
through all five charges, which is the cast sheet's *performing* setting, and it goes
down at **block 89**, when he stops prosecuting and admits he is the second party in the
room.

**Lei-Gong is the auditor and the accused at the same time**, which is this episode's
one structural joke and it is not played for laughs. He keeps the record of a hearing
whose subject is his own ignorance. His eleven blocks are findings — that the charge
sheet is real (8); that he has heard of neither the faults nor the virtues (14); that
the first charge convicts the *good* physician (27); that the accused descends from good
to foolish to coarse (40); that the third charge is the only one about method (49); that
the three constants are all about rank and ruin and ambition (54); that the chapter
gives no method for the thing the fourth fault demands (63); that it faults a man for
naming the death date and then accepts the date (76); that the four virtues never arrive
(82); that the cure it does prescribe is to read two books that no longer exist (84);
and the verdict at 90.

> `inner-canon-suwen13-longform-v1.md` and `inner-canon-suwen8-longform-v1.md` are both
> written to **Arthur 31–34 and Vesper 37–41**, which were superseded on 2026-08-10, and
> both Lingshu 28 longform documents write Lei-Gong at **34–39** against a measured
> 47–52. On the run that superseded the first pair, a 32-word Arthur line — legal and
> mid-range under the old table — came back at **6.446s, more than two seconds under the
> assembler's floor**, which is a hard error that stops the whole build. **Every Arthur
> block below is 38–44 words, every Vesper block is 41–43 and every Zane block is
> 47–52.**

**Every one of the 93 blocks below is inside its voice's range**, verified by parsing
this document's own narration table and dropping tokens containing no letter or digit —
because a spaced em-dash is not a word and counting it as one spends real budget on a
window 1.4s wide.

Four consequences shape every line here, and none is stylistic:

- **The short interjection is dead.** Under an 8.6s floor there is no quick objection
  from the bench and no one-line ruling, and `--clip-seconds` moves the window rather
  than widening it. **Every character line is a full paragraph.** In a courtroom form
  that is a real cost: a hearing is made of short exchanges and this one cannot have
  any. It is why Lei-Gong has eleven blocks rather than thirty — at 47–52 words each, an
  auditor who reacts to everything becomes the narrator.
- **No comma list of three or more items appears anywhere in this script, and there is
  not a single comma in any of the 93 narration cells.** The 2026-08-09 measurement put
  a 31-word Arthur line with seven commas at **12.022s** against **8.913s** for the same
  31 words with none — a 3.1s swing from punctuation alone, on a window 1.4s wide.
  **This chapter enumerates constantly** — five faults, three constants, four emotions,
  a curriculum of a dozen items — so it is close to the worst case for that failure.
  Every enumeration below is joined with *and* or split into sentences. **Do not add
  one.**
- **The rates are survivor-biased.** ~4.4 w/s is what Arthur's *passing* takes
  delivered, and his rate moved between **3.98 and 4.80 w/s within a single seven-block
  cut** on lines of near-identical length. A word count predicts a take only to about
  ±1.5s. **Generate two variants per block and keep the better one** — at ~1.45 a take
  that is the cheapest reliable method.
- **Reach the window with words, not full stops.** The assembler warns on internal pauses
  ≥0.8s, so padding a thin line with sentence breaks passes the duration gate and trips
  the pause warning. **No block below exceeds four sentences.**

These are **arithmetic against measured rates, not measurements**. Treat the first pass
as calibration and budget re-takes explicitly — the preflight below does.

### How to read this script

`CLAUDE.md` asks for **SOUND / VISUAL / CHARACTER** blocks and a numbered shot list;
`build_subtitles.js` needs a **three-column narration table** to build the required
`.srt`/`.vtt` sidecars. They are combined rather than duplicated:

- **The narration tables are canonical for words.** Blocks run continuously **1–93
  across the whole episode**, never per-act; the assembly manifest is flat and act-local
  numbering produces off-by-one errors that are expensive to find in a fifteen-minute
  render.
- **The SOUND / VISUAL / direction prose between them is canonical for performance**,
  and the **numbered shot list after block 93 is canonical for picture.** Neither
  restates a narration line.
- **The speaker is named in the beat column**, not a fourth column — the parser reads
  exactly three, and a fourth would leak into the caption text.
- **No markdown inside a narration cell.** `build_subtitles.js` copies the third column
  into the cue verbatim and strips nothing, so a `**bold**` emphasis burns into the
  picture as literal asterisks. **Put emphasis in the direction prose, never in the
  line.**

> **Do not insert a table whose first cell is a bare integer anywhere between blocks 1
> and 93.** `parseNarration` keeps the first consecutive run from block 1 and stops at
> the first row that breaks it, so a stray numeric table silently truncates the sidecar.
> Every other table in this document sits before block 1 or after block 93 for that
> reason, and the ON-SCREEN TEXT card table prefixes its first column with `Block` so it
> cannot collide. The shot list after block 93 is a numbered *list*, not a table, for the
> same reason.

## The hearing — the visual system

The slate assigns this chapter the **tribunal** form, and §2.2 makes clear why that
matters beyond taste: twenty episodes in the same Q&A shape is the Generic/Repetitive
Content failure mode, so **every episode carries a distinct structural form.** Here the
form is staged as a procedure, and the argument is staged as an absence.

**The whole episode plays in one room — a bare hall of review with a long low lacquer
bench across it, five bamboo tallies laid face up on the bench, and a plain empty mat
facing it where the accused would kneel and nobody ever does.**

| Act | The charge | The light, the tallies and the camera |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | none yet | first light from one side; five tallies face up; camera at bench height and still |
| Act I | the court convenes | the light reaches the first tally; nothing turned yet |
| Act II — **first tally down** | once noble and now nothing | light a quarter across the floor; one step closer |
| Act III — **second tally down** | what he ate and where he slept | light at the middle of the floor; one step closer |
| Act IV — **third tally down** | the technician | light past the bench; one step closer |
| Act V — **fourth tally down** | the physician who could not hold the room | long low light; one step closer, and the bench fills the frame |
| Act VI — **fifth tally down** | the man who only said the date | the light has gone off the floor and onto the wall; closest position |
| Act VII | the verdict | **the light is gone and the lamps are lit deliberately, and it is the widest shot in the episode** |

Three rules keep it from becoming decoration:

- **Nothing is ever examined, in any of the ninety-three shots.** No wrist is taken, no
  tongue is looked at, no body is touched, and **every hand that moves toward a body
  stops short and withdraws** — the wrist cushion in shots 23 and 74 has no arm on it
  and never gets one. This is the episode's argument rendered as a rule about the
  picture: the chapter's finding is that the examination comes back empty, so a shot of
  a successful examination says the opposite of its own narration.
- **Every case is carried by an object the man's life left behind, and never by the
  man.** A seal cord with no seal on it. An empty seal box. A counting board with the
  counters swept off. A court robe folded flat with the insignia unpicked. A bridle with
  no horse. **There is no patient in this episode**, and the mat facing the bench is
  empty in every shot it appears in. That is `CLAUDE.md`'s *portraits not bodies* taken
  literally: the portraits here are of possessions.
- **The register never changes.** Everything stays flat 2D ink-wash, and the three
  modern comparisons are carried by Arthur's voice over the same brushed idiom.
  **There is no photograph, no hospital, no consulting room, no white coat, no
  stethoscope, no chart and no scan in ninety-three shots.** For this chapter that is
  the single largest prompt-stage risk, because any one of them turns an episode about a
  Han-dynasty manual into commentary on contemporary medicine — which is the exact
  reclassification the slate rules out.

**One more rule, and it is the episode's closing image.** The five tallies turn face
down one at a time as each charge is proven, at shots 31, 43, 51, 64 and 77. **Four
blank slips lie beside them from shot 82 onward and nobody writes on any of them.** They
are the four virtues, and the episode never fills them in because the chapter never
does.

### §1 — the lament, the standard, and the announcement

> 黃帝曰：嗚呼遠哉。閔閔乎若視深淵，若迎浮雲，視深淵尚可測，迎浮雲莫知其際。聖人之術，為萬民式，論裁志意，必有法則，循經守數，按循醫事，為萬民副，故事有五過四德，汝知之乎。雷公避席再拜曰：臣年幼小，蒙愚以惑，不聞五過與四德，比類形名虛引其經，心無所對。

**Working translation.** *The Yellow Emperor said: Alas, how far off it is. Anxious as
one gazing into a deep abyss, or as one facing drifting cloud. The abyss can still be
sounded; the cloud gives you no way to know where it ends. The art of the sage is a
pattern for all the people. In weighing intention and judgement there must be rules and
principles — following the channels and keeping to the numbers, attending to medical
affairs, and so standing as a support to the people. Therefore in this business there
are Five Faults and Four Virtues. Do you know them?* — *Leigong left his mat and bowed
twice and said: I am young and small. I am clouded by ignorance and confused by it. I
have not heard of the Five Faults or of the Four Virtues. I can compare shapes and names
and cite the classics emptily, and my mind has nothing to answer with.*

### §2 — the five faults

> 帝曰：凡未診病者，必問嘗貴後賤，雖不中邪，病從內生，名曰脫營。嘗富後貧，名曰失精，五氣留連，病有所并。醫工診之，不在藏府，不變軀形，診之而疑，不知病名。身體日減，氣虛無精，病深無氣，洒洒然時驚，病深者，以其外耗於衛，內奪於榮。良工所失，不知病情，此亦治之一過也。凡欲診病者，必問飲食居處，暴樂暴苦，始樂後苦，皆傷精氣，精氣竭絕，形體毀沮。暴怒傷陰，暴喜傷陽，厥氣上行，滿脈去形。愚醫治之，不知補寫，不知病情，精華日脫，邪氣乃并，此治之二過也。善為脈者，必以比類奇恆從容知之，為工而不知道，此診之不足貴，此治之三過也。診有三常，必問貴賤，封君敗傷，及欲侯王。故貴脫勢，雖不中邪，精神內傷，身必敗亡。始富後貧，雖不傷邪，皮焦筋屈，痿躄為攣。醫不能嚴，不能動神，外為柔弱，亂至失常，病不能移，則醫事不行，此治之四過也。凡診者，必知終始，有知餘緒，切脈問名，當合男女。離絕菀結，憂恐喜怒，五藏空虛，血氣離守，工不能知，何術之語。嘗富大傷，斬筋絕脈，身體復行，令澤不息。故傷敗結留，薄歸陽，膿積寒炅。粗工治之，亟刺陰陽，身體解散，四支轉筋，死日有期，醫不能明，不問所發，唯言死日，亦為粗工，此治之五過也。

**Working translation.** *The Emperor said: Before examining any patient, you must ask
whether he was once noble and afterwards brought low. Even where no evil has struck him,
illness arises from within, and this is called tuoying — the shedding of the
constructive. Once rich and afterwards poor is called shijing — the loss of the essence.
The five breaths linger, and illnesses run together. The practitioner examines him: it
is not in the depots and palaces, and nothing has altered in the frame of his body. He
examines and doubts, and cannot name the illness. The body diminishes day by day, the
breath is depleted and there is no essence, the illness is deep and there is no breath
left, and he is startled and shivering at intervals. Where the illness runs deep it is
because he is spent without in the defensive and robbed within in the constructive. What
the skilled physician misses is that he does not know the circumstances of the illness.
This too is the first fault of treatment.*

*Whoever would examine a patient must ask about his food and drink and where he lives,
and about sudden joy and sudden hardship, and about joy first and hardship after. All of
these injure the essence and the breath. When essence and breath are exhausted, the frame
is ruined and broken down. Sudden anger injures the yin and sudden joy injures the yang.
The counterflowing breath runs upward, fills the vessels, and departs from the form. A
foolish physician treats him without knowing whether to supplement or to drain, and
without knowing the circumstances of the illness. So the man's finest part sheds away day
by day and the evil breath closes over it. This is the second fault of treatment.*

*One who is good at the pulse must know it by comparing like with like, by the anomalous
against the constant, and with composure. To practise and not know that way — such
diagnosis is not worth valuing. This is the third fault of treatment.*

*In diagnosis there are three constants. You must ask about high station and low; about
whether he held a fief and was broken and injured; and about whether he wishes to become
a marquis or a king. So when a man of standing loses his position, even where no evil has
struck him, essence and spirit are injured within and the body must go to ruin. Once rich
and afterwards poor, even where no evil has injured him, the skin scorches and the sinews
contract, and wasting and lameness turn to cramp. If the physician cannot be strict and
cannot move the spirit, being outwardly soft and weak, disorder arrives and the normal is
lost; the illness cannot be shifted, and then the practice of medicine does not work.
This is the fourth fault of treatment.*

*Whoever examines must know the beginning and the end, and know the remaining threads;
take the pulse and ask the name, and fit these to man or woman. Separation and severance
and pent-up knotting; worry and fear and joy and anger — the five depots go empty and the
blood and breath leave their guard. If the practitioner cannot know this, what art is
there to speak of? A man once rich and greatly injured* **[the clauses describing the
injury and its outcome are excluded — see below]** *…the harm settles and lodges and
turns toward the yang.* **[excluded]** *A coarse practitioner treats him and punctures
yin and yang in haste, and the body comes apart* **[excluded]** *and the day of death has
its term. The physician cannot make it clear, does not ask where it began, and speaks
only the day of death. He too is a coarse practitioner. This is the fifth fault of
treatment.*

### §3 — the verdict and the curriculum

> 凡此五者，皆受術不通，人事不明也。故曰：聖人之治病也，必知天地陰陽四時經紀五藏六府雌雄表裏刺灸砭石毒藥所主，從容人事，以明經道，貴賤貧富，各異品理，問年少長，勇怯之理，審於分部，知病本始，八正九候，診必副矣。治病之道，氣內為寶，循求其理，求之不得，過在表裏。守數據治，無失俞理，能行此術，終身不殆。不知俞理，五藏菀熟，癰發六府。診病不審，是謂失常，謹守此治，與經相明，上經下經，揆度陰陽，奇恆五中，決以明堂，審於終始，可以橫行。

**Working translation.** *All five of these arise from having received the art without
being versed in it, and from not being clear about human affairs. Therefore it is said:
when the sage treats illness he must know heaven and earth, yin and yang, the ordering of
the four seasons, the five depots and six palaces, male and female, exterior and
interior, and what needling and moxibustion and the stone lancet and the potent drugs
each govern. He must be composed in human affairs, so as to make the way of the channels
clear. High and low and rich and poor are each a different category with its own
principle. Ask whether he is young or old; understand the principle of boldness and
timidity; examine the divisions of the body with care; know where the illness began; and
apply the eight rectitudes and the nine indicators. Only then will the diagnosis match.
The way of treating illness holds the breath within as its treasure. Follow and seek its
principle; if you seek and do not find it, the fault lies in the exterior and interior.
Keep to the numbers in treating, and do not lose the principle of the transport points.
One who can practise this art will not be endangered to the end of his life. Not knowing
the principle of the points, the five depots become pent up and overheated and abscesses
break out in the six palaces. Examining a patient without care is called losing the norm.
Keep carefully to this method and make it clear against the classics — the upper classic
and the lower classic — measure yin and yang, the anomalous and the constant and the five
centres, decide by the Hall of Brightness, and examine the beginning and the end. Then one
may go anywhere.*

### Departures, exclusions and things to verify

Eleven items, and each is argued rather than asserted.

1. **The source reads 凡風診者 where standard editions read 凡診者, and the supplied
   English follows the 風.** It renders the fifth fault's opening as *"In diagnosing
   wind-related conditions."* This script reads **凡診者** — *whoever examines* — and
   block 65 is written to that reading. The 風 is generally taken as an intrusion, and
   nothing else in that passage is about wind. **This is the one philological judgement
   in the script that changes what a whole charge is about, and it should be checked
   against a reference edition before block 65's take and its card are recorded.**
2. **The source reads 精致神內傷 where the received text reads 精神內傷.** The supplied
   English renders it *"their essence and spirit suffer internal injury,"* which follows
   the standard reading rather than its own Chinese — so the source is internally
   inconsistent here. Block 55 follows **精神內傷**. It is recorded because it is a signal
   about the provenance of the export rather than because anything depends on it.
3. **寫 in 不知補寫 reads as 瀉, to drain.** The character in the received text is the
   older graph and it is not *to write*. Block 39 renders it as *drain*. Anyone reading
   the passage with a modern dictionary gets the wrong verb, which is why the working
   translation states it here.
4. **過 is rendered as *fault* and unpacked as an *overstepping*, and the episode says so
   out loud at block 12.** The supplied title reads *"On the Five Faults"* and that is
   kept, because it is the standard English. But 過 is a going-past rather than a crime,
   and 疏 in the title means to set something out item by item rather than to neglect it.
   **Block 12 exists to spring that** rather than to correct it quietly.
5. **脫營 and 失精 are kept as transliterations and glossed rather than translated.**
   Blocks 18 to 21 give *tuoying* and *shijing* and then gloss them as the shedding of the
   constructive and the loss of the essence. Translating them outright would make them
   sound like diagnoses; leaving them bare would make them sound like magic. **The gloss
   is the episode's own observation that both names are built out of losing something**,
   which is a reading of the text's structure and is marked as ours at block 21.
6. **The four virtues are announced at 五過四德 and never expounded in the received
   chapter.** This is not an editorial claim; it is what the text does. **What is a claim
   is block 83's statement of where the question stands** — that commentators have divided
   over whether a passage is lost or whether §3 is meant to stand in for the virtues.
   **Verify that characterisation against a reference edition before block 83's take is
   recorded.** Note also that the neighbouring chapter, **Suwen 78 《徵四失論》, lists four
   *failings* and not four virtues**, and the slate names it as a substitute chapter for
   this one; the episode does not conflate the two and block 83 does not name it.
7. **當合男女 is read as fitting the questions to the patient's circumstances rather than
   to two kinds of body.** Blocks 67 and 68 say so and 68 marks the reading as ours. The
   line is four characters and commentators have taken it more than one way. The reading
   here is supported by its neighbours, which are about grief and money and standing —
   but it is a judgement and it is the second thing on this list to verify.
8. **令澤不息 is textually uncertain and the script does not render it.** Commentators
   divide on the reading and no line in the episode depends on it. Block 71's *he is not
   recovered, he is upright* is written from 身體復行 alone.
9. **Three clauses are excluded on compliance grounds and none is an oversight.**
   **膿積寒炅** — the injury turning suppurative with alternating cold and heat — and
   **四支轉筋** — the limbs twisting at the end — appear in **no narration line and no
   shot**, and **斬筋絕脈** is summarised as *badly hurt* at block 71 rather than rendered.
   **Block 73 states on screen that clauses have been left out**, so that a viewer is not
   misled and so that nobody later restores them believing they were overlooked. They are
   marked in the working translation above for the same reason. **Do not add them at any
   runtime.**
10. **八正九候 and 明堂 are named in §3 and are deliberately not unpacked.** The eight
    rectitudes and nine indicators are another chapter's apparatus and unpacking them
    costs four blocks for no argument. **明堂 is excluded for a stronger reason**: it is
    inspection of the face, and an episode with no occult surface anywhere would acquire
    one by explaining physiognomy in its last two minutes. *Runtime levers* names the
    八正九候 blocks as available at 16:00 and rules 明堂 out at any runtime.
11. **The count of five is the chapter's own** and needs no arithmetic — unlike the
    twenty-seven of Lingshu 43, which is a sum this series performs on the text's own two
