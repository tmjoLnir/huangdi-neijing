# Su Wen, Chapter 6 — 靈蘭祕典論篇第六

**English translation for the series.** *Líng Lán Mì Diǎn Lùn* — "Discourse on the
Secret Canon of the Spirit-Orchid Chamber."

> A dramatized adaptation of a classical philosophical text. Not medical advice.
> Nothing in this chapter is a treatment, a dosage or a diagnosis, and nothing
> below should be read as one.

## About this translation

The source is the received classical text of this chapter, compiled around the
Han period and long in the **public domain**. **This English rendering is
original work made for this series**, not a reproduction of any published
translation.

**Provenance, recorded because it changed mid-task.** The chapter was first
requested from `8bei8.com`, which is **unreachable from the repo host** — the
agent proxy denies the domain outright (`gateway answered 403 to CONNECT`), as it
also denied `ctext.org` and `zh.wikisource.org`. The same block is recorded in
`inner-canon-ch1-translation-v4.md`, so it is a standing condition here, not a
one-off. The user then supplied the page as a **PDF** (太极书馆 edition, captured
2026-08-06), and the 原文 below is transcribed from that PDF. The edition's own
modern 注释 (notes) and 译文 (paraphrase) are **deliberately omitted** — they were
excluded by the brief, and nothing in this file derives from them.

The PDF is in **simplified** characters; the text below is given in
**traditional**, matching the rest of `output/`. The conversion touches
orthography only. One case is worth naming: the source writes 十二脏, rendered
here as 十二臟 — many received editions print 十二藏 in the older orthography, and
the three are the same word.

### ⚠ The chapter number is not the one you will find elsewhere

This edition numbers the chapter **篇第六**, the sixth, and the series slate
follows it — hence `output/episode-6/`. **In the received Wang Bing recension
that the rest of the world cites, this chapter is Su Wen 8.** The repo's own
blueprint already reconciles the two, listing it as *"Ch 06 (Su Wen 8)"*.

Both numbers are correct within their own edition, and neither is a mistake.
**Cite it on screen as Su Wen 8**, which is what a viewer checking the text will
find; keep `ch6` for the folder and the file names, which follow the input
script. Su Wen 6 in the received ordering is a completely different chapter
(陰陽離合論, on the separation and union of yin and yang) — so an unqualified
"Chapter 6" on screen would point viewers at the wrong text.

### Style

It is deliberately **plain rather than literary**. The series' arguments turn on
words that smoother translations often smooth away — 貴賤 *"noble and base"*,
不得相失 *"must not lose one another"*, 戒之戒之 *"beware, beware"* — so those are
rendered explicitly even where it costs elegance.

The chapter's governing conceit is that each organ holds a **post in a
government**. 官 is rendered throughout as **"office"** rather than "official",
because the Chinese names the position and not the person — which is the whole
argument of the chapter's second half.

Technical terms are left transliterated where English has no honest equivalent
(*qi*, *yin/yang*, *san jiao*, *shan zhong*) and glossed on first use. Bracketed
words are supplied for sense and are not in the Chinese.

## 1. The twelve offices

The Yellow Emperor asked:

> 願聞十二臟之相使，貴賤何如？

*"I should like to hear how the twelve depots assist one another — which are
noble and which are base?"*

Qi Bo replied:

> 悉乎哉問也。請遂言之！

*"What a thorough question. Allow me to go through it."*

> 心者，君主之官也，神明出焉。

*The **heart** is the office of the **sovereign**; spirit-brightness issues from
it.*

> 肺者，相傅之官，治節出焉。

*The **lung** is the office of the **chancellor**; regulation and measure issue
from it.*

> 肝者，將軍之官，謀慮出焉。

*The **liver** is the office of the **general**; planning and deliberation issue
from it.*

> 膽者，中正之官，決斷出焉。

*The **gallbladder** is the office of **the upright and impartial**; judgement
issues from it.*

> 膻中者，臣使之官，喜樂出焉。

*The **shan zhong** — the centre of the chest, the heart's enclosure — is the
office of the **envoy**; joy and gladness issue from it.*

> 脾胃者，倉廩之官，五味出焉。

*The **spleen and stomach** are the office of the **granaries**; the five
flavours issue from them.*

> 大腸者，傳道之官，變化出焉。

*The **large intestine** is the office of **conveyance along the road**;
transformation issues from it.*

> 小腸者，受盛之官，化物出焉。

*The **small intestine** is the office of **receiving what is sent full**; the
transforming of things issues from it.*

> 腎者，作強之官，伎巧出焉。

*The **kidney** is the office of **exertion**; skill and dexterity issue from
it.*

> 三焦者，決瀆之官，水道出焉。

*The **san jiao** — the "triple burner" — is the office of the **dredger of
channels**; the waterways issue from it.*

> 膀胱者，州都之官，津液藏焉，氣化則能出矣。

*The **bladder** is the office of the **regional seat where the waters gather**;
the fluids are stored in it, and only when qi transforms them can they issue.*

> 凡此十二官者，不得相失也。

*"**These twelve offices must not lose one another.**"*

**Note.** The Emperor asked which are 貴 noble and which 賤 base. The answer
names twelve posts and then, in its last clause, declines the ranking: what
matters is that none of them loses the others. 不得相失 is the hinge of the
chapter.

## 2. The sovereign's clarity

> 故主明則下安，以此養生則壽，歿世不殆，以為天下則大昌。

*"So when the sovereign is clear-sighted, those below are at peace. Nourish life
by this and there is length of years; to the end of one's days there is no peril.
Govern the world by this and it flourishes greatly."*

> 主不明則十二官危，使道閉塞而不通，形乃大傷，
> 以此養生則殃，以為天下者，其宗大危，戒之戒之。

*"When the sovereign is not clear-sighted, the twelve offices are in danger. The
roads of dispatch are blocked and do not open, and the body is gravely harmed.
Nourish life by this and there is calamity. Govern the world by this and the
ancestral line is in great peril. **Beware of it. Beware of it.**"*

**Note.** The passage runs the same sentence twice, once for a body and once for
a state, on the same grammar — 以此養生 / 以為天下. The doubling is the argument,
not decoration. And the warning is addressed, in the fiction of the text, to a
reigning emperor.

## 3. The Way is in the minute

> 至道在微，變化無窮，孰知其原。

*"The utmost Way lies in what is minute. Its transformations are without end. Who
knows its source?"*

> 窘乎哉，消者瞿瞿，孰知其要。閔閔之當，孰者為良。

*"How hard it is. The discerning look and look; who knows its essentials? The
matter is deep and obscure; who shall say what is sound?"*

> 恍惚之數，生於毫釐，毫釐之數，起於度量，
> 千之萬之，可以益大，推之大之，其形乃制。

*"Numbers too faint to see are born from the finest hair's breadth. The hair's
breadth itself arises from measurement. Thousand it and ten-thousand it, and it
can be made greater; extend it and enlarge it, and only then does its form take
shape."*

**Note.** The register changes completely here. The court metaphor stops, and the
chapter closes its argument on **measurement** — the large is reached by
multiplying the small, and the small is reached by measuring. This is the passage
most often dropped from retellings, and it is the one that qualifies everything
before it.

## 4. The sealed room

> 黃帝曰：善哉，余聞精光之道，大聖之業，而宣明大道，
> 非齋戒擇吉日不敢受也。

*The Yellow Emperor said: "Excellent. I have heard the Way of pure radiance, the
work of the great sages, proclaiming and making plain the great Way. Without
fasting and purification, and without choosing an auspicious day, I would not
dare receive it."*

> 黃帝乃擇吉日良兆，而藏靈蘭之室，以傳保焉。

*So the Yellow Emperor chose an auspicious day of good omen, and stored it in the
**Chamber of the Spirit-Orchid**, to be handed on and preserved.*

**Note.** The chapter ends by describing its own reception. The teaching is not
argued for, tested, or applied — it is fasted over, dated, and locked in a named
room. The title of the chapter comes from that room.

## Reading notes for production

Four things the series treats as load-bearing, all recoverable from the Chinese
above:

1. **The Emperor asks for a ranking and does not get one.** 貴賤何如 asks which
   organs are noble and which base. The reply lists twelve posts and ends on
   不得相失 — *they must not lose one another*. A hierarchy is requested; a
   dependency is delivered.
2. **The metaphor is governmental, not anatomical.** Every term — 官, 君主, 相傅,
   將軍, 中正, 臣使, 倉廩, 州都 — is a post in an administration. The chapter is
   describing how a system holds together, using the only complex system its
   audience already knew.
3. **The warning is aimed at the listener.** 主不明則十二官危 places the whole
   risk on one post, and the man being told this is a sovereign. 戒之戒之 —
   *beware, beware* — is a physician saying it twice to an emperor.
4. **The chapter qualifies itself, then seals itself.** §3 leaves the court
   entirely for 毫釐, hair's breadths, and grounds the whole edifice in
   measurement; §4 then puts the text in a locked room. Those two moves point in
   opposite directions, and the tension between them is the honest subject of the
   episode.

**One count to keep straight.** The chapter says 十二臟 and 十二官 — twelve — but
names **eleven organs plus one non-organ**: 三焦, the san jiao or "triple burner",
has no anatomical referent, and 膻中 names a location in the chest rather than a
discrete organ. The list reaches twelve because 脾胃, spleen and stomach, share a
single office while counting as two. Any on-screen tally has to survive a viewer
counting along.
