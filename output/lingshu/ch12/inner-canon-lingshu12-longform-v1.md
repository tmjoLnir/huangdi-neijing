## Why 15:10, and not 14:00 or 17:00

The requested window was 14–17 minutes with a hard floor of 11:30, and the brief asked
for the length that minimises padding and shortening. **91 blocks — 15:10 — is where
this chapter's usable material actually stops.** The number is odd on purpose: it is
what the arithmetic gives, and rounding it to 90 or 96 would have to come out of the
survey or go into filler.

This chapter has a shape the previous three did not. **It is short on text and long on
consequence.** Five sections, one of them excluded outright, and one of the remaining
four is a bare list of twelve one-line pairings. So the episode cannot be paced by the
source's word count — it is paced by how much argument each passage will carry without
being stretched:

| Material | Blocks | Why that many |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open — the hook, the reframe, the commission, the book, the terms, citation, title and thesis, the auditor's brief | 8 | the frame, and the promise the episode has to keep about the missing river |
| Act I — §1 entire: the twelve and the twelve, what *jing* means, the four verbs, **the destination stated and the exclusion stated**, the dating caveat | 11 | the chapter's own question, which is also the only place the exclusion can be announced before anybody is invested |
| Act II — §2's first half: the refusal, the six directions, **the man of eight chi**, the standard-figure caveat | 10 | the argument that makes the chapter matter, and it is four sentences of source |
| Act III — **the survey**: twelve correspondences, the sea crux, the sanjiao, the Four Waterways, **the ordering finding** | 19 | **the longest passage in the chapter and the slate's assigned form.** Twelve pairs cannot be a montage — the whole finding is that nothing distinguishes one pair from another |
| Act IV — **the turn**: 其死可解剖而視之, the word, the hedge, Wang Mang, Alexandria, 皆有大數 | 13 | the reason this chapter is ranked third on the slate, plus both comparative cases and the hedge that keeps them honest |
| Act V — the ring, the bearings across a real map, **此一隅之陰陽也** | 8 | the chapter's own admission that it is local, which nobody quotes |
| Act VI — **the river that is not there**: the Ji, the Four Waterways, the avulsion, Jinan | 8 | the cold open's promise, paid; it is the episode's most retainable single fact and it is not in the source at all |
| Act VII — §5: the middle measure, the man who cannot be measured, **the exclusion restated**, Quetelet, the reference man, both reckonings, the fan goes down, the close | 14 | four sentences of source carrying the episode's second thesis, plus two reckonings neither of which can be one line at fifteen minutes |

That totals **91**. Both edges of the requested window cost something real:

- **14:00 is 84 blocks — 7 short**, and there is no clean seven. The only seven that can
  go without breaking the survey or losing a refusal is **the entire comparative
  apparatus**: Wang Mang (57–58), Alexandria (59), Quetelet (84), the reference man
  (85), and two blocks of unpacking around them. **Cost:** every comparison the episode
  makes with another tradition, ancient or modern — which is what makes it intellectual
  history rather than a tour of a metaphor. It is available and specified block by block
  in *Runtime levers*, and it is the version to make if the runtime is forced down.
- **17:00 is 102 blocks — 11 too many, and only four of the eleven are good.** Those four
  are named in *Runtime levers* as the 15:50 stretch: the Lingshu 71 microcosm catalogue
  as a comparison, a fuller Alexandria beat, the *jing* etymology given its own unpacking,
  and Lingshu 31's measurement log as the counter-example. **The remaining seven are
  not.** What is left is either the twelve rivers given a block each — which pads the one
  act that must not feel like a list — or §4, which is excluded on compliance grounds and
  is not available at any runtime. **The honest answer is that 17:00 is reachable and
  that seven of the eleven blocks that get it there are padding**, which is what the
  brief asked to avoid.
- **11:30 (69 blocks) is the format floor, not a target.** It cannot be reached without
  dropping either the survey or the turn, and an episode called *The Rivers Inside* that
  drops the rivers is not a shorter version of this episode.

**15:10 is the recommendation.** It sits inside the requested window rather than at
either edge, and the thing that sets the length is not the source's word count but **a
nineteen-block survey that cannot be compressed without destroying its own point.** The
survey is long because its finding is that twelve pairings are indistinguishable from
one another, and you cannot demonstrate that in four blocks.

## What this episode argues

The obvious reading of this chapter is that the ancient Chinese thought the body was
like a country — twelve channels, twelve rivers, a pretty correspondence. That reading
is true and it is the least interesting thing in the text.

**The spine is that this chapter contains an empirical programme and a pure analogy in
the same speech, and never notices that they are pulling against each other.** Four
things happen in it, in this order:

1. **It rules two things out of measurement and one thing in.** Heaven cannot be
   measured. Earth cannot be quantified. **A man of eight chi can be** — because he is
   in the room, because a rule can be laid on him, and because when he is dead he can be
   opened and looked at. That is a statement about which class of objects is checkable,
   and it is the first move of any empirical programme.
2. **It then declares the interior countable.** The hardness of the depots, the size of
   the palaces, the length of the vessels, the clarity of the blood — 皆有大數, *all of
   these have a great number*. Everything after the dissection sentence is offered as
   quantity.
3. **And the quantity, when it arrives, is a list of rivers.** Twelve names off a map of
   the Han empire, in the order a physician learns the channels rather than the order
   water runs. **No property of any river is matched to any property of any organ
   anywhere in the chapter.** The warrant is empirical; what it warrants is a mnemonic.
4. **Then it ends by admitting the standard does not fit.** Take the middle measure, it
   says — a man not wasted, whose blood and qi have not declined. And then: but the man
   whose flesh is gone, how will you measure him? The chapter builds a measurement
   programme on an average body and closes by saying the average body is not the patient.

**The thesis, stated once and hedged hard:** the channels are not rivers, no structure
answering to the twelve has been identified by anatomy or physiology, and the
correspondence is a memory aid rather than a discovery. The dissection sentence did not
lead anywhere either — the tradition that follows this chapter did not become a
dissecting tradition. **What the chapter has instead is the right sorting question,
asked before anybody had a way to choose between two kinds of knowing.** The episode
says that out loud at blocks 87 and 88 and again at 90, and **the reckoning is
deliberately not saved for the end**: the survey carries its own at blocks 38 and 47,
and the analogy is scored at 77.

**The dramatic engine is a survey that fails on its own terms.** Fan-di commissions a
measurement and is handed a map; he keeps asking for a number and keeps being given a
place name. **Dr-Qi plays Qibo and speaks the chapter's answers directly** — unlike the
Suwen 77 cut, where Qibo is absent and she had to be written in, here she is the
chapter's actual second voice and every line of hers is the source or a plain rendering
of it. **Lei-Gong is the auditor**, and his eight blocks are the episode's spine of
findings: that the map does no work (37), that permission is not a report (53), that
geography was delivered twice where arithmetic was promised (61), that the chapter
scoped itself to one corner (69), that a correspondence whose outer term can vanish was
never load-bearing (76), and that the standard problem is stated and unsolved (86).

**The fan is the tell.** Fan-di has it open and moving from block 3 through the whole
survey and the whole turn — the cast sheet's *performing* setting — and it goes down at
**block 89**, when he stops demanding a table and says what he is actually leaving with.

**Every one of the 91 blocks below is inside its voice's range**, verified by parsing
this document's own narration table and dropping tokens containing no letter or digit —
because a spaced em-dash is not a word and counting it as one spends real budget on a
window 1.4s wide. **Block 91 is the deliberate exception** and is sized to measured
precedent rather than to Arthur's range — see the note under Act VII.

Four consequences shape every line here, and none is stylistic:

- **The short interjection is dead.** Under an 8.6s floor there is no one-line objection
  from the bench, and `--clip-seconds` moves the window rather than widening it. **Every
  character line is a full paragraph.** On a survey that is a real cost: Fan-di cannot
  say *"and?"*, so each of his interruptions has to be forty words of impatience. It is
  also why Lei-Gong has eight blocks rather than twenty — at 47–52 words each, an auditor
  who reacts to every pairing becomes a second narrator.
- **No comma list of three or more items appears anywhere in this script, and there is
  not a single comma in any of the 91 narration cells.** The 2026-08-09 measurement put a
  31-word Arthur line with seven commas at **12.022s** against **8.913s** for the same 31
  words with none — a 3.1s swing from punctuation alone, on a window 1.4s wide. **This
  chapter is a list of twelve items**, so it is close to the worst case for that failure.
  Every enumeration below is joined with *and* or split into sentences. **Do not add one.**
- **The rates are survivor-biased.** ~4.4 w/s is what Arthur's *passing* takes delivered,
  and his rate moved between **3.98 and 4.80 w/s within a single seven-block cut** on
  lines of near-identical length. A word count predicts a take only to about ±1.5s.
  **Generate two variants per block and keep the better one** — at ~1.45 a take that is
  the cheapest reliable method.
- **Reach the window with words and not with full stops.** The assembler warns on
  internal pauses ≥0.8s, so padding a thin line with sentence breaks passes the duration
  gate and trips the pause warning. **No block below exceeds four sentences.**

These are **arithmetic against measured rates, not measurements**. Treat the first pass
as calibration and budget re-takes explicitly — the preflight below does.

**Proper nouns are the take-length risk specific to this cut.** Twelve river names, four
channel names per pairing, *Herophilus*, *Erasistratus*, *Quetelet*, *Wang Sun Qing*,
*Wangwu*, *Jinan*. `seed_audio` slows on an unfamiliar name and the slowdown is not
predictable from the word count. **Blocks 31 to 46, 57, 59, 74 and 84 are the ones to
generate two variants of first**, and the repair for each is a named deletion in the
act prose rather than a re-punctuation.

### How to read this script

`CLAUDE.md` asks for **SOUND / VISUAL / CHARACTER** blocks and a numbered shot list;
`build_subtitles.js` needs a **three-column narration table** to build the required
`.srt`/`.vtt` sidecars. They are combined rather than duplicated:

- **The narration tables are canonical for words.** Blocks run continuously **1–91
  across the whole episode**, never per-act; the assembly manifest is flat and act-local
  numbering produces off-by-one errors that are expensive to find in a fifteen-minute
  render.
- **The SOUND / VISUAL / direction prose between them is canonical for performance**,
  and the **numbered shot list after block 91 is canonical for picture.** Neither
  restates a narration line.
- **The speaker is named in the beat column**, not a fourth column — the parser reads
  exactly three, and a fourth would leak into the caption text.
- **No markdown inside a narration cell.** `build_subtitles.js` copies the third column
  into the cue verbatim and strips nothing, so a `**bold**` emphasis burns into the
  picture as literal asterisks. **Put emphasis in the direction prose, never in the line.**

> **Do not insert a table whose first cell is a bare integer anywhere between blocks 1
> and 91.** `parseNarration` keeps the first consecutive run from block 1 and stops at the
> first row that breaks it, so a stray numeric table silently truncates the sidecar. Every
> other table in this document sits before block 1 or after block 91 for that reason, and
> the ON-SCREEN TEXT card table prefixes its first column with `Block` so it cannot
> collide. The shot list after block 91 is a numbered *list*, not a table, for the same
> reason.

## The survey table — the visual system

The slate assigns this chapter the **expedition** form, and §2.2 makes clear why that
matters beyond taste: twenty episodes in the same Q&A shape is the Generic/Repetitive
Content failure mode, so **every episode carries a distinct structural form.** Here the
form is staged as a survey, and the argument is staged as a map that fills in and then
loses a line.

**The whole episode plays over one object — a long silk map unrolled across a low table,
blank at the start, with a bronze measuring rule lying along its edge.** The camera
treats the silk as terrain and drifts along it downstream. Nothing else is ever the
subject of a shot.

| Act | The map | The bronze rule | Light |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | rolled and tied, then opening | lying beside it, untouched | first light, flat and grey |
| Act I | unrolled and completely blank | untouched | first light |
| Act II | still blank | **laid flat for the first time and it reaches nothing** | morning |
| Act III | **twelve river lines brushing on one at a time** | held against each line and measuring nothing | high sun |
| Act IV | set aside at the edge of frame | **laid across a sheet of blank silk with a lamp brought close.** This is the whole depiction of dissection | shutters closed and a single lamp |
| Act V | back on the table with grey bearings washed across it | laid along a bearing | flat afternoon |
| Act VI | **one line paling out of the silk** | not touched at all | long low light |
| Act VII | rolled closed on the last shot | **left lying alone on the bare table** | daylight gone and lamps lit |

Three rules keep it from becoming decoration, and the first is the slate's compliance
instruction taken literally:

- **No body is ever drawn, in any of the ninety-one shots.** Not living and not dead, not
  whole and not partial, not covered and not uncovered. There is no table other than the
  survey table, nothing lies on it but silk and instruments, and **no figure is ever prone
  anywhere in the episode.** The dissection turn is carried by a bronze rule on blank
  silk, a lamp, and a shut door with light under it. This is `CLAUDE.md`'s *portraits not
  bodies* and the safety filter's restraint problem answered by the same decision — a
  covered figure on a table is exactly the composition that has returned `nsfw` from this
  service on innocuous subject matter.
- **Every organ is a mark on a map and never an organ.** The twelve appear as brushed
  blue-grey channels and as small ink glyphs at their inland ends — a sealed jar, a gate,
  a fan of channels, a granary door. **There is no anatomical imagery of any kind in
  ninety-one shots**, which is the slate's *keep all imagery diagrammatic and ink-wash*
  with nothing left to interpret.
- **The map is always a map.** When the correspondence has to be shown, the same silk is
  turned ninety degrees under the camera so the lines read down the frame, and then turned
  back. **It is never redrawn as a torso and it never dissolves into one.** Shot 47 is the
  only place this happens and it happens once.

**One more rule, and it is the episode's closing image.** The map fills in across Act III
until all twelve lines are on it. From shot 75 **one of those twelve pales out of the
silk while every line around it stays exactly as it was**, and shot 77 holds the finished
map with a clean channel of untouched silk where the twelfth was. Nobody redraws it.

## Source text and translation

The chapter has five sections. **Four are used and one is excluded** — see the note under
§4, which is the slate's named compliance instruction for this chapter.

The Chinese below is given in **traditional** forms, converted by hand from the supplied
simplified source, because the on-screen cards use traditional. The English is this cut's
own working translation from the classical text.

### §1 — the question

> 黃帝問於岐伯曰：經脈十二者，外合於十二經水，而內屬於五藏六府。夫十二經水者，其有大小、深淺、廣狹、遠近各不同；五藏六府之高下、大小、受穀之多少亦不等，相應奈何？夫經水者，受水而行之；五藏者，合神氣魂魄而藏之；六府者，受穀而行之，受氣而揚之；經脈者，受血而營之。合而以治，奈何？刺之深淺，灸之壯數，可得聞乎？

*The Yellow Emperor asked Qibo: the twelve channels answer outwardly to the twelve great
rivers and belong inwardly to the five depots and six palaces. The twelve rivers differ
in size and in depth and in breadth and in distance. The depots and palaces differ in
height and in size and in how much grain they take. How do they correspond? The rivers
receive water and carry it. The depots gather spirit and qi and the hun and po souls and
store them. The palaces receive grain and move it and receive qi and raise it. The
channels receive blood and nourish with it. How are they combined in treatment? May I
hear about the depth of needling and the number of moxa cones?*

### §2 — the refusal and the warrant

> 岐伯答曰：善哉問也！天至高不可度，地至廣不可量，此之謂也。且夫人生於天地之間，六合之內，此天之高，地之廣也，非人力之所能度量而至也。若夫八尺之士，皮肉在此，外可度量切循而得之，其死可解剖而視之。其藏之堅脆，府之大小，穀之多少，脈之長短，血之清濁，氣之多少，十二經之多血少氣，與其少血多氣，與其皆多血氣，與其皆少血氣，皆有大數。其治以針艾，各調其經氣，固其常有合乎。

*Qibo answered: what a good question. Heaven is so high it cannot be measured and earth
so broad it cannot be quantified — that is what is meant. A person is born between heaven
and earth within the six directions, and that height and that breadth are not reached by
human effort. But as for a man of eight chi, his skin and flesh are here; on the outside
he can be measured and traced and palpated and known, and when he is dead he can be cut
open and looked at. The firmness or fragility of the depots, the size of the palaces, the
amount of grain, the length of the vessels, the clarity or turbidity of the blood, the
quantity of qi, and which of the twelve channels have much blood and little qi or little
blood and much qi or much of both or little of both — all of these have a great number.*

**其死可解剖而視之 is the episode.** It is the earliest explicit statement in this corpus
that the interior of a person is an object of inspection, and 解剖 is still the ordinary
modern Chinese word for anatomy and dissection.

### §3 — the twelve, and the bearings

> 黃帝曰：余聞之，快於耳不解於心，願卒聞之。岐伯答曰：此人之所以參天地而應陰陽也，不可不察。足太陽外合清水，內屬膀胱，而通水道焉。足少陽外合於渭水，內屬於膽。足陽明外合於海水，內屬於胃。足太陰外合於湖水，內屬於脾。足少陰外合於汝水，內屬於腎。足厥陰外合於澠水，內屬於肝。手太陽外合淮水，內屬小腸，而水道出焉。手少陽外合於漯水，內屬於三焦。手陽明外合於江水，內屬於大腸。手太陰外合於河水，內屬於肺。手少陰外合於濟水，內屬於心。手心主外合於漳水，內屬於心包。凡此五藏六府十二經水者，外有源泉，而內有所稟，此皆內外相貫，如環無端，人經亦然。故天為陽，地為陰，腰以上為天，腰以下為地。故海以北者為陰，湖以北者為陰中之陰；漳以南者為陽，河以北至漳者為陽中之陰；漯以南至江者，為陽中之太陽，此一隅之陰陽也，所以人與天地相參也。

*The Emperor said: I have heard it and it pleases my ear but my heart does not understand
it. I wish to hear the whole of it. Qibo answered: this is how a person is set beside
heaven and earth and answers to yin and yang, and it cannot be left unexamined.*

| Channel | River | Organ |
|---|---|---|
| Foot taiyang 足太陽 | 清水 the Qing — *and the water passages run through it* | bladder 膀胱 |
| Foot shaoyang 足少陽 | 渭水 the Wei | gallbladder 膽 |
| Foot yangming 足陽明 | 海水 the Hai — **the sea, not a river** | stomach 胃 |
| Foot taiyin 足太陰 | 湖水 the Hu | spleen 脾 |
| Foot shaoyin 足少陰 | 汝水 the Ru | kidney 腎 |
| Foot jueyin 足厥陰 | 澠水 the Sheng | liver 肝 |
| Hand taiyang 手太陽 | 淮水 the Huai — *and the water passages issue from it* | small intestine 小腸 |
| Hand shaoyang 手少陽 | 漯水 the Ta | sanjiao 三焦 |
| Hand yangming 手陽明 | 江水 the Jiang — **the Yangtze** | large intestine 大腸 |
| Hand taiyin 手太陰 | 河水 the He — **the Yellow River** | lung 肺 |
| Hand shaoyin 手少陰 | 濟水 the Ji — **the river that no longer exists** | heart 心 |
| Hand jueyin 手心主 | 漳水 the Zhang | pericardium 心包 |

*All of these five depots and six palaces and twelve rivers have springs outside and
receiving places inside. Outside and inside are threaded through one another like a ring
with no end, and a person's channels are the same. So heaven is yang and earth is yin;
above the waist is heaven and below the waist is earth. North of the Hai is yin, and north
of the Hu is yin within yin. South of the Zhang is yang. From the He north as far as the
Zhang is yin within yang, and from the Ta south to the Jiang is the greater yang within
yang. This is the yin and yang of one corner, and it is how a person is set beside heaven
and earth.*

### §4 — **excluded**

**§4 is not used in this episode and its content is deliberately not transcribed here.**
It is a table of needling depths in *fen* and retention times in breaths for each of the
six foot channels, a shorter rule for the hand channels, and a warning about what excess
moxibustion does. The slate's instruction for this chapter is explicit: *"The
needling-depth material at the end of the chapter should be cut entirely — it adds nothing
and it is the only part that could classify as instruction."*

**The numbers are withheld from this document as well as from the episode, and that is a
deliberate choice rather than an oversight.** A production document is where card text and
narration get lifted from, and a table of depths sitting in a section headed *Source text*
is the single most likely route by which one ends up on screen. Anybody who needs the
passage has the chapter.

**One clause of §4 is quoted, once, at block 34**, because it belongs to the river system
rather than to the technique: 足陽明，五藏六府之海也 — *the foot yangming is the sea of the
five depots and six palaces.* It is the reason the sea in the list at §3 is probably not a
scribal error, and it carries no instruction of any kind.

### §5 — the standard body

> 黃帝曰：夫經脈之大小，血之多少，膚之厚薄，肉之堅脆及膕之大小，可為量度乎？岐伯答曰：其可為度量者，取其中度也。不甚脫肉，而血氣不衰也。若夫度之人，消瘦而形肉脫者，惡可以度量刺乎。審、切、循、捫、按，視其寒溫盛衰而調之，是謂因適而為之真也。

*The Emperor asked: can the size of the channels and the amount of blood and the thickness
of the skin and the firmness of the flesh and the size of the hollow behind the knee be
measured? Qibo answered: those that can be measured are measured by taking the middle
standard — one whose flesh is not badly wasted and whose blood and qi have not declined.
But for a man who is emaciated and whose flesh is gone, how could he be measured? Examine
and palpate and trace and feel and press; observe cold or warmth and excess or deficiency
and regulate accordingly. This is called adapting to what is fitting and thereby making it
true.*

**The last sentence of §5 is a palpation procedure and is not narrated anywhere in this
episode.** Blocks 81 and 82 say that the chapter's answer at that point is a procedure and
that the episode stops at the door of it. The classical text is reproduced here because §5
is otherwise the source of the episode's second thesis; **nothing from that sentence
reaches a narration cell or a card.**

### Departures, exclusions and things to verify

Everything below needs checking against a standard edition before any card is burned, and
the first four are already known to differ from the supplied source file.

1. **The supplied source is simplified and every card in this cut is traditional.** The
   chapter arrived as `07_Ch_LS12__The_Rivers_Inside.md` with the Chinese in simplified
   form (经水 / 其死可解剖而视之 / 此一隅之阴阳也). **All eight card texts were converted by
   hand and are not from the source file.** ffmpeg renders a wrong glyph silently.
2. **The supplied source writes 歧伯 where standard editions write 岐伯.** This document
   uses 岐伯. It reaches no card and no narration line.
3. **The supplied English translation renders 河水 as "Heishui River". That is an error.**
   河水 is the He — the Yellow River — not 黑水. The reading is load-bearing here: it is
   what makes block 43 and block 46 work, and it is what makes the 1855 avulsion in Act VI
   the same river that took the Ji. **Do not restore the supplied reading.**
4. **Two more river names in the supplied English are read differently here.** 漯水 is
   given there as *Luoshui*; the ancient Shandong river is read **Ta**. 澠水 is given there
   as *Mianshui*; the character carries both *miǎn* and *shéng*, and as the Qi-state river
   paired with the Zi it is read **Sheng**. The episode says *the Ta* and *the Sheng*, and
   both cards carry the character rather than a romanisation. **Flag either to the user
   before recording block 36 or block 40** — this is the one place where a defensible
   alternative reading exists and the take costs money.
5. **海水 is a genuine textual crux and the episode treats it as one rather than resolving
   it.** Blocks 34 and 35 state that it is the sea, that some editions read a river name
   there, and that the sea reading is the interesting one. **No claim is made about which
   is original.**
6. **陽中之太陽 is rendered *yang inside yang* at block 65 and *greater yang inside yang* at
   block 66.** The first is a word-budget compression on Vesper's two-word window and the
   second restores it on Arthur's wider one. Deliberate; do not fix by lengthening 65.
7. **手心主 is the source's term for the hand jueyin channel.** Block 45 uses *hand jueyin*
   for consistency with the other eleven pairings and glosses the organ as *the heart
   governor*, which is what 心主 and 心包 name. The source table above keeps 手心主.
8. **Eight chi at about one metre eighty five** assumes a Han chi of roughly 23.1 cm.
   **Verify before block 26 is recorded.** The internal comparison in the same block —
   that Lingshu 14 《骨度》 measures its standard man at 七尺五寸 rather than 八尺 — is the
   load-bearing half of that block and is what makes the eight-chi man a standard figure.
9. **Wang Sun Qing.** The account is in the 《漢書·王莽傳》 and is usually dated to 16 CE:
   a captured associate of Zhai Yi was cut open by imperial order, a court physician and a
   skilled butcher measured the five depots, and bamboo rods were threaded along the
   vessels to trace where each began and ended, *云可以治病* — said to be usable for
   treating illness. **Verify the date and the wording before blocks 57 and 58 are
   recorded.** The episode does not claim this dissection produced any of Lingshu 12's
   content, and block 54 says explicitly that it did not.
10. **Alexandria.** Herophilus and Erasistratus, working in the early third century BCE.
    Block 59's claim is that no other period in antiquity practised human dissection
    routinely, which is the standard reading and is a claim about practice rather than
    about knowledge.
11. **The Ji and the 1855 avulsion.** The Ji 濟水 was one of the 四瀆 — the Four Waterways
    of the state sacrifices, with the Jiang, the He and the Huai — and was described in
    classical geography as crossing the Yellow River and continuing east. Its lower course
    was progressively taken by the Yellow River, and the **1855 avulsion at Tongwaxiang**,
    which swung the Yellow River from its southern course to the north, is the point at
    which the Ji ceased to exist as an independent river. **Verify the date before block 72
    is recorded** — the flooding runs from 1851 and the avulsion itself is 1855, and block
    72 names the later date.
12. **Jinan 濟南** means *south of the Ji*. Block 74 says *several million people* rather
    than a figure, deliberately, because the municipal number moves and a wrong one on a
    dated video is a correction in the comments.
13. **Quetelet, 1835**, *Sur l'homme et le développement de ses facultés*, and *l'homme
    moyen*. Block 84's *eighteen centuries after this chapter* is arithmetic on a rounded
    Han date and is consistent with the caveat at block 19.
14. **The reference man.** Block 85 names radiation science and pharmacology and a figure
    of about seventy kilograms. It makes **no claim about any dose, any drug or any
    procedure** and is a statement about the history of standards.
15. **The modern reckoning at block 87** is that no structure answering to the twelve
    channels has been identified by anatomy or physiology. That is a statement about the
    state of the evidence and is phrased as one.
