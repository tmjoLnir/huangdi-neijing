> A dramatized adaptation of a classical philosophical text. Not medical advice.

**Cite the chapter on screen as *Lingshu 12*, and here the collision is scheduled
rather than theoretical.** Both halves of the canon run to eighty-one separately
numbered chapters, and **Suwen 12 《異法方宜論》 is on the same Top-20 slate at rank 9

a month, not merely with the canon. `CLAUDE.md` names this exact pair as the live case
that forced the two-book folder structure, and citation accuracy is the channel's
stated differentiator.

Companion longform: `inner-canon-lingshu12-longform-v1.md` (91 blocks, 15:10), which
holds the classical text, this cut's working translation, all twelve pairings, the
fifteen-item verification list and the compliance audit this trailer inherits.

**Slate position.** Lingshu 12 is **rank 3 of the Top-20 slate and publish slot 7** —
`docs/Chronicle of Balance Top20 Chapter Slate.md` §3.3 and §5. Its stated job at slot 7:
*"First anatomy episode. The dissection reveal is the strongest single mid-video hook
available and is placed where retention curves typically first sag."* §3.3 calls the
dissection line *"the earliest explicit statement in the Chinese medical corpus that the
interior of the body is an object of empirical inspection — and it sits inside a chapter
that is otherwise pure analogy."* The assigned structural form is **expedition**: *"a
survey journey downstream, one river at a time, with the dissection line placed as the
turn at the midpoint."* Seventy seconds cannot sail twelve rivers, so **the trailer states
the pairing, spends its centre on the dissection sentence, and shows the survey as a map
that is still filling in.**

**The slate also writes this chapter's one hard exclusion.** §3.3: *"Dissection is
discussed, not depicted. Keep all imagery diagrammatic and ink-wash; no photographic or
realistic anatomical rendering. The needling-depth material at the end of the chapter
should be cut entirely."* Both halves bind this cut absolutely: **no body appears in any
of the seven shots**, and **no needling depth, moxa count or technique of any kind is in
any line or any card.** The trailer's central block says the word *dissection* over a
bronze rule on blank silk, which is the whole of the depiction.

**This is a fresh treatment.** No prior version of this chapter exists in `output/`;
nothing has been carried over from any earlier script, per the brief.

## Why 70 seconds

`CLAUDE.md` allows 30–90 sec and the pipeline assembles whole 10s blocks, so the real
choice is six, seven, eight or nine blocks. **Seven is the smallest count that keeps the
hook and its payoff in the same cut:**

- The end disclaimer card **costs a whole block** (`CLAUDE.md`), so a 60s trailer has five
  content blocks and a 70s trailer has six.
- The cut has to carry **five things**: the pairing, the question it was supposed to
  answer, the refusal that sets up the turn, **the dissection sentence**, and the audit
  that stops it being a mystery tease. Six content blocks is one each plus the closing
  claim about why the chapter is worth an episode.
- **At five content blocks the block that goes is block 5**, Lei-Gong's audit — and a
  trailer that says *the ancients knew the body could be opened* and then stops is exactly
  the credulous cut this channel's reckoning discipline exists to prevent. **The audit is
  not a lever.** If the runtime has to come down, the cut goes to 60s by dropping block 2;
  the reasoning is in *Runtime levers*.
- **80s and 90s are available and are specified in *Runtime levers*,** but the seventh and
  eighth content blocks both buy *more of the survey* — and the survey is nineteen blocks
  of the episode. A trailer that starts naming rivers has begun delivering the episode
  rather than selling it.

**The vanished river is deliberately withheld from the body of this cut and named only in
the block-7 tail.** It is the episode's most retainable single fact and its best late
turn, and it is the one thing a trailer can promise without spending. See *Source
mapping*.

## Shot list

Numbered to the blocks. **Vertical 9:16, flat 2D ink-wash, text-free except blocks 3 and
7.** Every shot needs motion from frame 1 — the assembler warns on a static open or a
frozen tail.

1. A long silk map unrolled across a low table at first light with **a bronze measuring rule lying along its edge.** Blue-grey river lines brushed on it and **one channel of blank silk running through them where a line should be.** Slow drift downstream along the map.
2. FAN-DI behind the table, gold robe with the contemporary tailored cut, thin round glasses on his nose, **his white folding fan snapped open and moving.** A survey ledger open in front of him with its columns ruled and empty. He is looking down at the map rather than at the camera.
3. DR-QI at the side of the table, blue cheongsam with cloud embroidery, open bamboo scroll held casually like a tablet. Behind her **a sounding line dropped into dark water and going taut, then lifted into open sky and hanging slack.** **CARD** set to one side: 天至高不可度，地至廣不可量. *(Default: the plate renders; the characters are added at edit — see Finishing steps.)*
4. **The turn.** A bronze measuring rule laid down across a sheet of blank silk with a lamp brought close to it, the graduations catching the light along their whole length. Beyond it **a shut door with lamplight under it and nobody in frame.** Nothing under the silk. No table but this one.
5. LEI-GONG cross-legged at the end of the table, cinnabar robe with lightning trim, small hand drum hanging at his hip, wax tablet on his knee with twelve pairs copied in two columns. Spark motes drifting off the cinnabar sleeve. He rules a hard line under them and goes on writing.
6. The map again with all twelve river lines now brushed on and **one of them paling out of the silk as we watch**, every line around it staying exactly as it was. The light has moved along the table.
7. **END CARD** — plain plate. The rolled silk map and the bronze rule alone on the bare table with the lamps small behind. Disclaimer and editorial credit added at edit time.

**Blocks 4, 6 and 7 are the stillness risks.** A laid-out object, a line disappearing, and
a plate. Give each a slow push, ink still settling, or a light change so the clip does not
read as a held frame. **Block 6 is the one to watch** — its whole subject is something
that stops being there, and an absence is exactly what the assembler's freeze probe flags.
Carry it on the lamp travelling across the emptying channel and on a slow pull back, never
on the blank silk alone.

**No body is drawn in any of the seven shots.** Not living and not dead, not whole and not
partial, not covered and not uncovered. There is no table but the survey table and nothing
lies on it but silk and instruments. **Every organ in the cut is a mark on a map**, which
is the slate's *diagrammatic and ink-wash* with nothing left to interpret — see
*Compliance notes*.

**The twelve are structural rather than careless.** The vertical two-line budget is

into five or more clauses — and `SKILL.md` caps a block at four sentences because the
assembler *warns* on internal pauses of 0.8s or more. **The two rules cannot both be
satisfied at 9:16.**

which the sidecar takes to five lines.** Block 4's second sentence is the same length at

characters and does the same. That reproduces the finding recorded on the Lingshu 43 and
Suwen 77 trailers: **splitting a wide clause reduces its width and increases the number of
cues**, because each fragment still overruns a two-line budget of about 44 characters and
then rounds up to a cue of its own.

the block is at 48 against a floor of 47.

the cue breaks without re-recording, and it poisons reproduction: the next person to
regenerate the take from this document would get the comma version, and a comma list is the
single most expensive structure to hand these voices. **The fix, if one is worth making, is

## Runtime levers

7 blocks = 70.0s. `CLAUDE.md` allows 30–90 sec, and every lever below moves whole 10s blocks
because the assembler has no other unit.

**Cut to 60s (6 blocks) — drop 1.** Drop **block 2**, Fan-di's commission. The hook, the
refusal, the turn, the audit and the closing claim all survive, and block 1's *a physician
matched them one to one* already establishes that somebody asked. **Cost:** the cut loses
the reason the chapter was written — that the Emperor wanted a conversion table and never got
one — and block 5's *he promised measurements* then refers to a promise the viewer never
heard. This is the cut to make only if the budget forces one, and it is still the right one
to make before touching any other block.

**Cut to 50s (5 blocks) — drop 2.** Drop blocks 2 and 6. **Not recommended.** Block 6 is the
only place the cut says what the chapter did that makes it worth fifteen minutes, and without
it the trailer is a debunk with a good image attached.

**Do not reach 60s by dropping block 5.** It is the cut's only reckoning, and a trailer that
says *they knew the body could be opened* and then stops is the credulous version of this
episode — the one the slate's *"take a claim seriously, describe it fully, then examine it
honestly"* discipline exists to prevent. **Do not reach 60s by dropping block 4 either**; it
is the chapter's reason for being on the slate at all.

**Stretch to 80s (8 blocks) — add 1.** Add a **Dr-Qi block after 3** carrying the first
pairing in the chapter's own form — that the foot taiyang answers the Qing outside and belongs
to the bladder inside, and that all twelve are that shape and nothing more. **This is the
strongest single addition available**, because it lets block 5's audit land on something the
viewer has actually heard rather than on a description of a list. It costs the cut its balance
by giving Dr-Qi two consecutive blocks of recitation, which is why 70s remains the
recommendation.

**Stretch to 90s (9 blocks) — add 2.** Add the above, plus an **Arthur block after 6** naming
what the episode delivers — the survey, the chapter scoping itself to one corner of one
country, and the river that is under another river now. **At 90s the trailer starts teaching
the episode rather than selling it**, and naming the vanished river spends the block-1 promise
that is this cut's single best asset. That is why 70s is the recommendation.

**Blocks that must not be split or dropped in any recut:**

- **Block 7** — the disclaimer card is mandatory and is not a lever.
- **Block 4** — the dissection sentence. It is why this chapter is ranked third on the slate
  and it is the whole reason for the cut.
- **Block 5** — the reckoning. Without it the cut is a mystery tease, which the slate's
  compliance model and this channel's stated differentiator both rule out.
- **Blocks 3 and 4 as a pair and in that order** — they are two halves of one source sentence,
  and the second is meaningless without the first.
