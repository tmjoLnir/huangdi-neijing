## Why 16:00, and not 14:00 or 17:00

The requested window was 14–17 minutes, with a hard floor of 11:30, and the brief
asked for the length that minimises padding and shortening. **96 blocks — 16:00 —
is where this chapter's material actually lands**, and the reasoning is arithmetic
rather than taste, because a 10s block is the unit that gets padded or cut.

The chapter is unusually well-shaped for this: it is a **question**, a **lexicon**,
a **regimen**, a **damage catalogue** and a **limit**, in that order, and none of
the five can be dropped without the next one arriving unmotivated. Costing the
material by how much each part can carry without inventing filler:

- **14:00 is 84 blocks — 12 short.** There are no 12 blocks of slack anywhere

  reason this chapter ranks fourth and prices highest is that *"it sets up a genuine
  comparative argument — against Plato's tripartite soul, against the Aristotelian
  faculties, against modern cognitive taxonomies — that gives the episode
  intellectual weight without requiring any medical claim."* **Cutting it is cutting
  the reason the episode is on the slate**, and it is also cutting the only act that
  requires no medical claim at all. A 14:00 cut is an amputation, not a tightening.
- **15:00 is 90 blocks — 6 short.** This one is real and is specified exactly in
  *Runtime levers*. It costs the depth of the comparative act and two lexicon
  unpackings, and it keeps every act, every card and every hedge. **If the runtime
  is fixed externally at 15:00, take that lever and nothing else.**
- **17:00 is 102 blocks — 6 too many, and the six are the wrong six.** The material
  that would fill them is the 養生 regimen expanded and a longer treatment of the
  emotional-damage passages. **Both are the chapter's compliance-sensitive
  material**, and the slate's care point for this chapter is precisely that the
  emotional sections must read as historical psychology and that *"the episode
  should not offer any coping advice of any kind."* Stretching into them is
  stretching into the only part of the chapter that has to be handled short and

- **11:30 (69 blocks) is far below what this chapter supports** and would lose the

**16:00 is the recommendation.** It is inside the requested window rather than at
either edge, it is where the material genuinely stops, and — unusually — the thing
that sets the length is not the source's length but its *structure*: a chain of
eleven definitions cannot be abridged in the middle, because every link after the
cut stops being derivable.

## What this episode argues

The obvious reading of this chapter is that it is a piece of pre-scientific
psychology: some old words for the soul, arranged by a culture that had not found
the brain yet. That reading produces sixteen minutes of gentle condescension, and
the chapter does not support it.

**The spine is that this is a constructed system, and the construction is the
achievement rather than the content.** Four things happen in a very short chapter:

1. **A technical manual opens by refusing its own technique.** 凡刺之法，先必本於神
   — *in all methods of needling, one must first take the spirit as the root.* The
   Lingshu is the half of the canon that teaches you where to put the needle. Its
   eighth chapter opens by saying the needle is the second thing, and then spends
   its entire length on the first thing.
2. **What follows is an axiomatic construction, not a list.** Eleven terms are
   defined in sequence, and **each definition uses only terms already defined.**
   生之來謂之精 — essence is what brings forth life. 兩精相搏謂之神 — spirit is what
   happens when two essences strike together. 隨神往來者謂之魂 — the *hun* is what
   comes and goes with the spirit. You cannot pull a term out of the middle; the
   ones after it stop being derivable. **That is a formal property, and it is
   extremely rare in any ancient text about mind.**
3. **The map has no brain on it.** Not as an oversight — 腦 does not appear in this
   chapter at all. The mind is distributed across five organs below the neck, and
   the chapter is entirely comfortable about that. **This is the episode's largest
   claim and its most careful one**, because it is true of this chapter and false of
   the canon as a whole.
4. **And then the chapter runs the whole thing backwards.** Every term it built, it
   now breaks: anxiety injures the spirit, grief injures the *hun*, joy injures the
   *po*. Then it inverts once more — 肝氣虛則恐，實則怒, *when liver qi is deficient
   there is fear, when it is excessive there is anger.* **Emotion damages the organ
   and the organ produces the emotion.** The loop runs both ways, and the chapter
   states both directions on the same page.

**The payoff is the last thing the chapter says about needles, and it is why this
episode exists.** 五者已傷，鍼不可以治之也 — *if all five are injured, the needle
cannot treat it.* A technical manual, in its own voice, naming the point at which
its technique stops working. Almost nothing in the ancient medical literature of
any culture does that.

**The thesis, stated once and hedged hard:** the specific map is wrong. The liver
does not store a soul, anger is not kept in the kidneys, and nobody dies in autumn
because of grief. **What is not wrong is the shape of the thing** — that mental
states and bodily states are one system, that the traffic runs in both directions,
and that a description of a person that leaves out either half is incomplete. That
is roughly where the study of interoception and stress physiology has arrived, by a
completely different route, and **an arrival at a similar shape is not a
vindication of the route.** The episode says that sentence out loud, at block 60
and again at block 80.

**The dramatic engine:** Fan-di has asked for definitions, which he expects to be a
small request, and receives a machine. **The fan is the tell** — snapped open he is
performing, set down he means it. It goes down once, at **block 81**, when the
chapter finishes running its own map backwards and he realises that the list he
demanded a definition of is also a list of the ways a person comes apart. It does
not come back up.

**Lei-Gong is the auditor**, and his brief this episode is *structure*. He is not
here to be charmed and he is not here to be sceptical about China. Each of his five
blocks is a finding: that the headline term was defined using only the term before
it and that this is unusual (30); that a chain can be tested by pulling a link out
(41); that a definition using only earlier terms is a construction rather than a
poem (53); that the five seasons are the system talking to itself rather than a
prophecy (77); and — the episode's best line, at **block 87** — that he has never
seen a manual tell you where its own tool stops.

> **⚠ Do not size this script off the two committed longform documents in this
> repo.** `inner-canon-suwen13-longform-v1.md` and `inner-canon-suwen8-longform-v1.md`
> are both written to **Arthur 31–34 and Vesper 37–41**, which were superseded on
> 2026-08-10. On the run that superseded them, a 32-word Arthur line — legal and
> mid-range under the old table — came back at **6.446s, more than two seconds under
> the assembler's floor**, which is a hard error that stops the whole build. Five of
> that cut's seven blocks failed their first pass and all five were Arthur's or
> Vesper's. **Every Arthur block below is 38–44 words and every Vesper block is
> 41–43**, and that is not a stylistic difference from the older documents. It is the
> difference between a cut that assembles and one that does not.

**Every one of the 96 blocks below is inside its voice's range**, verified by
parsing this document's own narration table and dropping tokens containing no
letter or digit — because a spaced em-dash is not a word and counting it as one
spends real budget on a window 1.4s wide.

Four consequences shape every line here, and none is stylistic:

- **The short interjection is dead.** Under an 8.6s floor there is no quick jab,
  and `--clip-seconds` moves the window rather than widening it. **Every character
  line is a full paragraph.** This is why Lei-Gong has five blocks rather than
  fifteen: at 47–52 words each, an auditor who comments on everything becomes the
  narrator.
- **No comma list of three or more items appears anywhere in this script**, and no
  parenthetical comma pair either. The 2026-08-09 measurement put a 31-word Arthur
  line with seven commas at **12.022s** against **8.913s** for the same 31 words
  with none — a 3.1s swing from punctuation alone, on a window 1.4s wide. **This
  chapter is a list of thirteen things and is therefore the worst possible case for
  that failure.** Every enumeration below is joined with *and* or split into
  sentences. **Do not add commas to these lines**; it is free in the document and
  catastrophic in the take.
- **The rates are survivor-biased.** ~4.4 w/s is what Arthur's *passing* takes
  delivered, and his rate moved between **3.98 and 4.80 w/s within a single
  seven-block cut** on lines of near-identical length. A word count predicts a take
  only to about ±1.5s. **Generate two variants per block and keep the better one** —
  at ~1.45 a take that is the cheapest reliable method, and it is what the last run
  actually did.
- **Reach the window with words, not full stops.** The assembler warns on internal
  pauses ≥0.8s, so padding a thin line with sentence breaks passes the duration gate
  and trips the pause warning. **No block below exceeds four sentences.**

These are **arithmetic against measured rates, not measurements**. Treat the first
pass as calibration and budget re-takes explicitly — the preflight below does.

### How to read this script

`CLAUDE.md` asks for **SOUND / VISUAL / CHARACTER** blocks and a numbered shot
list; `build_subtitles.js` needs a **three-column narration table** to build the
required `.srt`/`.vtt` sidecars. They are combined rather than duplicated:

- **The narration tables are canonical for words.** Blocks run continuously
  **1–96 across the whole episode**, never per-act; the assembly manifest is flat
  and act-local numbering produces off-by-one errors that are expensive to find in
  a sixteen-minute render.
- **The SOUND / VISUAL / direction prose between them is canonical for
  performance**, and the **numbered shot list after block 96 is canonical for
  picture.** Neither restates a narration line.
- **The speaker is named in the beat column**, not a fourth column — the parser
  reads exactly three, and a fourth would leak into the caption text.
- **No markdown inside a narration cell.** `build_subtitles.js` copies the third
  column into the cue verbatim and strips nothing, so a `**bold**` emphasis burns
  into the picture as literal asterisks. Six lines in this document were drafted
  with emphasis and had it removed for exactly that reason — blocks **34, 52, 57,
  68, 79 and 91**. **Put emphasis in the direction prose, never in the line**, and
  note that no other cut in this repo has ever carried markdown in a narration cell.

> **Do not insert a table whose first cell is a bare integer anywhere between
> blocks 1 and 96.** `parseNarration` keeps the first consecutive run from block 1
> and stops at the first row that breaks it, so a stray numeric table silently
> truncates the sidecar. Every other table in this document sits before block 1 or
> after block 96 for that reason, and the ON-SCREEN TEXT card table prefixes its
> first column with `Block` so it cannot collide. The shot list after block 96 is a
> numbered *list*, not a table, for the same reason.

## The chain — the visual system, and why it is structural

The slate assigns this chapter the **lexicon** form, and §2.2 of the slate makes
clear why that matters beyond taste: twenty episodes in the same Q&A shape is the
Generic/Repetitive Content failure mode, so **every episode carries a distinct
structural form**. Here it is taken literally.

**The whole episode plays against one tall hanging scroll, and the chain writes
itself down it in real time.**

| Act | The scroll | The blank field above it |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | blank silk, one hairline crease down the centre | unexplained |
| Act I | two marks only — 德 at the top left, 氣 at the top right, far apart | still blank |
| Act II | **the chain descends, one link per definition, each joined to the one above by a single hairline** | still blank |
| Act III | Plato's three boxes drawn *beside* the chain, not touching each other or it | **named for the first time — this is where a brain would go** |
| Act IV | the chain runs backwards; ink drains upward out of the links and five jars at the foot of the scroll crack | still blank |
| Act V | the chain complete, and the camera pulls back far enough to show it was always the outline of a standing figure | **still blank, and the episode ends on it** |

Three rules keep it from becoming decoration:

- **Nothing is ever drawn in the blank field above the top link.** The absence is
  the episode's largest claim, and drawing anything there — a brain, a skull, a
  glow, a question mark — converts an argument into clickbait. **The field stays
  empty in all ninety-six shots**, including the last one.
- **Ink only ever moves downward until Act IV**, where it moves upward for the
  first time. That reversal *is* the act's argument, and it should be the first
  thing the viewer notices about it without being told.
- **The register never changes.** Everything stays flat 2D ink-wash, and the modern
  reckoning is carried by Arthur's voice over the same brushed idiom. **There is no
  live action, no photograph, no scan, no diagram of a nervous system and no
  anatomical rendering in ninety-six shots.** See *Compliance notes* — for this
  chapter that is the single largest prompt-stage risk and it is designed out rather
  than managed.

## Source text and translation

> **⚠ The Chinese below is the received text of Lingshu 8, supplied from knowledge
> of the canon rather than extracted from the source file.** The chapter arrived as
> `03_Ch_LS08__The_Ancient_Map_of_the_Mind.pdf`, a ctext.org print in which the
> Chinese is set in an embedded subset font with a custom encoding: **the English
> translation extracted cleanly and the Chinese did not.** The English in the
> right-hand column of §1–§7 below is quoted from that extraction and is reliable.
> **Every Chinese character that will appear on a card must be verified against the
> source before burn** — sixteen cards depend on this section and nothing downstream
> re-checks it.

### §1 — The question

**黃帝問於岐伯曰：凡刺之法，先必本於神。血、脈、營、氣、精神，此五藏之所藏也。至其淫泆離藏則精失、魂魄飛揚、志意恍亂、智慮去身者，何因而然乎？天之罪與？人之過乎？何謂德、氣、生、精、神、魂、魄、心、意、志、思、智、慮？請問其故。**

*Supplied English:* "In all methods of acupuncture, one must first take as a basis
the spirit. Blood, vessels, nutritive essence, qi, and spirit — these are what the
five zang organs store. When these become excessive and depart from their respective
zang, essence is lost, the soul and spirit scatter, willpower becomes confused, and
wisdom departs from the body — what causes such conditions? Is it a punishment from
Heaven? Or is it due to human faults? What are meant by virtue, qi, life, essence,
spirit, soul, corporeal spirit, mind, intention, will, thought, wisdom, and
deliberation? Please explain the reasons."

**Blocks 3, 10, 14, 17.**

### §2 — The lexicon

**岐伯答曰：天之在我者德也，地之在我者氣也。德流氣薄而生者也。故生之來謂之精；兩精相搏謂之神；隨神往來者謂之魂；並精而出入者謂之魄；所以任物者謂之心；心有所憶謂之意；意之所存謂之志；因志而存變謂之思；因思而遠慕謂之慮；因慮而處物謂之智。**

*Supplied English:* "Heaven's aspect within me is virtue; Earth's aspect within me
is qi. Life arises from the flow of virtue and the subtlety of qi. Therefore, that
which brings forth life is called essence; When two essences interact, it is called
spirit; That which accompanies the spirit in its comings and goings is called the
soul; That which emerges and enters together with essence is called the corporeal
spirit; What enables one to deal with external things is called the mind; When the
mind recalls something, it is called intention; Where intention resides is called
will; Thought that arises from will and undergoes change is called thought
(cognition); Deliberation that extends from thought to distant aspirations is called
deliberation; Wisdom arises when one deliberates and deals with things
appropriately."

**Blocks 21, 24, 26, 28, 31, 32, 35, 37, 39, 42, 44, 46.**

### §3 — The regimen

**故智者之養生也，必順四時而適寒暑，和喜怒而安居處，節陰陽而調剛柔。如是則僻邪不至，長生久視。**

*Supplied English:* "Therefore, the wise person in cultivating life must follow the
four seasons and adapt to cold and heat, harmonize joy and anger while living in
peace and stability, regulate yin and yang, and balance rigidity and gentleness. If
one does so, then pernicious influences will not arise, and longevity with enduring
vision can be achieved."

**Blocks 61, 62.** Carried under the explicit refusal at block 63 — see *Compliance
notes*.

### §4 — The six states

**是故怵惕思慮者則傷神，神傷則恐懼流淫而不止。因悲哀動中者，竭絕而失生。喜樂者，神憚散而不藏。愁憂者，氣閉塞而不行。盛怒者，迷惑而不治。恐懼者，神蕩憚而不收。**

*Supplied English:* "Therefore, those who are constantly anxious and preoccupied
injure the spirit; when the spirit is injured, fear and anxiety flow unchecked.
Those whose inner being is deeply moved by sorrow and grief will exhaust their
essence and lose life. Excessive joy and pleasure cause the spirit to scatter and
not be stored. Those who are troubled by sorrow and worry experience qi that becomes
blocked and stagnant. Intense anger causes confusion and disorder. Those who are
fearful experience a spirit that is scattered and cannot be gathered."

**Blocks 66, 67, 70.**

### §5 — The five organs and the five seasons

**心怵惕思慮則傷神，神傷則恐懼自失，破䐃脫肉，毛悴色夭，死於冬。脾愁憂而不解則傷意，意傷則悗亂，四肢不舉，毛悴色夭，死於春。肝悲哀動中則傷魂，魂傷則狂忘不精，不精則不正當人，陰縮而攣筋，兩脅骨不舉，毛悴色夭，死於秋。肺喜樂無極則傷魄，魄傷則狂，狂者意不存人，皮革焦，毛悴色夭，死於夏。腎盛怒而不止則傷志，志傷則喜忘其前言，腰脊不可以俛仰屈伸，毛悴色夭，死於季夏。恐懼而不解則傷精，精傷則骨痠痿厥，精時自下。是故五藏主藏精者也，不可傷，傷則失守而陰虛，陰虛則無氣，無氣則死矣。**

*Supplied English (abridged to what the script uses):* "The mind, when constantly
anxious and preoccupied, injures the spirit … and death in winter. The spleen, when
burdened by sorrow and worry without relief, injures intention … and death occurs in
spring. The liver, when deeply moved by sorrow and grief, injures the soul … and
death occurs in autumn. The lungs, when subjected to excessive joy and pleasure
without limit, injure the corporeal spirit … leading to death in summer. The
kidneys, when subjected to intense anger without cessation, injure will … resulting
in death during the late summer. … Therefore, the five zang organs are primarily
responsible for storing essence; they must not be injured. If injured, the body
loses its foundation and yin deficiency results; When yin is deficient, there is no
qi; without qi, death occurs."

**Blocks 72, 73, 74, 75, 82.**

### §6 — The limit of the needle

**是故用鍼者，察觀病人之態，以知精神魂魄之存亡得失之意，五者已傷，鍼不可以治之也。**

*Supplied English:* "Therefore, those who use acupuncture must observe the patient's
condition to discern whether essence, spirit, soul, and corporeal spirit are present
or lost, and understand gains and losses. If all five aspects are injured,
acupuncture cannot cure it."

**Blocks 83, 85.**

### §7 — The storage table

**肝藏血，血舍魂，肝氣虛則恐，實則怒。脾藏營，營舍意，脾氣虛則四肢不用、五藏不安，實則腹脹、經溲不利。心藏脈，脈舍神，心氣虛則悲，實則笑不休。肺藏氣，氣舍魄，肺氣虛則鼻塞不利少氣，實則喘喝胸盈仰息。腎藏精，精舍志，腎氣虛則厥，實則脹。五藏不安，必審五藏之病形，以知其氣之虛實，謹而調之也。**

*Supplied English:* "The liver stores blood; blood houses the soul. When liver qi is
deficient, fear arises; when excessive, anger results. The spleen stores nutritive
essence; nutritive essence houses intention … The heart stores the vessels; the
vessels house the spirit. When heart qi is deficient, sorrow arises; when excessive,
one laughs incessantly. The lungs store qi; qi houses the corporeal spirit … The
kidneys store essence; essence houses will … One must carefully examine the
pathological conditions of the five zang organs to understand whether their qi is
deficient or excessive, and then regulate them with caution."

**Blocks 88, 89, 90, 92.**

### Five departures from the supplied English

The supplied translation is serviceable and is quoted above unaltered. This script
departs from it in five places, each deliberately, and each departure is a
translation judgement rather than a dramatisation:

1. **魂 and 魄 are kept untranslated as *hun* and *po*.** The supplied English gives
   *soul* and *corporeal spirit*, which is defensible but asymmetric — one is a
   familiar word and the other is a phrase, and a viewer hears them as a thing and a
   description of a thing rather than as a pair. **The chapter defines them
   symmetrically**, purely by which earlier term each travels with, and blocks 31 to
   34 depend on that symmetry being audible.
2. **心 is *heart* and never *mind*.** The supplied English gives "what enables one
   to deal with external things is called the mind," which quietly resolves the
   episode's central ambiguity in the reader's favour. **The word is the organ**,
   and block 36 exists to say that the double meaning is the position rather than a
   translation problem.
3. **兩精相搏 is *strike against each other*, not *interact*.** 搏 is a word for
   striking or grappling. *Interact* is smoother and drains the definition of the
   thing that makes it remarkable — that spirit is defined as a **collision**, which
   is the trailer's hook and blocks 28 and 29 here.
4. **神 is *spirit* throughout and never *mind* or *consciousness*.** Both modern
   glosses are in circulation and both import a whole philosophy of mind that this
   chapter does not hold. The cost is that *spirit* sounds religious in English,
   which block 29 addresses directly rather than translating around.
5. **長生久視 is rendered *life is long* and is not given as *longevity*.** The
   supplied English has "longevity with enduring vision can be achieved," which is
   accurate. **The word *longevity* is kept out of this script entirely** on channel
   grounds rather than translation grounds — see *Compliance notes*.

**One passage of §5 is deliberately not rendered anywhere in the script.** The
physical descriptions of collapse — 破䐃脫肉 (broken flesh and loss of muscle),
皮革焦 (skin and leather parched), 陰縮而攣筋 — are quoted in this section for
completeness and **appear in no narration line and in no shot.** `CLAUDE.md` requires
mortality to be kept reflective and non-graphic, portraits rather than bodies, and
this is the chapter's one passage that would breach it. Blocks 72 to 74 carry the
hair and the complexion only, which the source names in the same breath.
