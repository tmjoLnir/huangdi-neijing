## Why 15:30, and not 14:00 or 17:00

The requested window was 14–17 minutes with a hard floor of 11:30, and the brief asked
for the length that minimises padding and shortening. **93 blocks — 15:30 — is where
this chapter's usable material actually lands**, and the reasoning is arithmetic rather
than taste, because a 10s block is the unit that gets padded or cut.

This chapter has the opposite problem from most of the slate. **The received text is
four paragraphs long — under seven hundred characters — and almost every sentence in it
is usable.** Nothing has to be excluded for length. What sets the runtime is not the
source but the two demonstrations the source cannot do for itself: the **uniqueness
proof**, which is the episode's whole reason for existing, and the **calendar audit**,
which is the only thing in the episode that tests the chapter against anything outside
it. Costing the material by what each part can carry without inventing filler:

| Material | Blocks | Why that many |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open — the excavated board, what is written on it, citation, title, thesis, the auditor's brief | 9 | the object is the hook and it is real; the thesis has to be stated before the arithmetic starts or the arithmetic reads as trivia |

That totals **93**. Both edges of the requested window cost something real:

- **14:00 is 84 blocks — 9 short**, and there is no clean nine. The available nine are
  four off the proof, three off the omen act and two off the eight-wind recitation.
  **Cost:** the proof stops being a proof and becomes an assertion with a diagram, which
  removes the only thing this episode has that no other episode on the slate can do. It
  is available and is specified exactly in *Runtime levers*.
- **17:00 is 102 blocks — 9 too many, and the nine are the wrong nine.** The material
  that would fill them is one block per wind instead of two winds per block, which is a
  taxonomy read aloud at dictation speed, and the chapter's pathology clauses — the
  discharge-and-fever set, the atrophy clause and the hemiplegia clause. **The first is

- **11:30 (69 blocks) is the format floor, not a target.** It cannot be reached without

**15:30 is the recommendation.** It sits in the middle of the requested window rather
than at either edge, and the thing that sets the length here is not the chapter's
length but the **shape of a demonstration**: a proof has a fixed number of steps in it,
and an audit has to be set up, performed and then answered fairly or it is a cheap shot.

## What this episode argues

The obvious reading of this chapter is that ancient Chinese doctors had a weather
superstition. That reading is not wrong so much as uninteresting, and it misses the
thing that makes the chapter worth an episode.

**The spine is that one grid carries two things of completely different quality, and
the good one is why nobody ever checked the bad one.** Four things happen here:

1. **A real theorem is sitting inside a medical textbook.** The nine palaces are the
   Luoshu, the oldest magic square known anywhere. Every row and column and diagonal
   sums to fifteen, and — this is the part that is never said in popular treatments —
   **the arrangement is forced.** There is exactly one magic square of order three, up
   to rotation and reflection, and the episode proves it on screen in six blocks using
   nothing but the numbers one to nine.
2. **The chapter's medical doctrine is hung on that grid, and it is not a discovery.**
   Eight compass points already existed, so the body was divided into eight matching
   pieces. The number of winds was decided by the compass before anybody looked at a
   sick person. The correspondences are a filing system wearing the clothes of an
   observation.
3. **The doctrine has a clause that makes it untestable.** A wind harms you only if it
   comes from the quarter where you are already weak — so wind with illness confirms the
   scheme and wind without illness confirms it too. A rule that absorbs both outcomes
   has stopped being about the weather.
4. **And the calendar drifts, and nobody notices.** Six palaces of forty six days and
   two of forty five is three hundred and sixty six — about three quarters of a day
   longer than a year. Inside sixty years the whole grid is a full palace out of
   register with the sky it claims to describe. **The chapter never adds up its own
   numbers**, and the episode does.

**The thesis is that the second, third and fourth things happened because of the
first.** A doctrine resting on a figure that beautiful looks like it has already been
proved. Nobody audits a thing that comes out level.

**And the slate's own point is the close.** The same lacquered board named the harvest,
the fate of a general and the illness in a man's shoulder, and nobody in the room
thought those were three different questions. Asking whether this was science imports a
boundary that did not exist. That is a better answer than any verdict.

**Every one of the 93 blocks below is inside its voice's range**, verified by parsing
this document's own narration table and dropping tokens that contain no letter or
digit — because a spaced em-dash is not a word and counting it as one spends real
budget on a window 1.4s wide. **Block 93 is the single deliberate exception** and the
reason is under *Where the re-rolls will be*.

Four consequences shape every line here, and none is stylistic:

- **The short interjection is dead.** Under an 8.6s floor there is no quick jab, and
  `--clip-seconds` moves the window rather than widening it. **Every character line is a
  full paragraph** — including six audit findings that would each be one sentence on
  stage. This is why Lei-Gong has nine blocks and not twenty-five: at 47–52 words each,
  an auditor who comments on everything becomes the narrator.
- **There is not a single comma in any of the ninety-three narration cells.** The
  2026-08-09 measurement put a 31-word Arthur line carrying a four-item comma list at
  **12.022s** against **8.913s** for the same 31 words with none — a 3.1-second swing
  from punctuation alone, on a window 1.4 seconds wide. **This chapter is nothing but
  enumerations** — nine palaces, eight winds, five offices, nine numbers, eight lines,
  eight triples — and is therefore the worst possible case for that failure. Every
  enumeration below is joined with *and* or split into sentences. **Do not add one.**
- **The rates are survivor-biased.** ~4.4 w/s is what Arthur's *passing* takes
  delivered, and his rate moved between **3.98 and 4.80 w/s within a single seven-block
  cut** on lines of near-identical length. A word count predicts a take only to about
  ±1.5s. **Generate two variants per block and keep the better one** — at ~1.45 a take
  that is the cheapest reliable method.
- **Reach the window with words and not with full stops.** The assembler warns on
  internal pauses ≥0.8s, so padding a thin line with sentence breaks passes the duration
  gate and trips the pause warning. **No block below exceeds four sentences.**

These are **arithmetic against measured rates, not measurements**. Treat the first pass
as calibration and budget re-takes explicitly — the preflight below does.

**One extra hazard is specific to this chapter and it is worth naming here.** This is a
script full of spoken numbers, and *forty six* and *three hundred and sixty six* are
each counted as several words while occupying the mouth for a long time. **Numeral-heavy
blocks are the ones most likely to run over**, and blocks 14, 15, 18, 27, 29, 30, 55,
75 and 76 are the numeral-heaviest in the cut. If one of those misses it will miss high,
and the fix is to move a number into the shot list rather than to break the sentence.

### How to read this script

`CLAUDE.md` asks for **SOUND / VISUAL / CHARACTER** blocks and a numbered shot list;
`build_subtitles.js` needs a **three-column narration table** to build the required
`.srt`/`.vtt` sidecars. They are combined rather than duplicated:

- **The narration tables are canonical for words.** Blocks run continuously **1–93
  across the whole episode**, never per-act; the assembly manifest is flat and act-local
  numbering produces off-by-one errors that are expensive to find in a fifteen-minute
  render.
- **The SOUND / VISUAL / direction prose between them is canonical for performance**,
  and the **numbered shot list after block 93 is canonical for picture.** Neither
  restates a narration line.
- **The speaker is named in the beat column**, not a fourth column — the parser reads
  exactly three, and a fourth would leak into the caption text.
- **No markdown inside a narration cell.** `build_subtitles.js` copies the third column
  into the cue verbatim and strips nothing, so a `**bold**` emphasis burns into the
  picture as literal asterisks. **Put emphasis in the direction prose, never in the
  line.**

> **Do not insert a table whose first cell is a bare integer anywhere between blocks 1
> and 93.** `parseNarration` keeps the first consecutive run from block 1 and stops at
> the first row that breaks it, so a stray numeric table silently truncates the sidecar.
> Every other table in this document sits before block 1 or after block 93 for that
> reason, and the ON-SCREEN TEXT card table prefixes its first column with `Block` so it
> cannot collide. The shot list after block 93 is a numbered *list*, not a table, for
> the same reason.

## The floor and the ninth square — the visual system

The slate assigns this chapter the **grid traversal** form, and §2.2 makes clear why
that matters beyond taste: twenty episodes in the same question-and-answer shape is the
Generic/Repetitive Content failure mode, so **every episode carries a distinct
structural form.** Here the form is the set itself.

**The whole episode plays on a nine-square lacquer floor in an open hall.** A lacquered
diviner's board — a round upper plate turning on a square lower plate — sits under cloth
on a low table at the southern edge. That board is the object excavated in the cold
open, and it is the last thing touched before the close.

| Act | The floor | The ninth square |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | unmarked black lacquer; Fan-di on a high seat at the north edge, not on it | dark and unremarked |
| Act I | the eight outer cells light one at a time in the circuit order, one per residence | **dark while all eight around it light** |
| Act II | knotted dot clusters ink themselves into every cell | it takes its dots like the rest and still does not light |
| Act III | ink grain crosses from one named edge at a time and the cell it reaches dims | dark |
| **Act IV** | **eight straight lines of light join opposite cells, and every one of them passes through the ninth square** | **still dark, and now conspicuously so — everything crosses it and nothing lands** |
| Act V | four office seals settle onto the four cardinal cells and the board is unwrapped | dark |
| Act VI | **the entire floor rotates by one cell width under everyone standing on it** and nothing else changes | dark |
| Act VII | Fan-di steps onto it for the first time in the episode | **it lights, once, and holds to the end card** |

Three rules keep it from becoming decoration:

- **Nobody stands on the centre square and it is never lit before shot 87.** Not
  Fan-di, not Dr-Qi, not Lei-Gong, not the marker. This is true to the chapter — the
  annual circuit is of the eight, and the middle palace is where the Great One stands to
  face outward rather than a station of the year — and it is also the episode's argument
  rendered as a rule about the picture. **Any shot that breaks it says the opposite of
  its own narration.**
- **The numbers are knotted dot clusters and never digits, in all ninety-three shots.**
  That is the historically correct form of the Luoshu diagram, and it removes the entire
  class of failure where a generative model renders a wrong numeral confidently and the
  error survives to the delivered file. **Arabic numerals and the running sums are
  on-screen text added at edit**, and they are the second-largest hand step in the cut.
- **Wind is ink grain entering from a named edge of frame. It is never a face and never
  a figure, and the Great One is a small brushed marker and never a person.** See
  *Compliance notes* — for this chapter a personified wind or a robed star deity is the
  single largest prompt-stage risk, because it converts a history-of-mathematics episode
  into occult imagery in one shot.

## Source text and translation

> **⚠ The source file supplied this chapter in SIMPLIFIED Chinese. Every card in this
> cut and every character below is set in TRADITIONAL, converted by hand.** The chapter
> arrived as `10_Ch_LS77__Magic_square_of_the_winds.md`, a ctext.org export whose Chinese
> extracted cleanly — 九宫八风 / 叶蛰之宫 / 圣人日避虚邪之道 — but in simplified form
> throughout. **The conversion below is ours and nothing downstream re-checks it.**
> ffmpeg renders a wrong or missing glyph silently, fourteen CJK cards depend on this
> section, and a tofu box survives to the delivered file without erroring. **Verify every
> card glyph against a traditional edition before burn.**

> **⚠ The supplied English carries at least four extraction or transliteration errors**,
> and three of them touch material this episode uses. 天宮 is romanised *"Tiangu"* where
> the Chinese plainly reads 天宮 (Tiangong). 大弱風 is romanised *"Dauruo Wind"*. 嬰兀風
> is a corruption of the received 嬰兒風. And 後之則多汗 is rendered *"much perspiration
> (or heat)"*, which is a reading of 汗 that most editors reject. **All four are worked
> from the Chinese rather than from the supplied English and are argued individually
> below.**

### §1 — The annual circuit

**太一常以冬至之日，居葉蟄之宮四十六日，明日居天留四十六日，明日居倉門四十六日，明日居陰洛四十五日，明日居天宮四十六日，明日居玄委四十六日，明日居倉果四十六日，明日居新洛四十五日，明日復居葉蟄之宮，曰冬至矣。**

*Working rendering:* The Great One customarily, on the day of the winter solstice,
resides in the palace of Leaf Hibernation for forty-six days. The next day it resides in
Heavenly Detention for forty-six days; then Granary Gate for forty-six; then Hidden Luo
for forty-five; then the Heavenly Palace for forty-six; then Dark Delegation for
forty-six; then Granary Fruit for forty-six; then New Luo for forty-five. The next day it
resides again in Leaf Hibernation, and this is called the winter solstice.

**The source's own textual note is preserved:** the received graph is 大一, and 《四庫全書》
reads 太. This cut uses **太一 / Taiyi / the Great One** throughout, which is the standard
form and the form on the excavated board.

**The eight palaces and their compass points**, which the passage does not state and
which every commentary supplies: 葉蟄 north, 天留 north-east, 倉門 east, 陰洛 south-east,
天宮 south, 玄委 south-west, 倉果 west, 新洛 north-west. The solstices and equinoxes in
§3 confirm the four cardinal assignments internally — winter solstice with 葉蟄, spring
equinox with 倉門, autumn equinox with 倉果, summer solstice with 天宮.

**The sum is ours and the chapter never performs it.** 46 × 6 = 276, 45 × 2 = 90, total
**366**. Blocks 14, 18, 75 and 76 are built on that arithmetic and on the comparison
against the tropical year of about 365.2422 days, giving an annual excess of about
**0.758 days** — one full day in about sixteen months and one full 46-day palace in
about **sixty-one years**. **Verify both figures before the takes are recorded.**

**An observation about the two short palaces, deliberately kept out of the narration.**
The two palaces docked a day are 陰洛 and 新洛 — the only two of the eight whose names
contain 洛, the river of the Luoshu. That is a fact about the names and not a claim about
anyone's intent, and it is left out of the script only because it costs a block the
budget has not got. It is the first thing to add if the runtime moves — see *Runtime
levers*.

### §2 — The daily journey

**太一日游，以冬至之日，居葉蟄之宮，數所在日，從一處至九日，復返於一。常如是無已，終而復始。**

*Working rendering:* The Great One also travels daily. On the day of the winter solstice
it resides in the palace of Leaf Hibernation, counting the days of its station, from the
first place to the ninth day, and then returning to the first. It is always thus without
end; it finishes and begins again.

**This is the passage that requires a ninth palace**, and it is why block 21 hands
straight into Act II. An eight-point circuit does not need a centre; a nine-day count
does.

### §3 — Heaven's answer, the omens, and the two winds

**太一移日，天必應之以風雨，以其日風雨則吉，歲美民安少病矣。先之則多雨，後之則多汗。**

*Working rendering:* When the Great One moves on a given day, heaven must answer it with
wind and rain. Wind and rain on that day is auspicious: the year will be plentiful, the
people at peace, and sickness rare. If it comes before its day there will be much rain;
if after, drought.

> **Departure 1 — 汗 read as 旱.** The received graph is 汗 *sweat*, which the supplied
> English renders *"much perspiration (or heat)"*. The parallelism with 多雨 wants a
> weather term, 汗 and 旱 are homophones (*hàn*), and most editors read **旱 drought**.
> **This cut reads drought and says so on screen** — block 65 is a whole block spent
> disclosing the crux rather than hiding it. It is the only place in ninety-three blocks
> where the episode tells the viewer that a word is disputed, and it is there because a
> chapter about exact counting deserves to be handled exactly.

**太一在冬至之日有變，占在君；太一在春分之日有變，占在相；太一在中宮之日有變，占在吏；太一在秋分之日有變，占在將；太一在夏至之日有變，占在百姓。所謂有變者，太一居五宮之日，病風折樹木，揚沙石，各以其所主，占貴賤。**

*Working rendering:* A change on the day of the winter solstice is an omen concerning the
ruler; on the day of the spring equinox, the chancellor; on the day of the central palace,
the officials; on the day of the autumn equinox, the generals; on the day of the summer
solstice, the common people. What is meant by a change is that on the days the Great One
occupies one of the five palaces there is a sickening wind that breaks trees and lifts
sand and stones. Interpret each according to what that palace governs, and so divine
high fortune and low.

**因視風所從來而占之，風從其所居之鄉來為實風，主生，長養萬物；從其衝後來為虛風，傷人者也，主殺，主害者。謹候虛風而避之，故聖人日避虛邪之道，如避矢石然，邪弗能害，此之謂也。**

*Working rendering:* Observe the quarter the wind comes from and divine accordingly. Wind
coming from the quarter where it resides is the full wind: it rules生, and raises and
nourishes the ten thousand things. Wind coming from behind the opposing quarter is the
empty wind: it is what injures people, and it rules killing and rules harm. Watch
carefully for the empty wind and avoid it. Therefore the sage daily avoids the way of the
empty evil as one avoids arrows and stones, and the evil cannot harm him. This is what is
meant.

> **Departure 2 — 朝八風 rendered as *holds court for*.** The supplied English of §4 has
> *"faces the Eight Winds"*. 朝 is the court-audience verb — a ruler receiving those who
> attend on him — and the whole passage is staged as a sovereign in a central hall
> receiving reports from eight directions. **"Holds court for" is doing real work at block
> 36**, because the political framing is the episode's Act V argument and the text puts it
> there itself.

### §4 — The eight winds, and the three vacuities

**是故太一入徙立於中宮，乃朝八風，以占吉凶也。風從南方來，名曰大弱風，其傷人也，內舍於心，外在於脈，氣主熱。風從西南方來，名曰謀風，其傷人也，內舍於脾，外在於肌，其氣主為弱。風從西方來，名曰剛風，其傷人也，內舍於肺，外在於皮膚，其氣主為燥。風從西北方來，名曰折風，其傷人也，內舍於小腸，外在於手太陽脈，脈絕則溢，脈閉則結不通，善暴死。**

**風從北方來，名曰大剛風，其傷人也，內舍於腎，外在於骨與肩背之膂筋，其氣主為寒也。風從東北方來，名曰凶風，其傷人也，內舍於大腸，外在於兩脅腋骨下及肢節。風從東方來，名曰嬰兒風，其傷人也，內舍於肝，外在於筋紐，其氣主為身濕。風從東南方來，名曰弱風，其傷人也，內舍於胃，外在肌肉，其氣主體重。**

**此八風皆從其虛之鄉來，乃能病人。三虛相搏，則為暴病卒死。兩實一虛，病則為淋露寒熱。犯其兩濕之地，則為痿。故聖人避風，如避矢石焉。其有三虛而偏中於邪風，則為仆偏枯矣。**

*Working rendering of the eight:* south — 大弱風 Great Weak Wind, lodging in the heart,
outwardly in the vessels, its qi ruling heat. Southwest — 謀風 Scheming Wind, lodging in
the spleen, outwardly in the flesh, ruling weakness. West — 剛風 Hard Wind, lodging in the
lung, outwardly in the skin, ruling dryness. Northwest — 折風 Breaking Wind, lodging in the
small intestine, outwardly in the hand taiyang vessel, and sudden death is common.
North — 大剛風 Great Hard Wind, lodging in the kidney, outwardly in the bones and the
sinews of shoulder and back, ruling cold. Northeast — 凶風 Fierce Wind, lodging in the
large intestine, outwardly below the ribs at the flanks and at the joints of the limbs.
East — 嬰兒風 Infant Wind, lodging in the liver, outwardly in the sinews, ruling dampness
of the body. Southeast — 弱風 Weak Wind, lodging in the stomach, outwardly in the muscles,
ruling heaviness of the body.

> **Departure 3 — 嬰兀風 corrected to 嬰兒風.** The source file reads 婴兀风 and the
> supplied English transliterates *"Yingwu Wind"*. 兀 for 兒 is a graphic corruption; the
> received text is **嬰兒風, the Infant Wind**, which is also the reading every
> commentary carries. **Block 42 says Infant Wind.** Do not restore the source's form.

> **Departure 4 — 天宮 is not "Tiangu", and 大弱風 is not "Dauruo".** Both are
> romanisation failures in the supplied English rather than variants. The Chinese is
> unambiguous in each case.

**招搖 is not in this passage at all.** The centre palace goes unnamed here — the text
says only 中宮, the central palace. **招搖 comes from the wider nine-palace tradition and
from the excavated board**, and block 87's card is labelled accordingly on screen. Do not
present it as a quotation from this chapter.

**戴九履一 is not in this passage either.** The Luoshu number-formula quoted on block
23's card comes from the Hall of Brightness material, not from Lingshu 77. **The chapter
names nine palaces and never once writes their numbers.** That gap is the single most
important thing in this section: **every numerical claim this episode makes about the
grid is imported**, and the episode says so at block 58 in Arthur's own voice.

### Excluded from every narration line, and the exclusion is deliberate

Three clauses of §4 appear nowhere in the ninety-three blocks and appear in no shot.
They are quoted above **only so that nobody restores them believing they were
overlooked**:

- **兩實一虛，病則為淋露寒熱** — a symptom list attached to a diagnostic condition.
- **犯其兩濕之地，則為痿** — atrophy and paralysis as an outcome of exposure.
- **其有三虛而偏中於邪風，則為仆偏枯矣** — collapse and hemiplegia.
- **脈絕則溢，脈閉則結不通** — the vessel mechanism behind 善暴死. **The 善暴死 clause
  itself is reported once**, at block 39, as *"the chapter says men die suddenly of it"* —
  reported speech about a text, with the mechanism left out.

**Do not add any of the four at any runtime.** They are the chapter's only genuinely
compliance-sensitive material, they are prognostic and pathological rather than
historical, and the episode loses nothing by omitting them because its argument is about
the grid rather than about the pathology hung on it.
