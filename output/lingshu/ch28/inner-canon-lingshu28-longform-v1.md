## What this episode argues

**The spine is the first sentence of the chapter, not its contents.** Lingshu 28
opens with the Yellow Emperor at leisure **辟左右** — *dismissing those to left and
right* — and asking for **口問**, the oral questions. Qi Bo **避席再拜**: rises from
the mat and bows twice. Then answers a question about yawning.

Every other chapter in this canon presents itself as doctrine. This one presents
itself as **先師之所口傳** — what the former teachers passed down by mouth. It is
the canon's own admission that some of what it knows was never written down,
and the chapter we have is a transcript of somebody finally writing it.

Four things follow, and they are the episode:

1. **The questions are undignified, and that is why they are interesting.** Why do
   we yawn, hiccup, belch, sneeze, drool, sob, shiver, go limp, hear ringing, bite
   our own tongue, weep, sigh. Nobody writes down what everybody already knows, so
   the ordinary is the part of any tradition most likely to be lost.
2. **A cleared room is a real epistemic instrument, and it has a cost.** It buys
   candour — Fan-di asks about his own tinnitus and his own sneezing only once
   nobody can hear him. It also removes the other people.
3. **The chapter runs out of road on exactly one question**, and the reason is
   structural rather than a wrong guess: **every answer in it has a single body in
   it.** One person, one set of channels, one clock.
4. **Yawning is catching.** It is documented in humans, in chimpanzees, and in
   dogs watching people, and the mechanism is unknown. It takes two people. **Two
   people is the one thing an emptied room does not have.**

**The dramatic engine:** the room, and who is left in it. Fan-di clears the hall
to ask what he would not ask in front of the court. **The fan is the tell** — snapped
open he is performing, set down he means it; he sets it down once, at block 106,
and does not pick it up. **Lei-Gong is in the doorway** and was never dismissed,
because nobody thinks to dismiss a boy. He has four blocks. The last two are the
payoff.

**The closing argument:** the chapter's best question defeated it, and it defeated
it in the one way an emptied room guarantees. What it did right was ask at all —
it treated twelve ordinary, undignified events as things with causes, and reached
for no spirit, no curse, no ancestor and no moral fault anywhere in twelve
answers.

**Every count in this script is machine-generated from the parsed narration
table**, dropping tokens that contain no letter or digit — a spaced em-dash is not
a word, and counting it as one spends a whole word of budget on a window 1.4s wide.

Four consequences shape every line below, and none of them is stylistic:

- **The short interjection is dead.** Under an 8.6s floor there is no quick jab,
  and `--clip-seconds` moves the window rather than widening it. **Every character
  line here is a full paragraph.** It bites hardest on Lei-Gong: a boy in a doorway
  wants one-line interruptions and cannot have them, so his four blocks are written
  as the longest speeches in the cut.
- **The rates are survivor-biased.** They are what *passing* takes delivered.
  Across every recorded Suwen 1 v5 attempt Arthur averaged **3.04 w/s** against a
  passing rate of 3.65. These counts are sized for a voice's fast mode and will
  regularly return over the ceiling.
- **The run-to-run spread exceeds the window.** Identical 26-word text came back at
  **10.23s and 7.79s** on consecutive generations — 2.44s apart on a window 1.4s
  wide. Generate two variants per block and keep the better one; expect misses on
  **either** side.
- **Reach the window with words, not full stops.** The assembler warns on internal
  pauses ≥0.8s, and the mandated disclaimer is the measured proof: its two short
  full-stopped sentences returned **11.33s, 16.93s and 10.54s** before the trailing
  clause was cut. **Block 108's tail is budgeted at ~18 words for that reason.**

These remain **arithmetic against measured rates, not measurements of this script**.
Treat the first pass as calibration and budget re-takes explicitly.

### How to read this script

`CLAUDE.md` asks for **SOUND / VISUAL / CHARACTER** blocks; `build_subtitles.js`
needs a **three-column narration table**. The two are combined rather than
duplicated, because two copies of 108 lines is how they drift apart:

- **The narration tables below are canonical for words.** Blocks run continuously
  **1–108 across the whole episode**, never per-act; the assembly manifest is flat
  and act-local numbering produces off-by-one errors that are expensive to find in
  an 18-minute render.
- **The SOUND / VISUAL / direction prose between them is canonical for staging and
  performance.** It never restates a line.
- **The numbered shot list sits after block 108**, keyed to the same block numbers.
- **The speaker is named in the beat column**, not a fourth column — the parser
  reads exactly three, and a fourth would leak into the caption text.

> **Do not insert a table whose first cell is a bare integer anywhere between
> blocks 1 and 108.** `parseNarration` keeps the first consecutive run from block 1
> and stops at the first row that breaks it, so a stray numeric table silently
> truncates the sidecar. Every other table in this document sits before block 1 or
> after block 108, and the ON-SCREEN TEXT card table prefixes its first column with
> `Block` so it cannot collide. The shot list is safe where it is for the same
> reason: it restarts at 1 *after* the run has completed, which ends the scan
> rather than corrupting it.
