# CHRONICLE OF BALANCE
## Episode 01 Trailer — "The First Sentence"
### Runtime 1:08 · 16:9 master + 9:16 vertical cut (0:38)

**Companion to:** Episode 01 long-form, *The First Sentence*
**Framing:** History and philosophy of science. Not health or wellness.
**Cast:** FAN-DI (gold) · DR. QI (jade) · XIAO-LEI (cinnabar)
**Spoken word count:** ~135 · **The turn lands at 0:26**

---

## STRATEGY

The trailer does not summarise the episode. It sells **one idea** and refuses to resolve it:

> *The founding text of Chinese medicine opens with a sentence that is false. It was copied faithfully for two thousand years anyway.*

The withheld element is **why.** No answer is given. The answer is the episode.

This also does the channel's positioning work in sixty seconds: a new viewer learns immediately that this is a series which **contradicts its own source material.** That is the differentiator against the enormous volume of uncritical ancient-wisdom content, and it is the single clearest signal to both viewers and classifiers that this is history-of-science, not wellness.

**Structure:** claim → contradiction → escalation → withhold.

## AUDIO NOTES

**No guqin in the trailer.** This is deliberate and it is a scheduling decision, not an aesthetic one: guqin recordings require independent licensing regardless of the instrument's antiquity, and that clearance remains unresolved. Scoring the trailer with bell, sub-bass and silence decouples the trailer's ship date from the licensing blocker entirely, so it can go out ahead of the episode as a teaser without waiting on rights.

**Silence is the instrument.** The two hardest beats — shot 5 and shot 12 — are both silence. Do not fill them.

**Thumbnail direction:** Xiao-Lei's hand flat on the bamboo scroll, cinnabar against gold, with the scroll text visible beneath. It reads as *objection*, not as *ancient secret*. Avoid any thumbnail featuring an aged face, a hundred-year claim, or a herb.

**Note on the hook:** "It's false" is a strong claim to lead with, and it is defensible — the correction runs *toward* mainstream historical consensus, not away from it. That direction is what keeps this clear of misleading-claims review. Do not soften it to "misleading" or "exaggerated"; the accuracy of the strong version is precisely what makes it safe.
