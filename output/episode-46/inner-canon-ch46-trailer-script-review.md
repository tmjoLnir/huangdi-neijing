# Script review — `ch46lingshu11trailerscript.md` ("CHRONICLE OF BALANCE — The Night Watch")

Review of the submitted Chapter 46 trailer script against `CLAUDE.md`,
`.claude/skills/higgsfield-production/SKILL.md`, `docs/`, and the cuts shipped at
the time of review. Chapter 46 is the Blueprint's sleep chapter —
营卫生会 · *Meeting of Nutritive & Defensive Qi* — and the publish sequence's
**Ep 2**, the highest-RPM pick on the whole slate.

**Verdict:** the best-structured script this repo has received. The eleven-shot
spine (vigil → system → exchange → silence → the honest line) is genuinely well
built, the silence beat is the right instinct, and **VO 6 is the best line
anyone has written for this series** — *"Almost every mechanism in it was wrong.
Every observation in it has survived."* That posture should become a house rule,
not a one-cut flourish.

But as submitted it is a **source script, not a production document**, and three
of its problems are content, not format. The most serious is on the title card:
**the chapter citation is wrong**, and it is wrong in a way a viewer can check
in thirty seconds.

Nothing here needs the script thrown out. Everything below is a re-cut, and the
re-cut is `output/episode-46/inner-canon-ch46-trailer-v1.md`.

## 1. Blockers — the cut cannot be produced until these are resolved

### 1.1 The chapter citation is wrong (most important)

The script's subtitle, header and title card all read **"Lingshu 11 · 營衛生會."**

In the received *Ling Shu*, **營衛生會 is chapter 18**. Chapter 11 is **經別**
(*Divergent Channels*) — a different chapter on a different subject.

The error is inherited from `docs/Emperors Inner Canon Series Blueprint.md`, and
it is systematic rather than a typo. Every Ling Shu parenthetical in that
document is exactly `index − 35`:

| Blueprint says | Arithmetic | Received Ling Shu chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Ch 40 (Ling Shu 5) · 寿夭刚柔 | 40 − 35 = 5 | **6** |
| Ch 42 (Ling Shu 7) · 本神 | 42 − 35 = 7 | **8** |
| **Ch 46 (Ling Shu 11) · 营卫生会** | **46 − 35 = 11** | **18** |
| Ch 57 (Ling Shu 22) · 五阅五使 | 57 − 35 = 22 | **37** |
| Ch 61 (Ling Shu 26) · 顺气一日分为四时 | 61 − 35 = 26 | **44** |
| Ch 68 (Ling Shu 33) · 天年 | 68 − 35 = 33 | **54** |
| Ch 72 (Ling Shu 37) · 阴阳二十五人 | 72 − 35 = 37 | **64** |

Seven for seven on the offset, zero for seven on the canonical number. The
Blueprint's 1–80 index is a **curated slate numbering** (it selects 45 of Ling
Shu's 81 chapters and 35 of Su Wen's 81), so its parentheticals are index
positions dressed up as citations.

Why this matters more here than anywhere else: this channel's entire
differentiator is **contradicting uncritical ancient-wisdom content**. The one
thing it cannot afford is to be the channel caught miscounting the source it is
correcting — and the wrong number is burned onto a card, in the trailer, at the
top of the funnel.

**Fix, applied in the re-cut:**

- The document header carries the canonical citation: **灵枢·营卫生会第十八**.
- **"Chapter 46" survives only as the *series* episode index**, labelled as such,
  never as a Ling Shu chapter number.
- **The spoken title drops the number entirely** — *"The Emperor's Inner Canon.
  The Night Watch."* Chapter 1 spoke *"Chapter One — The First Sentence"* and
  could, because for Su Wen 1 the slate index and the canonical number coincide.
  From Su Wen 6 onward they diverge, so a spoken number is a liability for every
  remaining chapter on the slate, not just this one. If a number is wanted on
  screen, it must read *"Ling Shu 18"*, not *"Chapter 46."*

**Knock-on, out of scope for this cut but recorded:** the same arithmetic breaks
the Su Wen half of the index from #6 onward, so any chapter drawn from the slate
at index 6 or higher needs its canonical number checked before a citation goes on
screen. Logged as a P3 against the Blueprint itself.

### 1.2 Three factual errors and one overclaim in the narration

The physiology is otherwise well read — 營 inside the vessels, 衛 outside them,
合陰 at midnight, and the 三焦 triptych are all correct and well chosen. Four
things are not.

**a) "Fifty circuits, every night" (VO 4) is wrong by a factor of two.**
The text is 營周不休，**五十而復大會** — fifty circuits per **day *and* night**,
not per night. The day/night split is a separate figure and belongs to the
defensive qi specifically: 衛氣行於陰二十五度，行於陽二十五度，分為晝夜 — twenty-five
circuits through yin, twenty-five through yang. As written the trailer doubles
the canon's own number.

*The correction is a better line.* "Fifty circuits, a day and a night. The guard
walks twenty-five in the light, twenty-five in the dark" is more concrete than
the original, keeps the clock image, and is exactly what the text says.

**b) "And why the young cannot stay that way" (VO 2) inverts the source.**
The Emperor's paired questions are 老人之**不夜瞑**者 — why do the old not sleep at
night — and 少壯之人**不晝瞑**者 — why do the young **not sleep in the daytime**.
The script asks why the young *cannot stay awake*, which is the opposite, and
shot 3 illustrates it with an undated daytime bench-nap.

The accurate pairing is also the poignant one, and it is the twist the Blueprint
promises: 壯者…**晝精而夜瞑** against 老者…**晝不精，夜不瞑**. The young get both
halves of the day; **the old lose both**. Fixed in the re-cut by dating the
sleeping-boy image to the same night as the vigil, which preserves the comic
image and stops it contradicting the text.

**c) The counter completing "循 50 / 50" at cold dawn (shot 10) contradicts
合陰 at shot 9.** The great meeting is 夜半而大會，萬民皆臥，命曰合陰 — **midnight**.
Dawn is 平旦陰盡而陽受氣, the handover, not the completion. The script puts 合陰 in
the right place and then puts the count in the wrong one. The re-cut drops the
counter (see §1.5) and keeps the meeting at midnight.

**d) "Every observation in it has survived" (VO 6) is the ch1 superlative
problem again.** The claim is unbounded and a commenter can break it in one
reply — the chapter also asserts that blood and sweat share a source
(奪血者無汗，奪汗者無血) and that wine's fluid leaves the body before grain's does.
The *central* observation — that sleep fragments with age — is well supported and
is what the line is actually about. The re-cut narrows it to **"What it noticed
is still true, two thousand years later,"** which loses nothing and cannot be
contested. This is the same call the ch1 review made on *"the most copied
medical text in East Asian history."*

### 1.3 The timing grid is not the assembler's grid

`explainer_video` assembles **fixed 10-second blocks**: a short take is centred,
a slightly long one is sped up pitch-safely, the video is never stretched. No
frame-accurate cut, no beat control, no gap.

The script is eleven cues of 4–7 seconds, and much of its power is in timing the
pipeline cannot express:

| Script asks for | What the pipeline does |
|---|---|
| 0:62 total runtime | blocks × 10s → 60s, 70s, 80s. Never 62s |
| Shot 1 water-clock drip + drum strike | no SFX layer; block audio is one voice take |
| Shot 8 "music drops out on the last image" | no music layer at all; scoring is a manual edit-time add |
| **Shot 9 — 7 seconds of true silence** | **every block needs its one `audio` item; a silent block is not a supported shape** |
| Shot 11 title card, 4s | minimum unit is a 10s block |
| 0:45 and 0:30 alternates | → 0:40 / 0:50 / 0:60; 0:45 is not on the grid |

**Shot 9 is the one worth arguing about, because the script is right that it is
the emotional centre.** On this grid, silence is what a 10s window sounds like
around a *short centred take* — Chapter 1 used that deliberately twice. The
re-cut writes block 6 to **12 words** so the take lands near 5s and leaves ~2.5s
of held silence either side, and records it as a chosen exception so the next
reviewer does not "fix" it as the dead-air mistake.

A `## Source-script mapping` table is required (`CLAUDE.md`, `SKILL.md` §5.5) and
is in the re-cut.

### 1.4 Aspect ratio is inverted

The script leads with **"16:9 master · 9:16 vertical derivative."** `CLAUDE.md`:
*"Render trailers in 9:16 vertical format; long form in 16:9 landscape."* All
every shipped cut is 9:16, the whole style-key lineage is 9:16 768×1376, and
`build_subtitles.js` labels its `16:9` profile "longform episode."

Cost point, identical to ch1 §1.4: **two masters is two full clip bills.**
9:16 is the master; derive landscape with `reframe` on the finished cut if it is
wanted. The subtitle sidecar must then be rebuilt per format — a 720-wide
vertical frame gives captions roughly half the horizontal room.

The script's vertical note is *substantively* good and is ported (see §6); only
its direction is backwards.

### 1.5 In-frame text — four violations of the text-free clip rule

`SKILL.md` §2: *"Keep the clips text-free — no titles, no captions in-frame."*
The script puts text inside four clips:

| Shot | In-frame text | Resolution in the re-cut |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 營 and 衛 brushed on, wet ink | **Dropped from the clip.** The two currents are carried as *image* — a gold line inside a drawn vessel, a pale line outside it. Clearer than the glyphs, and it survives sound-off viewing |
| 5 | counter `循 07 · 19 · 31` | **Dropped.** Also: 循 is not the text's word (營 / 周 are), and generated numerals are unreliable |
| 9 | 合陰 brushed on | **Kept, as a hand-added edit-time overlay** on block 6, alongside the history lower-third and the credit |
| 11 | title card text | **Kept, as an overlay** on block 7 — the same treatment every shipped cut gives its title |

The practical argument matters as much as the rule: a video model asked for 營
will very likely return a malformed glyph, and nobody on this host can check —
the CDN has been blocked for a long time. Chinese characters that must be
*correct* belong in the edit, not in a paid render.

### 1.6 No end disclaimer card block

`CLAUDE.md` made the end disclaimer card a standing requirement on every cut
after chapter 1, and it **costs a block**: the pipeline assembles fixed 10s
windows, so the card has to be planned into the block count *and* the cost
preflight, rendered as a plain plate with the narrator reading the disclaimer to
fill the block's one audio slot.

The script's shot 11 folds title, seal and disclaimer into 4 seconds. The re-cut
splits them: **block 7 = title, block 8 = disclaimer card.**

### 1.7 Character lines — two speakers inside nine seconds, both far too short

Shots 6 and 7 put **Fan-Di at 0:31–0:36 and Dr. Qi at 0:36–0:40** — two speakers
inside one 10-second window. `explainer_video` takes exactly one `audio` per
block, so every speaker change is its own block. Not a rewrite, but it costs a
block boundary and it is not optional.

Length is the bigger problem. Against the measured cast table:

| Line | Words | Voice | Predicted | Window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fan-Di — *"Same palace…"* | 15 | Xavier, 4.55 w/s | ~3.3s | 6–8s |
| Dr. Qi — *"…different armies."* | 6 | Vesper, 4.42 w/s | ~1.4s | 6–8s |

Both are interjections in a window built for a paragraph. `SKILL.md` gives two
outs and the choice is per line: write the character a real paragraph, or let
the take run short and centred *deliberately*. Here the exchange **is** the hook,
so the re-cut writes both out to 32–33 words. Dr-Qi's expansion is written to the
cast rule — *she never sounds like she's winning; the more Fan-di performs, the
stiller she gets* — so she answers flatly and completely rather than landing a
zinger.

**Lei-Gong is asleep in this cut and stays silent, which is lucky:** Zane is the
one voice measured on a short line only, and `SKILL.md` requires him re-measured
at length before anything is written for him. Keeping him asleep avoids that.

### 1.8 Naming and cast drift — the same three already resolved twice, plus two new ones

| Script says | Repo says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **CHRONICLE OF BALANCE** | ***The Emperor's Inner Canon*** | `CLAUDE.md`; Blueprint title convention |
| **Xiao-Lei** | **Lei-Gong** | `CLAUDE.md` core cast |
| **Dr. Qi** / **Fan-Di** | **Dr-Qi** / **Fan-di** | `CLAUDE.md` core cast |
| **營衛生會 / 合陰** (traditional) | simplified — **营卫生会 / 合阴** | Blueprint and every shipped document |
| **"the archivist"** — a recurring on-screen figure | **not in the cast** | `CLAUDE.md` lists four: Fan-di, Dr-Qi, Lei-Gong, Narrator |

The last one is new and is a real decision, not a typo. The vigil figure appears
in three of eleven shots and carries the whole emotional spine, which makes them
a de-facto fifth cast member — and casting is series-permanent. The re-cut keeps
the figure as an **anonymous ink-wash silhouette in a lit window**: never
named, never framed as a character, never given a face. Same image, no new cast.

  draft (480p)**, **+200 to upgrade to 720p**. Double the clip line if a second
  aspect master is generated rather than reframed.

## 3. Compliance — the reasoning is right, three hard requirements are missing

The COMPLIANCE table is well argued, and item 1 is the load-bearing one: this is
the highest-RPM chapter on the slate *because* of sleep-app and supplement
advertiser overlap, which is exactly the pressure that turns a history channel
into a wellness channel. **"The trailer never suggests the video explains how to
sleep"** is the right line and the re-cut holds it.

**Three hard `CLAUDE.md` requirements are absent or wrong:**

1. **The disclaimer string is not the repo's.** The script uses *"A history
   series. Not medical advice."* `CLAUDE.md` mandates verbatim:
   > *A dramatized adaptation of a classical philosophical text. Not medical advice.*

   The script's version is tighter English, but changing it in one cut silently
   desynchronises Ep 2 from the rest of the series. Either use the repo string, or
   propose the change to `CLAUDE.md` as its own edit. Ch1 hit this and made the
   same call.
2. **The history lower-third is absent.** *"Presented as history & philosophy"*,
   small, on screen **within the first 10 seconds**, on every cut. The script's
   header line *"Framing: History of ideas. Not health content."* is a document
   note, not an on-screen deliverable. It goes on block 1.
3. **The human editorial credit is description-only.** `CLAUDE.md` requires it
   *"on the end card **and** in the description."* Item 5 covers the description;
   the end card has no credit line — and at 4 seconds there was no room for one,
   which is a second argument for §1.6's dedicated card block.

**Two bullets the audit needs that it does not have:**

- **The fermenting vat is alcohol-adjacent, and the audit claims it isn't.**
  Item 4 says *"No alcohol imagery — the wine passage does not appear."* True of
  the wine passage, but **shot 8 is "a vat rolling and bubbling"** — 中焦如沤, the
  middle burner as ferment. It is defensible (no bottles, no pouring, no
  drinking, which is what `CLAUDE.md` actually prohibits), but an audit that
  claims zero alcohol imagery while showing a fermenting vat will not survive its
  own re-read. The re-cut resolves it twice over: the triptych is **not in the
  80s cut at all** (it moves to the stretch lever), and if it is added it renders
  as **grain steeping in a covered vessel** — no wine jar silhouette, no cups.
- **The prompt-stage gate.** `CLAUDE.md` and `SKILL.md` both require the audit to
  run on prompts *before* generating. Read against the recorded `nsfw` incident, where
  restraint and bound-figure imagery tripped the filter on otherwise fine subject
  matter: nothing here goes near it. The sleeping boy is comic, not unconscious;
  the vigil figure is a silhouette in a window. Low risk — recorded as checked.

**One conflict, carried forward from the ch1 review and now overdue.** The
banned-terms rule bars *"secret," "ancient trick," "cure."* The Blueprint's own
Ch 46 hook is ***"The Ancient Science of Sleep — and Why the Old Sleep Badly."***
*"Ancient Science of"* is worse than clickbait here — it asserts scientific
status for a mechanism the trailer's own payoff calls wrong. The cut's thesis and
its title would be arguing with each other. Recommended replacements, all of
which honour the payoff: *"What the Oldest Chinese Medical Text Got Right About
Sleep — and Wrong,"* or *"They Were Wrong About Why. They Were Right About
What."* Same P3 as ch1's: `docs/Emperors Inner Canon Series Blueprint.md` needs
its hooks reconciled with the banned-terms rule.

## 4. Audio — the licensing argument is backwards

> *"No guqin recordings without independent licence… Commission, or use a cleanly
> licensed library cue. Shot 9 is silent, which reduces exposure by seven seconds."*

The first half is correct and worth keeping: an ancient instrument is not a
public-domain recording. The conclusion does not follow.

- `CLAUDE.md` says **licensed guqin only**. "Use a cleanly licensed library cue"
  is a deviation from a standing series rule, not a per-cut scoring choice, and
  belongs in a `CLAUDE.md` edit rather than in one script.
- The script's other cues make the exposure *worse*, not better. A water-clock
  drip, two drum strikes, a rising drone and room tone are **four licensed assets
  with the same Content-ID exposure as a guqin recording**, and **none of them
  can be produced by this pipeline** — `explainer_video` replaces per-block audio
  with the voice take and discards native clip audio. Every one is a manual
  edit-time addition, which is the exact step the guqin already was.
- "Shot 9 is silent, which reduces exposure by seven seconds" — seven seconds of
  a sixty-second bed is not a licensing strategy.

The *"silence is the instrument"* instinct is right and is kept. On this grid
silence comes free: it is what a 10s block sounds like around a centred 5s take.
It just cannot be placed to the frame. Same finding as ch1 §4.

## 5. The re-cut

`output/episode-46/inner-canon-ch46-trailer-v1.md` — **8 × 10s blocks, 0:80,
9:16 vertical**, narrator **Arthur** plus one line each for **Fan-di (Xavier)**
and **Dr-Qi (Vesper)**, Lei-Gong on screen and asleep. Same shape and length as
Chapter 1.

It preserves the script's own spine — **vigil → system → exchange → silence →
the honest line** — in the source's own order, and restores the text at every
point §1.2 flagged. The 三焦 triptych is the stretch beat rather than a shipped
block, which is also what the script's own 0:45 alternate does.

Three structural choices worth calling out, because a future reviewer will
otherwise re-litigate them:

- **Block 1 does the work of the script's shots 1–3 in one continuous move** — a
  slow lateral drift across a rainy night courtyard, from a single lit paper
  window with an upright silhouette inside, past dark windows, settling on
  Lei-Gong face-down asleep on a stone bench. It buys a block back, it dates the
  sleeping boy to the same night as the vigil (fixing §1.2b), and it is a better
  shot than the hard cut it replaces.
- **Block 6 is deliberately short** (12 words, ~5s expected) to buy the silence
  the script's shot 9 asks for. Recorded as a chosen exception, per ch1 blocks
  3 and 8.
- **The spoken title carries no chapter number**, per §1.1.

## 6. What this script does better than the repo — port it upstream

1. **The honest-line posture.** *"Almost every mechanism in it was wrong. Every
   observation in it has survived."* Narrowed for accuracy (§1.2d), this is the
   clearest statement of what this series *is* that anyone has written, and it
   solves the compliance problem and the retention problem in one sentence. It
   belongs in `CLAUDE.md` as a series-level framing rule, not in one cut.
2. **The `Framing:` header line.** *"History of ideas. Not health content."* One
   line at the top of a script that tells every downstream decision what the cut
   is. No shipped document has it.
3. **The alternate-cut specs.** Concrete, reasoned, and each one says what is
   *lost* and what placement it is for. More useful than any shipped runtime-lever
   section. Off-grid as written (§1.3), but the shape should be the house standard.
4. **The vertical-derivative note.** *"Open on the comic image rather than the
   lamp — the comic image stops the scroll, the vigil holds the viewer. Assume
   sound-off viewing."* Correct, and the reasoning generalises to every trailer.
   The ch1 review flagged the same class of note as worth porting; two scripts in
   a row have now supplied it and the repo still has no such section.
5. **`Residual risk: LOW`** as a one-line verdict closing the compliance audit.
   Cheap, and it makes the audit scannable.
