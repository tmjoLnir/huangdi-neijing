## 1. Six questions, and what a body runs on

> 黃帝問於岐伯曰：人焉受氣？陰陽焉會？何氣為營？何氣為衛？營安從生？衛於焉會？老壯不同氣，血氣異處，願聞其會。

**The Yellow Emperor asked Qi Bo:** From what does a person receive qi? Where do
yin and yang meet? Which qi is the nutritive? Which qi is the defensive? Where
does the nutritive arise? Where does the defensive join it? The old and the
vigorous do not share one qi; in them blood and qi keep to different places. I
would hear where they meet.

> 岐伯答曰：人受氣於穀，穀入於胃，以傳與肺，五臟六腑，皆以受氣。其清者為營，濁者為衛。營在脈中，衛在脈外。營周不休，五十而復大會。陰陽相貫，如環無端。

**Qi Bo replied:** A person receives qi from grain. Grain enters the stomach and
is handed on to the lung, and the five *zang* and six *fu* all receive their qi
from it. What is **clear** in it becomes the **nutritive**; what is **turbid**
becomes the **defensive**. The nutritive travels **within** the vessels; the
defensive travels **outside** them. The nutritive circles without pause — **fifty
circuits, and then the great meeting comes round again.** Yin and yang are
threaded one through the other, **a ring with no end.**

> 衛氣行於陰二十五度，行於陽二十五度，分為晝夜。故氣至陽而起，至陰而止。

The defensive qi runs **twenty-five circuits through yin and twenty-five through
yang**, and by this the day is divided from the night. So when the qi arrives at
yang, one rises; when it arrives at yin, one comes to rest.

## 2. The day as a tide

> 故曰：日中而陽隴，為重陽；夜半而陰隴，為重陰。故太陰主內，太陽主外，各行二十五度，分為晝夜。

Therefore it is said: at midday yang is at **flood** — this is **doubled yang**;
at midnight yin is at flood — this is **doubled yin**. So *taiyin* governs what is
within and *taiyang* what is without, each running twenty-five circuits, dividing
day from night.

> 夜半為陰隴，夜半後而為陰衰，平旦陰盡而陽受氣矣。日中為陽隴，日西而陽衰，日入陽盡而陰受氣矣。

Midnight is yin at flood. After midnight, yin declines. At first light yin is
spent, and yang receives the qi. Midday is yang at flood. As the sun goes west,
yang declines. At sundown yang is spent, and yin receives the qi.

> 夜半而大會，萬民皆臥，命曰合陰。平旦陰盡而陽受氣，如是無已，與天地同紀。

**At midnight comes the great meeting, when the ten thousand people all lie down.
This is named 合陰 — the joining of the dark.** At first light yin is spent and
yang receives the qi; and so it goes without ending, **on the same reckoning as
heaven and earth**.

## 3. Why the old lie awake

> 黃帝曰：老人之不夜瞑者，何氣使然？少壯之人不晝瞑者，何氣使然？

**The Yellow Emperor said:** The old do not close their eyes at night — what qi
brings this about? The young and vigorous do not close their eyes by day — what qi
brings *that* about?

> 岐伯答曰：壯者之氣血盛，其肌肉滑，氣道通，營衛之行不失其常，故晝精而夜瞑。

**Qi Bo replied:** In the vigorous, qi and blood are abundant, the flesh is
supple, the qi-roads run clear, and the movement of nutritive and defensive never
departs from its regularity — **therefore they are keen by day and sleep at
night.**

> 老者之氣血衰，其肌肉枯，氣道澀，五臟之氣相搏，其營氣衰少而衛氣內伐，故晝不精，夜不瞑。

In the old, qi and blood are in decline, the flesh is withered, the qi-roads run
rough, the qi of the five *zang* grinds against itself, the nutritive qi is
thinned and scant, and **the defensive qi turns and campaigns inward** —
**therefore they are not keen by day, and do not sleep at night.**

> **The symmetry is the chapter's argument, and it is the easiest thing here to
> lose.** The vigorous get *both* halves — 晝精 keen by day, 夜瞑 asleep by night.
> The old lose *both* — 晝不精，夜不瞑. In this model ageing does not move your
> sleep to a different hour. It takes one end of the day, and then the other.

## 4. Where the two currents come from

> 黃帝曰：願聞營衛之所行，皆何道從來？
> 岐伯答曰：營出於中焦，衛出於下焦。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** I would hear where the nutritive and defensive
travel — by what road do they come?

**Qi Bo replied:** The nutritive issues from the **middle burner**; the defensive
issues from the **lower burner**.

> *Textual variant.* Many received editions read 衛出於**下**焦, as above. A
> substantial line of transmission and commentary reads 衛出於**上**焦 — "the
> defensive issues from the upper burner" — which sits more comfortably with the
> outward, surface-going character the same chapter gives the defensive qi. Both
> readings are ancient. **No cut in this repository may assert either as settled.**

## 5. The upper burner

> 黃帝曰：願聞三焦之所出。
> 岐伯答曰：上焦出於胃上口，並咽以上，貫膈而布胸中，走腋，循太陰之分而行，還至陽明，上至舌，下足陽明。常與營俱行於陽二十五度，行於陰亦二十五度，一周也。故五十度而復大會於手太陰矣。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** I would hear where the three burners issue from.

**Qi Bo replied:** The upper burner issues from the upper mouth of the stomach,
rises alongside the gullet, pierces the diaphragm and spreads through the chest,
runs out to the armpit, follows the division of the *taiyin*, turns back to the
*yangming*, ascends to the tongue and descends into the foot *yangming*. It
travels constantly together with the nutritive — twenty-five circuits through yang
and twenty-five through yin make **one** round — **and so at the fiftieth circuit
they come again to the great meeting, at the hand *taiyin*.**

---

## 6. Leakage — sweat that takes the wrong road

> 黃帝曰：人有熱飲食下胃，其氣未定，汗則出，或出於面，或出於背，或出於身半，其不循衛氣之道而出，何也？

**The Yellow Emperor said:** A person takes hot food or drink down into the
stomach, and before its qi has settled sweat breaks out — sometimes on the face,
sometimes on the back, sometimes over half the body — and it does not come out
along the road of the defensive qi. Why?

> 岐伯曰：此外傷於風，內開腠理，毛蒸理泄，衛氣走之，固不得循其道。此氣慓悍滑疾，見開而出，故不得從其道，故命曰漏泄。

**Qi Bo said:** This is injury by wind from without, which opens the interstices
within. The body hair steams, the interstices leak, and the defensive qi runs out
through them, so of course it cannot keep to its road. **This qi is fierce and
violent, slippery and quick** — it sees an opening and out it goes. Because it
cannot follow its road, this is named **漏泄 — leakage.**

---

## 7. The middle burner — where blood is made

> 黃帝曰：願聞中焦之所出。
> 岐伯答曰：中焦亦並胃中，出上焦之後。此所受氣者，泌糟粕，蒸津液，化其精微，上注於肺脈，乃化而為血，以奉生身，莫貴於此，故獨得行於經隧，命曰營氣。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** I would hear where the middle burner issues from.

**Qi Bo replied:** The middle burner also lies alongside the stomach, issuing
behind the upper burner. What it receives as qi, it strains for the dregs, steams
for the fluids, and transforms into their finest essence; this it pours upward
into the vessel of the lung, **where it is transformed into blood, to serve the
living body. Nothing is more precious than this.** Therefore it alone may travel
in the channel-tunnels, and it is named the **nutritive qi**.

---

## 8. Blood and qi — two names, one kind

> 黃帝曰：夫血之與氣，異名同類，何謂也？
> 岐伯答曰：營衛者精氣也，血者神氣也，故血之與氣，異名同類焉。故奪血者無汗，奪汗者無血，故人生有兩死而無兩生。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** Blood and qi are said to be different in name and one
in kind. What is meant by this?

**Qi Bo replied:** The nutritive and defensive are **essence-qi**; blood is
**spirit-qi**. Therefore blood and qi are different in name and one in kind. And
so: **one robbed of blood has no sweat; one robbed of sweat has no blood.** Thus
in a person's life there are **two deaths, and not two lives.**

---

## 9. The lower burner

> 黃帝曰：願聞下焦之所出。
> 岐伯答曰：下焦者，別回腸，注於膀胱而滲入焉。故水穀者，常并居於胃中，成糟粕，而俱下於大腸，而成下焦，滲而俱下。濟泌別汁，循下焦而滲入膀胱焉。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** I would hear where the lower burner issues from.

**Qi Bo replied:** The lower burner branches off at the coiled intestine, pours
into the bladder, and seeps in there. Water and grain lie together in the stomach;
they become dregs and descend together into the large intestine, and this forms
the lower burner. Seeping, they go down together; the fluids are strained and
separated off, and follow the lower burner to seep into the bladder.

---

## 10. The wine question

> 黃帝曰：人飲酒，酒亦入胃，穀未熟而小便獨先下，何也？
> 岐伯答曰：酒者，熟穀之液也，其氣悍以清，故後穀而入，先穀而液出焉。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** When a person drinks wine, the wine enters the
stomach as well. The grain is not yet ripened — and yet the urine alone descends
first. Why?

**Qi Bo replied:** Wine is the liquid of grain **already ripened**. Its qi is
**fierce and clear**. **Therefore it goes in after the grain, and its fluid comes
out before the grain's.**

> *Production note, not translation.* This is an observation about transit time,
> and nothing else. `CLAUDE.md` permits indulgence in cautionary framing only and
> bars feast close-ups — no bottles, no pouring, no drinking. Any cut using this
> passage carries it on **the vessel and the reasoning**, never on a person
> drinking. See also the 悍 note below: this is the same word the chapter uses for
> the defensive qi, and that is the interesting thing about the passage.

---

## 11. The closing image

> 黃帝曰：善。余聞上焦如霧，中焦如漚，下焦如瀆，此之謂也。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** Good. I have heard it said that **the upper burner is
like mist, the middle burner like fermenting froth, and the lower burner like a
drainage ditch.** This is what was meant.

## Translator's notes — five things a script must not get wrong

These are notes on **this translation**, written for the people scripting off it.
They are not commentary on the classical text and carry no interpretive authority.

1. **Fifty circuits is a whole day *and* night — not a night.** 五十而復大會 counts
   one complete cycle; the 25/25 split then divides that cycle into light and dark.
   A line saying "fifty circuits every night" doubles the canon's own figure.
2. **The great meeting is at midnight.** 夜半而大會…命曰合陰. First light (平旦) is
   the **handover**, not the completion. A counter that finishes at dawn contradicts
   the text it is illustrating.
3. **The old/young pairing is symmetrical, and it is about both halves of the day.**
   晝精而夜瞑 against 晝不精，夜不瞑. The chapter is not asking why the young sleep
   heavily; it asks why they do not doze *by day* — and answers that the old lose
   both ends at once. Drop either half and the argument disappears.
4. **悍 appears twice, and the echo is the chapter's sharpest moment.** The
   defensive qi is 慓悍滑疾 — fierce, violent, slippery, quick (§6) — and wine is
   其氣悍以清, fierce and clear (§10). The chapter is classifying wine *with* the
   defensive qi, by temperament, which is why it takes the fast road out. That
   parallel is in the Chinese and survives translation; it is worth a beat.
5. **The chapter is Ling Shu 18.** Not 11, not 46, not 99 unless the continuous
   Neijing numbering is being used and said out loud.

## What is honestly claimable, and what is not

Stated plainly because this chapter sits in the highest-advertising-pressure
vertical on the slate, and the pressure runs one direction.

- **The observation holds.** That alertness and sleep track a roughly 24-hour cycle
  keyed to light, and that sleep becomes shorter and more broken with age, are both
  well supported by modern sleep research. The chapter noticed both — and noticed
  that they are one phenomenon seen from two ends, which is the harder half.
- **The mechanism does not.** Nutritive and defensive qi making fifty circuits of a
  body are not a description of any physiological process. Nothing here is a
  finding about circadian biology and it must never be presented as one.
- **Nothing in this chapter is advice.** It names no remedy, prescribes no practice,
  sets no schedule and promises no outcome. Neither may any cut derived from it.
