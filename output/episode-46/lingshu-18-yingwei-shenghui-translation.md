## 1. The opening question — what a body runs on

> 黃帝問於岐伯曰：人焉受氣？陰陽焉會？何氣為營？何氣為衛？營安從生？衛於焉會？
> 老壯不同氣，血氣異處，願聞其會。

**The Yellow Emperor asked Qi Bo:** From what does a person receive qi? Where do
yin and yang meet? Which qi is the nutritive? Which qi is the defensive? Where is
the nutritive born? Where does the defensive join it? The old and the vigorous do
not have the same qi; in them blood and qi keep to different places. I wish to
hear how they meet.

> 岐伯答曰：人受氣於穀，穀入於胃，以傳與肺，五臟六腑，皆以受氣。
> 其清者為營，濁者為衛。營在脈中，衛在脈外。營周不休，五十而復大會，陰陽相貫，如環無端。

**Qi Bo answered:** A person receives qi from grain. Grain enters the stomach and
is passed on to the lung, and the five *zang* and six *fu* all receive qi from
it. What is clear in it becomes the **nutritive**; what is turbid becomes the
**defensive**. The nutritive runs *inside* the vessels; the defensive runs
*outside* them. The nutritive circles without rest — **fifty circuits, and then
the great meeting comes round again**. Yin and yang are threaded through each
other like a ring with no end.

> 衛氣行於陰二十五度，行於陽二十五度，分為晝夜，故氣至陽而起，至陰而止。

The defensive qi runs **twenty-five circuits through yin and twenty-five through
yang**, and this divides day from night. So when the qi arrives at yang, one
wakes; when it arrives at yin, one rests.

## 2. The day as a tide

> 故曰：日中而陽隴為重陽，夜半而陰隴為重陰。故太陰主內，太陽主外，各行二十五度，分為晝夜。

Hence it is said: at midday yang is at flood — this is **doubled yang**; at
midnight yin is at flood — this is **doubled yin**. So *taiyin* governs the
interior and *taiyang* governs the exterior, each running twenty-five circuits,
dividing day from night.

> 夜半為陰隴，夜半後而為陰衰，平旦陰盡而陽受氣矣。
> 日中而陽隴，日西而陽衰，日入陽盡而陰受氣矣。

Midnight is yin at its flood. After midnight, yin ebbs. At first light yin is
spent and yang receives the qi. At midday yang is at its flood. As the sun goes
west, yang ebbs. At sundown yang is spent and yin receives the qi.

> 夜半而大會，萬民皆臥，命曰合陰。平旦陰盡而陽受氣，如是無已，與天地同紀。

**At midnight comes the great meeting, when the ten thousand people all lie down.
This is called 合陰 — the joining of the dark.** At first light yin is spent and
yang receives the qi. So it goes, without end, **on the same reckoning as heaven
and earth**.

---

## 3. Why the old lie awake

> 黃帝曰：老人之不夜瞑者，何氣使然？少壯之人不晝瞑者，何氣使然？

**The Yellow Emperor said:** The old do not sleep at night — what qi makes it so?
The young and vigorous do not sleep by day — what qi makes it so?

> 岐伯答曰：壯者之氣血盛，其肌肉滑，氣道通，營衛之行不失其常，故晝精而夜瞑。
> 老者之氣血衰，其肌肉枯，氣道澀，五臟之氣相搏，其營氣衰少而衛氣內伐，故晝不精，夜不瞑。

**Qi Bo answered:** In the vigorous, qi and blood are abundant, the flesh is
smooth, the qi-roads are open, and the movement of nutritive and defensive never
loses its regularity — **therefore they are sharp by day and sleep by night.**

In the old, qi and blood are ebbing, the flesh is withered, the qi-roads are
rough, the qi of the five *zang* grind against one another, the nutritive qi is
thinned and scant, and the defensive qi turns and attacks inward — **therefore
they are not sharp by day, and do not sleep by night.**

> **The symmetry is the whole point of the chapter, and it is easy to miss.** The
> young get *both* halves — 晝精 (sharp by day) and 夜瞑 (asleep by night). The old
> lose *both* — 晝不精, 夜不瞑. Aging does not move your sleep to a different hour
> in this model. It takes one end of the day and then the other.

## 4. Where the two come from

> 黃帝曰：願聞營衛之所行，皆何道從來？
> 岐伯答曰：營出於中焦，衛出於下焦。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** I wish to hear where nutritive and defensive travel —
by what road do they come? **Qi Bo answered:** The nutritive issues from the
**middle burner**; the defensive issues from the **lower burner**.

> *Textual variant.* Many received editions read 衛出於**下**焦 as above; a
> substantial line of transmission and commentary reads 衛出於**上**焦, "the
> defensive issues from the upper burner," which sits better with the defensive
> qi's outward, surface-going character described elsewhere in the chapter.
> Both readings are ancient. Nothing in this repository's scripts should assert
> one as settled.

## 5. The upper burner

> 黃帝曰：願聞三焦之所出。
> 岐伯答曰：上焦出於胃上口，並咽以上，貫膈而布胸中，走腋，循太陰之分而行，還至陽明，
> 上至舌，下足陽明。常與營俱行於陽二十五度，行於陰亦二十五度一周也，故五十度而復大會於手太陰矣。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** I wish to hear where the three burners issue from.

**Qi Bo answered:** The upper burner issues from the upper mouth of the stomach,
rises alongside the throat, pierces the diaphragm and spreads through the chest,
runs out to the armpit, follows the *taiyin* division, turns back to *yangming*,
rises to the tongue and descends to the foot *yangming*. It travels constantly
together with the nutritive — twenty-five circuits through yang and twenty-five
through yin make one round — **and so at the fiftieth circuit they meet again at
the hand *taiyin*.**

---

## 6. Leakage — sweat that takes the wrong road

> 黃帝曰：人有熱飲食下胃，其氣未定，汗則出，或出於面，或出於背，或出於身半，
> 其不循衛氣之道而出，何也？

**The Yellow Emperor said:** When a person takes hot food or drink down into the
stomach and its qi has not yet settled, sweat breaks out — sometimes on the face,
sometimes on the back, sometimes over half the body — and it does not come out
along the road of the defensive qi. Why?

> 岐伯曰：此外傷於風，內開腠理，毛蒸理泄，衛氣走之，固不得循其道。
> 此氣慓悍滑疾，見開而出，故不得從其道，故命曰漏泄。

**Qi Bo said:** This is an outward injury by wind, which opens the interstices
within. The hairs steam, the interstices leak, and the defensive qi runs out
through them, so it cannot keep to its road. This qi is fierce, swift, slippery
and quick — it sees an opening and it goes. Because it cannot follow its road,
this is called **漏泄 — leakage.**

---

## 7. The middle burner — where blood is made

> 黃帝曰：願聞中焦之所出。
> 岐伯答曰：中焦亦並胃中，出上焦之後。此所受氣者，泌糟粕，蒸津液，化其精微，
> 上注於肺脈，乃化而為血，以奉生身，莫貴於此，故獨得行於經隧，命曰營氣。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** I wish to hear where the middle burner issues from.

**Qi Bo answered:** The middle burner also lies alongside the stomach, issuing
behind the upper burner. What it takes in as qi, it strains for the dregs, steams
for the fluids, and transforms into their finest essence; this it pours upward
into the vessel of the lung, **where it is transformed into blood, to sustain the
living body. Nothing is more precious than this.** Therefore it alone is allowed
to travel in the channel-tunnels, and it is called the **nutritive qi**.

---

## 8. Blood and qi — two names, one kind

> 黃帝曰：夫血之與氣，異名同類，何謂也？
> 岐伯答曰：營衛者精氣也，血者神氣也，故血之與氣，異名同類焉。
> 故奪血者無汗，奪汗者無血，故人生有兩死而無兩生。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** Blood and qi are said to have different names but be
of one kind. What does this mean?

**Qi Bo answered:** The nutritive and defensive are **essence-qi**; blood is
**spirit-qi**. Therefore blood and qi have different names but are of one kind.
And so: **one robbed of blood has no sweat; one robbed of sweat has no blood.**
Thus a person has two ways to die, and not two ways to live.

---

## 9. The lower burner

> 黃帝曰：願聞下焦之所出。
> 岐伯答曰：下焦者，別回腸，注於膀胱而滲入焉。
> 故水穀者，常并居於胃中，成糟粕而俱下於大腸，而成下焦，滲而俱下，濟泌別汁，循下焦而滲入膀胱焉。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** I wish to hear where the lower burner issues from.

**Qi Bo answered:** The lower burner branches at the coiled intestine, pours into
the bladder, and seeps in there. Water and grain dwell together in the stomach;
they become dregs and descend together into the large intestine, and this forms
the lower burner. Seeping, they descend together; the fluids are strained and
divided off, and follow the lower burner to seep into the bladder.

---

## 10. The wine question

> 黃帝曰：人飲酒，酒亦入胃，穀未熟而小便獨先下，何也？
> 岐伯答曰：酒者熟穀之液也，其氣悍以清，故後穀而入，先穀而液出焉。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** When a person drinks wine, the wine enters the
stomach too. The grain is not yet ripened — and yet the urine alone descends
first. Why?

**Qi Bo answered:** Wine is the liquid of already-ripened grain. Its qi is fierce
and clear. **Therefore it goes in after the grain, and its fluid comes out before
the grain's.**

## 11. The closing image

> 黃帝曰：善。余聞上焦如霧，中焦如漚，下焦如瀆，此之謂也。

**The Yellow Emperor said:** Good. I have heard it said that **the upper burner
is like mist, the middle burner like fermenting froth, and the lower burner like
a drainage ditch.** This is what was meant.

## Translator's notes — the four things a script must not get wrong

1. **Fifty circuits is a whole day and night, not a night.** 五十而復大會 counts a
   full cycle; the 25/25 split then divides it into light and dark. Any line that
   says "fifty circuits every night" doubles the canon's own figure.
2. **The great meeting is at midnight.** 夜半而大會…命曰合陰. First light (平旦) is
   the *handover*, not the completion. A counter that finishes at dawn contradicts
   the text.
3. **The old/young pairing is symmetrical, and it is about both halves of the
   day.** 晝精而夜瞑 against 晝不精，夜不瞑. The chapter is not asking why the young
   sleep hard; it is asking why they do not doze by day — and answering that the
   old lose both ends.
4. **The chapter is Ling Shu 18.** Not 11, not 46.

## What is honestly claimable, and what is not

Worth stating plainly in any cut, because this chapter sits in the
highest-pressure advertising vertical on the slate:

- **The observation holds.** That sleep becomes shorter and more broken with age,
  and that alertness and sleep track a roughly 24-hour cycle keyed to the light,
  are both well supported by modern sleep research. The chapter noticed both, and
  noticed that they are the *same* phenomenon seen from two ends.
- **The mechanism does not.** Nutritive and defensive qi making fifty circuits of
  the body are not descriptions of any physiological process. Nothing here is a
  finding about circadian biology, and it must never be presented as one.
- **Nothing in this chapter is advice.** It names no remedy, prescribes no
  practice, and promises no outcome. Neither may any cut derived from it.
