# The Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic — A Plain-English Guide

*Source text: 《黄帝内经》 Huángdì Nèijīng, as published at 8bei8.com/book/huangdineijing.html*

---

## 1. What this book actually is

Strip away the mystique and the Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic is three things at once:

1. **The founding textbook of Chinese medicine.** Compiled roughly between 300 BC and 100 AD by many anonymous hands, then edited into its received form. Everything that came after it — acupuncture, herbal formula theory, pulse diagnosis, qigong — is a footnote to this book.
2. **A theory of how a human being is put together**, built not from dissection but from pattern-matching the body against the seasons, the weather, the landscape, and the imperial court.
3. **A conversation.** Almost the entire book is dialogue. The Yellow Emperor asks; his physician-ministers answer. That is why it reads like drama rather than a manual — and why it adapts to video so well.

### The cast

| Character | Role | Function in the text |
|---|---|---|
| **Huangdi 黄帝** — the Yellow Emperor | Legendary ruler, c. 2600 BC | Asks the questions. Curious, occasionally impatient, sometimes stunned by the answer. |
| **Qi Bo 岐伯** | Chief physician-minister | Delivers the doctrine. Patient, systematic, the voice of the tradition. |
| **Lei Gong 雷公** | Junior disciple | Gets things wrong, gets corrected, gets scolded. The audience's stand-in. |

### The two halves

The book is **162 chapters in two parts of 81 each**:

- **素问 Sùwèn — "Basic Questions" (81 chapters).** The theory half. Cosmology, physiology, what causes disease, how to diagnose it, how to live so you don't get it. This is where the philosophy and the famous lines live.
- **灵枢 Língshū — "The Spiritual Pivot" (81 chapters).** The practice half. Needles, channels, anatomy, constitutional types, the mechanics of treatment. Also called the *Canon of Acupuncture*.

Why 81? It is 9 × 9. Nine was the number of completeness. The structure is a statement: *this book is finished; there is nothing left to ask.*

### The core ideas, in one paragraph each

**Qi (气).** Not "energy" in the vague sense. Closer to *functional capacity in motion* — the ability of a thing to do what that thing does. Your digestion has qi. So does a season, a river, a mood. Illness is qi that is blocked, depleted, moving the wrong way, or in the wrong place.

**Yin and Yang.** Not good and evil. A pair of opposed, mutually-generating tendencies: dark/light, still/moving, cooling/heating, storing/releasing. Nothing is yin or yang absolutely — only relative to something else. Health is the pair staying in dynamic balance; disease is one running away from the other.

**The Five Phases (五行).** Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water — not substances but five *stages of a cycle*, mapped onto everything: five organs, five seasons, five emotions, five flavours, five colours, five sounds. It is a filing system for correlating what a doctor observes to what a doctor should do.

**The Zang-Fu organs.** The "organs" here are less anatomical lumps than *functional departments*. The "Liver" in this book means the whole business of planning, smooth flow, and the free movement of emotion — which is why anger damages it. Read them as job descriptions, not body parts.

**The Channels (经络).** Twelve main routes plus branches, along which qi is said to circulate on a daily cycle. This is the map that makes acupuncture a system rather than a collection of tricks.

**Prevention over cure.** The book's central ethical claim, and its most modern-sounding: the superior physician treats the illness that has not yet arrived. Waiting until you are sick is compared to digging a well once you are already thirsty.

## 2. The top 20 chapters for a 15–20 minute video

Selected from all 162 chapters against four filters: **(a)** a self-contained dramatic spine — a question, a turn, a resolution; **(b)** a genuine idea a modern viewer can use or argue with; **(c)** enough content for 15–20 minutes without padding; **(d)** commercial viability as a monetised episode.

Grouped into four seasons of five episodes.

### Arc I — Foundations: how the body is supposed to work

| # | Chapter | Plain-English title | The hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Suwen 1 · 上古天真论 | Why the Ancients Lived to 100 and You Won't | Opens the entire book with the Emperor demanding to know why people used to reach a hundred still vigorous, and now fall apart at fifty. The answer is a lifestyle indictment. Contains the famous life-cycle clock: women in 7-year stages, men in 8. |
| 2 | Suwen 2 · 四气调神大论 | Living by the Four Seasons | A seasonal operating manual — when to sleep, when to rise, what to want in spring vs. winter. Ends on the book's most quoted line about treating disease before it exists. |
| 3 | Suwen 3 · 生气通天论 | Your Inner Sun: The Daily Arc of Yang Qi | Yang qi is compared to the sun: it generates at dawn, peaks at noon, closes down at dusk — and if you fight that curve, you pay. A circadian-rhythm chapter written two millennia before the word existed. |
| 4 | Suwen 5 · 阴阳应象大论 | The Grand Theory of Yin and Yang | The single best explainer chapter in the book. The whole correspondence system laid out at once — this is the episode that makes every other episode legible. |
| 5 | Suwen 8 · 灵兰秘典论 | The Body as an Empire | The twelve organs as offices of an imperial court. The Heart is the sovereign; the Lung is the chancellor who sets the rhythm of governance; the Liver is the general who devises strategy; the Gallbladder is the judge who makes the decision. If the sovereign is enlightened, the realm is at peace. Pure cinema. |

### Arc II — The mind inside the body

| # | Chapter | Plain-English title | The hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Suwen 13 · 移精变气论 | When the Old Magic Stopped Working | An astonishing admission: the ancient incantation cures used to work, and now they don't — because life became complicated, anxious and materially entangled. A 2,000-year-old discussion of placebo, belief, and psychosomatic illness. |
| 7 | Suwen 39 · 举痛论 | All Illness Begins with Qi | The emotional physiology chapter. Anger drives qi upward; joy slackens it; grief consumes it; fear drives it down; fright throws it into chaos; overthinking ties it in knots. Then it traces how cold and stagnation actually produce pain. |
| 8 | Lingshu 8 · 本神 | The Five Spirits | Ancient psychiatry. Five aspects of mind — spirit, ethereal soul, corporeal soul, intent, will — each housed in an organ, each damaged by a specific excess. What chronic fear does to the kidneys; what unresolved grief does to the lungs. |
| 9 | Lingshu 43 · 淫邪发梦 | The Emperor's Dream Dictionary | Dreams read as diagnostic data. Excess above makes you dream of flying; excess below, of falling. Too much heat brings dreams of fire, too much cold brings dreams of water. Go to bed hungry and you dream of taking; go to bed full and you dream of giving. |
| 10 | Lingshu 64 · 阴阳二十五人 | The Twenty-Five Kinds of People | Five constitutional types by element, each subdivided five ways. Wood people are lean, restless and worry too much; earth people are broad, steady and generous; water people run cold and are hard to read. An ancient personality typology — with health predictions attached. |

### Arc III — Sickness: how things go wrong

| # | Chapter | Plain-English title | The hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | Suwen 31 · 热论 | Six Days of Fever | A febrile illness tracked day by day as it marches inward through six layers of the body, with the symptoms of each stage and the point at which it turns fatal. The direct ancestor of all later Chinese epidemic medicine. |
| 12 | Suwen 43 · 痹论 | Wind, Cold and Damp: Why Joints Ache | Three environmental factors that combine to lodge in the joints — and whichever dominates gives a different disease: wandering pain, fixed severe pain, or heavy immovable stiffness. Why your knee knows the weather is turning. |
| 13 | Suwen 47 · 奇病论 | Strange Cases | A file of bizarre presentations with explanations: the woman nine months pregnant who suddenly cannot speak; the "sweet mouth" disease of wealthy men who eat too richly, which progresses to wasting-thirst — a recognisable description of diabetes; epilepsy traced to a shock the mother suffered while carrying the child. |
| 14 | Suwen 74 · 至真要大论 | The Nineteen Rules | The Nineteen Pathomechanisms: a compressed diagnostic checklist that assigns whole categories of symptom to a responsible organ or factor. All shaking and dizziness belongs to the Liver; all sudden rigidity to Cold. The closest thing the ancient world produced to a clinical algorithm. |
| 15 | Lingshu 10 · 经脉 | The Map Beneath the Skin | The definitive chapter on the twelve channels — each route traced, each set of failure symptoms listed. This is the chapter that turned needling into a system, and it is the one that most needs animation to be understood. |

### Arc IV — The physician, and the length of a life

| # | Chapter | Plain-English title | The hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | Suwen 12 · 异法方宜论 | Why Medicine Is Different in Every Country | Different regions breed different diseases and therefore different cures: stone needles came from the east coast, herbal drugs from the west, moxibustion from the frozen north, fine needling from the humid south, and therapeutic exercise and massage from the central plains. A theory of medical geography. |
| 17 | Suwen 17 · 脉要精微论 | Reading a Life at the Wrist | Pulse diagnosis: why it must be done at dawn before the day disturbs the body, how the pulse should change with each season, and what the face and voice reveal alongside it. |
| 18 | Suwen 25 · 宝命全形论 | Nothing Is More Precious Than a Human Being | Heaven covers, earth carries, and among the ten thousand things nothing is more valuable than a person. From that premise the chapter derives the discipline required of anyone who picks up a needle. A humanist manifesto with a technical second half. |
| 19 | Suwen 77 · 疏五过论 | The Five Failures of a Doctor | The book turns on its own profession. Five ways physicians fail patients — chief among them never asking whether this person was once wealthy and is now poor, or once honoured and now disgraced, because that history is the illness. Medical ethics, two thousand years early. |
| 20 | Lingshu 54 · 天年 | A Hundred Years, Decade by Decade | The whole span of a human life in ten-year steps: at ten you love to run, at twenty to walk fast, at thirty to sit, at forty to lie down; at fifty the eyes dim, at sixty the heart weakens and sorrow comes easily, at seventy the flesh dries, at eighty words start to go wrong, and at a hundred the spirit departs and the body remains alone. Then it asks why some people are born frail and never reach it. |

### Strong runners-up

Held back only because twenty was the brief: **Lingshu 80 · 大惑论** (insomnia, excessive sleep and dizziness — commercially the strongest topic not on the list); **Lingshu 49 · 五色** (diagnosis by reading the face); **Suwen 66–74 · the Seven Great Treatises** on the Five Movements and Six Qi (cosmic cycles used to forecast epidemic years — timely, but needs three episodes, not one); **Suwen 11 · 五脏别论** (why some organs store and others transport); **Suwen 4 · 金匮真言论** (the five-organ correspondence tables).

## 3. How the RPM figures were estimated

RPM is revenue per 1,000 views *after* YouTube's 45% share, across all views including unmonetised ones. It is not CPM. The ranges in the deck assume:

- **~60% Tier-1 audience** (US, UK, CA, AU, DE). This channel will pull significant traffic from India, SE Asia and China-diaspora audiences, where RPM runs 70–90% lower — the blended figure is dragged down accordingly.
- **15–20 minute runtime**, which clears the 8-minute threshold and supports 2–3 mid-roll breaks. This is the single largest controllable RPM lever and is why the brief's chosen length is correct.
- **Standard monetisation, not "limited ads."** This is the real risk. Content that reads as *medical advice* — especially unproven treatment claims — routinely gets restricted under advertiser-friendly guidelines. Framing an episode as **history, philosophy and anthropology of medicine** rather than as health guidance protects the RPM. Every disease-centred chapter in Arc III carries this risk and is priced lower for it.
- **Advertiser demand by topic.** Longevity, sleep, stress and mental health draw premium advertisers (supplements, mattresses, therapy apps, insurance). Pure doctrine and technique chapters draw general education inventory at roughly half the rate.

Benchmark sources for the underlying niche ranges are listed at the end of the deck. Treat every figure as an order-of-magnitude planning estimate: real RPM on a new channel varies by more than 3× based on audience geography alone, and no estimate survives contact with the first 90 days of analytics.
